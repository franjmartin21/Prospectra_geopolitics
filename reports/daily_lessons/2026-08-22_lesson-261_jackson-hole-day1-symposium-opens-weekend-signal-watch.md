## The Jackson Hole Symposium: Structure and Information Content

### What Jackson Hole Actually Is

The annual Jackson Hole Economic Symposium is hosted by the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City. The 2026 symposium theme (typical for this period) is expected to address the monetary policy transmission mechanism in a structurally higher-inflation world. Understanding the structure determines how to filter its output:

| Speaker Category | Who | Information Value for the Powell Call |
|---|---|---|
| **Academic presenters** | Leading macroeconomists (often from top universities or think tanks) | HIGH for framing — sets the intellectual context Powell responds to. LOW for prediction — academics don't speak for the Fed. |
| **International central bankers** | ECB, BOJ, BOC, BOE, RBA governors | MEDIUM — signals coordination or divergence. A hawkish ECB statement strengthens Scenario A probability; a dovish BOJ signal is orthogonal. |
| **Fed Reserve Bank presidents** | Regional Fed presidents (non-voting or voting) | HIGH — these are FOMC members. But the blackout period should be verified. If active, they cannot speak on policy. |
| **Powell himself** | Chair of the Federal Reserve | THE ONLY DEFINITIVE INPUT. Everything else is context. |
| **Media/analyst commentary** | Bloomberg, FT, WSJ "Fed watchers" | NOISE — they are speculating, not reporting. Treat as sentiment indicator only. |

**The critical discipline:** Non-Powell speakers at Jackson Hole do not preview Powell's speech. They present their research. Their views may influence the conversation, but Powell has his remarks prepared. The only exception is if a Fed Reserve Bank president (if not in blackout) makes a direct statement about near-term policy direction — that is an FOMC member speaking, and it adjusts probabilities.

## The Signal Discrimination Framework: Four Questions

Apply this framework to every piece of information produced at the symposium this weekend:

### Question 1: Who is the source?

- **Powell:** Definitive. Update the scenario matrix immediately.
- **FOMC voting member (if not in blackout):** Material. Adjust scenario probabilities 5-10%.
- **Non-voting Fed president or international CB:** Informative context. Adjust at most 2-3%.
- **Academic presenter:** Framework context. Do not adjust scenario probabilities.
- **Media commentary:** Sentiment signal only. Do not adjust scenario probabilities.

### Question 2: Is the Fed in blackout?

The Federal Reserve enters a communications blackout period before each FOMC meeting. The standard blackout begins approximately 10 days before the FOMC meeting decision date. The next FOMC meeting is September 16-17, 2026. The blackout would begin approximately September 6.

**For Jackson Hole (August 22-27):** Fed officials are NOT in blackout for the September FOMC meeting. However, Jackson Hole is governed by a different norm — Fed officials speaking at the symposium typically do not preview their FOMC vote in their presentations. Powell's speech is the designated policy communication point.

**Practical conclusion:** FOMC members can speak at Jackson Hole, but their presentations are typically academic or framework-level. If any FOMC member makes an explicit statement about the September rate decision ("I lean toward X in September"), that is extraordinary and should immediately update the scenario probabilities.

### Question 3: Does the presentation bear on the September 2026 FOMC decision?

The scenario matrix (Lessons 255-260) is built around what Powell signals for September — specifically whether the Fed will hold, cut, or communicate a holding pattern with tightening bias. A presentation that bears directly on this question adjusts probabilities. One that doesn't, doesn't.

**Bear on the decision:**
- Any discussion of current US inflation trajectory
- Labor market tightness vs. slackening evidence
- Financial stability concerns that could affect the timing of cuts
- Explicit policy rate guidance by a voting FOMC member

**Do not bear on the decision (interesting but noise for this framework):**
- Long-run r* estimates (academic; not the September call)
- International coordination mechanisms
- Historical analyses of past policy regimes
- Climate stress testing of monetary policy
- Any non-US central bank policy discussion

### Question 4: Does it move the vol surface?

This is the market-behavior check. Even if a statement seems like noise analytically, check whether VIX, MOVE, GVZ, or OVX respond:

- **If MOVE rises +5 or more after a presentation:** The bond market is treating it as signal. Investigate.
- **If VIX spikes +2 or more:** Equity market is re-pricing something. Check whether it is Fed-related or geopolitical.
- **If GVZ rises without an obvious Iran/geopolitical headline:** The gold market may be reading a Fed-related risk that the text analysis misses.

The vol surface is the aggregate signal processor of all market participants. If it moves on something you classified as noise, your classification may be wrong. Investigate.

## Historical Grounding: What the Jackson Hole Weekend Has Signaled in Key Years

The three days before a pivotal Powell speech have historically produced predictable patterns:

### 2022: The Silent Weekend Before the "Pain" Speech

August 20-21, 2022 (Saturday-Sunday before the August 26 speech):
- No pre-speech signals from the symposium. Academic presentations focused on supply-side inflation — interesting but not policy-predictive.
- Gold moved -0.3% over the weekend (noise).
- S&P 500 futures opened flat Sunday.
- The consensus remained: Scenario B (dovish step-down) at ~60% probability.

**What the "correct" read would have been:** The absence of signals was itself a signal. Powell was going to let the speech do the work — which is what happened (8-minute "pain" speech, no pre-leaks, market shocked). A flat weekend into a major Jackson Hole speech historically correlates with a speech that delivers a genuine surprise, because the pre-event price action hasn't already "borrowed" from the move.

**Application to August 22-24, 2026:** If the weekend is quiet — no major symposium statements, flat futures Sunday evening — treat this as a mild signal that the speech content is not pre-telegraphed. The scenario probability distribution (A:18%, B:38%, C:32%, D:12%) stands without revision.

### 2020: AIT Signals Were In the Weekend Papers

August 22-23, 2020 (Saturday-Sunday before the August 27 AIT speech):
- Academic presentations included a paper on "Monetary Policy Frameworks" that explicitly discussed average inflation targeting. Bloomberg reported on it Sunday.
- Fed watchers (Nick Timiraos at WSJ) noted the paper's proximity to expected Powell themes.
- 10Y yields drifted -3bp Sunday evening (markets reading the paper as dovish).

**What the correct read would have been:** Academic paper on AIT + Fed watcher commentary = HIGH PROBABILITY that Powell's speech would formalize the AIT framework. This was a genuine weekend signal — the 2020 equivalent of an FOMC member previewing the outcome. The Scenario B probability should have risen from 40% to 55%+.

**Application:** If any academic presenter this weekend explicitly models or advocates for a rate path that aligns with Scenario B or A, AND if Fed watchers (Timiraos/Derby/Hilsenrath) report that paper as "in line with what we expect from Powell" — update Scenario B upward by 5-10 percentage points.

### 2019: The Weekend Was Flat, Then the Speech Disappointed Both Directions

August 24-25, 2019 (Saturday-Sunday before the August 23 speech):
- Note: 2019's speech was Friday, so the preceding weekend was August 17-18.
- The symposium produced no major signals. Goldman and JPMorgan both predicted Scenario B (dovish 50bp cut preview).
- Gold rose 0.8% on the Sunday open — modest risk-off positioning.

**What happened:** Powell delivered 25bp cut signal, not 50bp. Markets had priced 50bp. The "ambiguous" scenario (C) materialized — Powell was neither hawkish enough for bulls nor dovish enough for bears. Gold dropped; equities initially rallied then fell.

**Application:** The 2019 case is the closest historical analog to the current Scenario C probability (32%). A flat-to-modestly-risk-off weekend with no specific policy signals is the setup for a "threads-the-needle" speech. If the weekend produces: (1) no material symposium signals AND (2) no geopolitical escalation, the Scenario C probability should nudge up from 32% to 34-35%.
