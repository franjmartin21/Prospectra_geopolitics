## 1. The Architecture You Never See

When two banks in different countries settle a transaction, they don't send each other money directly. They communicate through a layered infrastructure most investors have never examined:

**SWIFT** — Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication. Founded in 1973, headquartered in Belgium. SWIFT is *not* a payment system. It is a **messaging system** — a secure network that tells banks what to do. It transmits payment orders, confirmations, and trade finance instructions. More than 11,000 financial institutions in 200+ countries use it. Without SWIFT connectivity, a bank cannot reliably communicate with the global financial system.

**CHIPS** — Clearing House Interbank Payments System. A private U.S. settlement system owned by large commercial banks. Processes approximately $1.8 trillion per day. Critically: CHIPS is where most **dollar-denominated international payments actually settle**. The vast majority of international dollar transactions flow through CHIPS before final settlement at the Federal Reserve (Fedwire).

**Fedwire** — The Federal Reserve's real-time gross settlement system. The ultimate backstop for dollar settlement. Unlike CHIPS (which nets positions), Fedwire settles transaction-by-transaction in real time.

**The choke point:** To settle in dollars, a bank ultimately needs access to U.S. correspondent banks and the U.S. domestic payment system. Exclude a bank from SWIFT and you sever its ability to communicate. Exclude it from dollar correspondent banking (Lesson 84) and you sever its ability to *settle*. Both together make a country financially stateless.

## 2. From Utility to Weapon — The Weaponization Timeline

For most of their existence, these systems were treated as neutral infrastructure — boring plumbing that moved money without political judgment.

**2012: Iran's first disconnection.** The U.S. and EU pressured SWIFT to disconnect Iranian banks as part of the nuclear sanctions regime. This was unprecedented. A neutral financial messaging utility had never been used as a geopolitical weapon. Iranian oil revenues collapsed. The rial lost 80% of its value within months. Iran returned to the negotiating table. The nuclear deal (JCPOA) followed in 2015, and Iranian banks were partly reconnected in 2016. After the U.S. withdrew from JCPOA in 2018, disconnection resumed.

**The lesson Tehran drew:** Financial infrastructure is not neutral. Access can be revoked. You need a backup.

**2022: Russia's partial disconnection.** Following the invasion of Ukraine, 10 Russian banks were disconnected from SWIFT. Russia is a far larger economy than Iran — this was a far larger deployment of the weapon. The ruble initially collapsed. But Russia had prepared: it ran more transactions through domestic systems (SPFS), accelerated yuan settlement, and found workarounds through third-country banks in UAE, Turkey, and Central Asia. The weapon worked — but less completely than in 2012.

**The lesson the world drew:** The dollar payment rail is real and painful, but not absolute. There are workarounds — especially if China is willing to provide an alternative.

**2026: Iran, Hormuz, and the stress test.**  
The U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February 2026 triggered the largest oil supply disruption in market history. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz. SWIFT disconnection was re-imposed comprehensively. Iranian banks had no legitimate dollar access.

But here is what changed: China's CIPS — Cross-Border Interbank Payment System — began processing oil-for-yuan transactions at record volumes. In March 2026, CIPS processed 1.22 trillion yuan in a single day (~$178 billion). China changed CIPS rules to allow non-yuan currency transactions, explicitly positioning it as a dollar-alternative rail. Countries needing to pay for diverted Iranian oil, or to settle Gulf trade outside Hormuz, were using CIPS.

## 3. CIPS — The Parallel Rail

CIPS was launched by China in 2015, explicitly as both a domestic yuan settlement system and a long-run alternative to dollar rails. Understanding it clearly requires separating marketing from reality:

**What CIPS is today:**
- A real-time gross settlement system for cross-border yuan transactions
- Connects ~1,400 financial institutions across 110+ countries
- As of 2026, expanding to handle multi-currency settlements (not just yuan)
- Transaction volumes growing >50% year-over-year since 2023

**What CIPS is not (yet):**
- It does not have the network depth of SWIFT (which connects 11,000+ institutions)
- It lacks CHIPS' correspondent bank infrastructure for deep liquidity provision
- Most non-Chinese banks still route through SWIFT even for yuan trades
- Dollar liquidity in CIPS is thin compared to the Eurodollar system

**The 2026 shift:** The Iran war has done what years of de-dollarization rhetoric never did — it has created actual transaction flow on CIPS that bypasses dollar rails. Countries that need to buy Iranian oil diverted around Hormuz, or need to settle Gulf trade in an environment of financial warfare, are using CIPS. The use-case has arrived.

**The structural long-term threat:** SWIFT's value is network effects. The more institutions use it, the more valuable it is, and the harder it is to leave. Every dollar transaction routed through CIPS instead of CHIPS is a small erosion of that network effect. At low volumes, it doesn't matter. At the scale now emerging, it starts to matter directionally.

## 4. The Dollar's Structural Moat — and Its Vulnerabilities

Why does the dollar payment rail still dominate despite 15 years of adversary awareness?

**The moat:**
1. **Invoice currency dominance.** ~80% of global trade is invoiced in dollars — not because of politics but because it reduces FX risk for both parties. Changing this requires coordinating millions of contracts simultaneously.
2. **Reserve asset depth.** Dollar-denominated assets (Treasuries, agency debt, dollar money markets) are the world's safe harbor. No alternative has the depth or liquidity.
3. **Correspondent bank network effects.** U.S. correspondent banks have centuries of relationship infrastructure. CIPS can't replicate this overnight.
4. **U.S. legal system and contract enforceability.** Dollar contracts are governed by New York law — the world's most reliable legal framework for cross-border finance.

**The vulnerabilities:**
1. **Overuse of the weapon.** Every time SWIFT disconnection is deployed, it signals to every non-allied country that their financial access is conditional. This accelerates the search for alternatives — even by countries that are nominally allied but wish to preserve optionality.
2. **China as a credible alternative.** For the first time in the modern financial era, there is a large, liquid, globally connected alternative to the dollar rail. It is not ready to replace it. But it is ready to serve as a parallel track for a meaningful subset of global trade.
3. **Petrodollar erosion.** The petrodollar system (Lesson 66) is premised on Gulf producers pricing and settling oil in dollars. If even a fraction of Gulf oil settles in yuan via CIPS — which is happening now during the Hormuz crisis — the structural foundation of dollar dominance begins to shift at the margin.

## 5. Investment Implications

This is not an abstract academic debate. The dollar payment rail is infrastructure for global financial pricing. Changes to it reprice assets across multiple classes.

**Dollar/DXY:** A gradual erosion of dollar invoice dominance is bearish for DXY over a 3–7 year horizon. Not a collapse — a structural headwind. Any sustained increase in non-dollar trade settlement reduces the structural demand for dollar liquidity. **Directional view: mildly bearish USD on 5-year horizon; neutral to modestly bearish near-term as Hormuz crisis sustains safe-haven USD demand.**

**Gold:** The beneficiary of every dollar weaponization cycle. Central banks that fear SWIFT exclusion hold gold — it is the only reserve asset with no issuer and no payment rail vulnerability. Iranian, Russian, and Chinese central banks have been accumulating gold for exactly this reason. **Directional view: structurally bullish gold. Every escalation of financial warfare strengthens the case.**

**Chinese yuan (CNH):** If CIPS gains sustainable transaction volume from the 2026 crisis, CNH becomes a more important settlement currency. This is bullish CNH at the margin — but only at the margin, because China's capital controls limit the yuan's reserve asset role. **Directional view: modest positive for CNH internationalisation thesis, long-horizon.**

**Energy equities and oil:** Hormuz closure + SWIFT weaponization = oil price spike + supply route fragmentation. This is bullish for non-Gulf oil producers (U.S. shale, Canada, Norway, Brazil) who face no payment rail risk and benefit from price elevation. **Directional view: overweight non-Gulf energy producers while Hormuz is restricted.**

**EM sovereign bonds:** Countries dependent on dollar payment rails for debt service face acute risk in a weaponization scenario. Turkey, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Vietnam — all flagged in current data as stressed — face stagflationary energy import costs *plus* financial access risk. **Directional view: selective EM underweight, particularly energy importers with dollar-denominated debt.**

**Defense and payment tech:** Firms building alternative clearing infrastructure — and defense contractors on the back of European NATO buildup — are structural beneficiaries of this geopolitical environment. Not a tactical call; a structural one.

## 6. The Signal vs. Noise Question

The financial press in 2026 is full of "dollar collapse" and "SWIFT is dead" narratives. These are almost certainly wrong — at least on any investable timeline.

What is *actually* happening is more subtle and more important for long-horizon investors:

- The dollar payment rail is not being replaced. It is being *duplicated*.
- A parallel system is being built that serves a specific subset of global transactions — primarily transactions where one or both parties fear dollar weaponization.
- This parallel system is small today but growing. The 2026 Iran war has given it a use case it didn't fully have before.
- Over a 10–20 year horizon, a world with two payment rails is structurally different from a world with one. It reduces dollar pricing power, increases commodity volatility, and creates new arbitrage opportunities between financial systems.

**CEO Portfolio Note:** The structural multi-year thesis is: long gold, long non-dollar reserve assets, long energy producers outside the Gulf, modestly long CNH internationalisation plays (via Hong Kong infrastructure). Short: dollar hegemony *at the margin* over 5–10 years. Not short the dollar tomorrow.
