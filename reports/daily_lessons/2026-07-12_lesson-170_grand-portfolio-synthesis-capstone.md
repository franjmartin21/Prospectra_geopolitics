## I. The Meta-Framework: Five Structural Regimes Running Simultaneously

Before building a portfolio, a geopolitical investor must answer one prior question: **What kind of world are we in?** The answer determines which of your structural theses are in season and which are dormant.

The curriculum has identified **five structural regimes** operating simultaneously as of mid-2026. This is unusual — normally one or two dominate. The concurrent activation of all five is the defining feature of this investment environment and the reason why standard portfolio frameworks (Sharpe ratio maximization, factor models, mean-variance optimization) are insufficient tools. They were built for worlds that are more stable than this one.

### Regime 1: Fiscal Dominance — Emerging but Not Yet Arrived

**Definition:** The moment when a government's financing needs begin to materially constrain central bank policy — when the central bank can no longer raise rates sufficiently to control inflation without triggering a sovereign debt crisis or political interference.

**Where we are:** The US is in the early stages. The deficit-to-GDP ratio runs at approximately 6.5% even at full employment. Interest expense has overtaken defense as the largest mandatory spending line. The bond vigilantes returned in Q1 2026 (Lesson 149), driving the 10-year Treasury to 5.4% before the Fed signaled a structural floor. The Warsh Fed (Lessons 126-127) has maintained independence, but the $1.5 trillion FY2027 defense budget proposal — combined with Social Security and Medicare obligations — makes the math increasingly unsustainable without either growth acceleration or some form of financial repression.

**Investment implication:** The long-term neutral rate for US Treasuries is higher than the market priced for the 2010s. Duration risk is asymmetric — long duration positions in US Treasuries face structural headwinds over a 3-5 year horizon. Real assets (gold, commodities, inflation-linked bonds, real estate in supply-constrained markets) deserve a permanent allocation that would have been unnecessary in the 2015-2021 era.

**Falsification condition:** Fiscal dominance thesis fails if: (a) real growth accelerates above 3% sustainably (AI productivity boom validates the optimist case), OR (b) a significant entitlement reform passes Congress (extremely low probability), OR (c) a deflationary shock (deep recession, credit crisis) causes the Fed to cut aggressively and the long-end rallies. Monitor the 10-year TIPS yield — if it falls below 1.5% sustained, revisit.

### Regime 2: Geopolitical Fragmentation — Structural, Not Cyclical

**Definition:** The unwinding of the rules-based international order as the organizing logic of global economic integration, and its replacement by a system organized around great-power blocs, preferential trading arrangements, and explicit economic statecraft.

**Where we are:** The fragmentation is structural and advancing, not reversing. The evidence:
- Section 301 tariffs (Lesson 153) are permanent features of the US trade posture, not negotiating chips
- IEEPA tariffs (Lesson 155) expanded the legal architecture for trade as a weapon
- Export controls on semiconductors (Lesson 56) are tightening, not relaxing, and other countries are copying the architecture
- FDI screening (Lesson 54) has become standard practice across the G7
- Friendshoring (Lesson 59) is reshaping where factories are built — Mexico, India, Vietnam are the primary beneficiaries

**Investment implication:** The one-world, most-efficient-supplier model is over. The new model is friend-first, efficiency-second. This is inflationary (redundant supply chains cost more than optimized ones) but creates structural investment opportunities in the friendshoring destinations and in the infrastructure (ports, energy, logistics) required to make them work.

**Falsification condition:** Fragmentation thesis fails if: a transformative US-China trade deal fundamentally reverses the decoupling trajectory. The probability is below 10% over a 3-year horizon given domestic political constraints in both countries. This is not a condition to watch closely — it is a tail, not a base case.

### Regime 3: The Energy Transition Dislocation — Running Two Systems Simultaneously

**Definition:** The world is attempting to transition from a fossil fuel energy system to an electrified, renewable energy system while simultaneously experiencing energy insecurity (Hormuz, Russia, supply fragility) that requires maintaining the legacy system at high investment levels.

**Where we are:** The energy transition has not reduced demand for hydrocarbons — it has created a **two-system investment mandate**. Oil demand remains at historically high levels (approximately 103 mb/d) driven by non-OECD growth. Natural gas demand is growing as it serves as the transition fuel. Simultaneously, solar, wind, battery storage, and EV adoption are growing at rates that will eventually displace hydrocarbon demand — but "eventually" is measured in decades, not years. In the interim, the capital starvation of traditional energy (ESG-driven underinvestment from 2015-2022) has created structural undersupply that Hormuz (Lesson 98-99, 129-144) made visible.

**The critical minerals overlay:** The energy transition requires more copper, lithium, cobalt, rare earths, and nickel than the current global supply system can deliver. This is not a future problem — it is a current problem. EV adoption growth is already constrained by lithium and nickel supply. The DRC (Lesson 116) and critical mineral supply chains (Lesson 5, 159) are structurally underinvested.

**Investment implication:** Long hydrocarbons (oil, LNG, uranium) AND long critical minerals simultaneously. These are not contradictory positions — they are both required by the two-system reality. The pure energy transition narrative (short fossil fuels, long renewables) and the peak demand narrative (all fossil fuels are stranded assets) have both been proven premature. The **energy security premium** is now permanently embedded in energy prices.

**Falsification condition:** Energy thesis fails if: AI-driven material science discovery dramatically reduces critical mineral intensity of batteries (reducing lithium and cobalt demand) AND a rapid demand destruction event (deep global recession) crashes oil prices below $50 sustained. Neither is the base case.

### Regime 4: Great-Power Competition Militarization — The Defense Supercycle

**Definition:** The simultaneous rearmament of the US, all major NATO members, Japan, South Korea, India, and Australia — driven by the credible threat of peer-level military conflict — creating a multi-decade defense spending supercycle with no precedent since the Cold War.

**Where we are:** This is the most clearly confirmed structural regime:
- US: $895B base defense budget, $1.5T FY2027 proposal
- Germany: Debt brake suspended, 3.5% GDP defense target
- Japan: 2% GDP target, $48B/year increase, new FMS purchases
- South Korea: $350B defense investment pledge
- India: Fastest-growing major defense budget globally
- Australia: AUKUS nuclear submarine commitment

The total global defense spend is now above $2.4 trillion and will exceed $3.6 trillion by 2030. The US primes capture Foreign Military Sales flows from every allied rearmament — Japan buying F-35s, Korea buying PAC-3, Poland buying HIMARS, all flow through US defense contractors.

**Investment implication:** Defense equities are in a multi-decade demand supercycle. The capacity constraint stocks (BWXT, GD submarine, RTX munitions ramp) are more attractive than the program-dependent stocks because demand growth is constrained by physical production, not budget appropriation.

**Falsification condition:** Defense supercycle thesis fails if: a comprehensive great-power peace agreement (US-China normalization, Russia-Ukraine settlement, Iran comprehensive deal) removes the structural threat that is driving rearmament. The probability of this scenario within 3 years is below 15%. Monitor: US-China diplomatic channel openings, Taiwan Strait incident frequency (declining = risk to thesis), Russia-Ukraine ceasefire durability.

### Regime 5: The Dollar System Under Managed Stress — Not Collapse, But Restructuring

**Definition:** The dollar's role as the world's primary reserve currency, trade invoicing currency, and financial infrastructure backbone is being challenged — not by a single competitor, but by a diffuse set of parallel architectures (CIPS, bilateral currency agreements, gold accumulation by central banks, BRICS settlement mechanisms) that are reducing the margin of dollar dominance without replacing it.

**Where we are:** The dollar's share of global FX reserves has fallen from ~72% in 2001 to approximately 57% in 2026. Gold central bank buying is at multi-decade highs. China's CIPS payment system processes growing volumes. BRICS members increasingly invoice bilateral trade in local currencies. But the dollar has no substitute as a global reserve asset — the Triffin Dilemma (Lesson 67) ensures that any credible alternative faces the same structural tension between domestic and reserve currency demands. The RMB (Lesson 61) is not ready: China's capital account remains closed, its bond market lacks the depth and liquidity of US Treasuries, and the Communist Party is not willing to accept the external constraints that come with a true reserve currency.

**The practical implication:** The dollar's structural position is resilient but its value is not. Dollar weakness (in inflation-adjusted terms) is the most likely long-term outcome of fiscal dominance. **Gold** (Lesson 65) is the cleanest hedge against this specific combination of "dollar remains dominant system" plus "dollar loses purchasing power." This is exactly the scenario where gold outperforms.

**Falsification condition:** Dollar restructuring thesis fails if: US fiscal consolidation exceeds expectations (extremely low probability), OR the Fed delivers a sustained real rate shock that attracts capital into dollar assets at the expense of growth (possible but would require politically difficult policy). Monitor: DXY trend (falling DXY over 12+ months = thesis confirmation), central bank gold buying (rising = thesis confirmation), CIPS transaction volume growth.

## II. The Portfolio Framework: Translating Five Regimes into Positions

### A. Portfolio Construction Principles

Before the positions: five principles from the curriculum that must constrain portfolio construction.

**Principle 1 — Minimum 6-month horizon on every position.** Geopolitical cycles are slow. Any position where the thesis depends on being right within 90 days is not a geopolitical investment thesis — it is a trade. We are not in the trading business.

**Principle 2 — Every position must have a stated falsification condition.** If you cannot articulate what would make you wrong, you do not have a thesis — you have a preference. The investment log requires an explicit falsification condition for every recommendation.

**Principle 3 — No single-stock concentration risk above 8% of portfolio.** Geopolitical analysis identifies *structural themes* — which sectors, asset classes, and geographies benefit from structural forces. Single-stock selection introduces company-specific risk (management failure, accounting fraud, single program failure) that geopolitical analysis cannot hedge.

**Principle 4 — Real assets deserve a permanent allocation in this regime.** In a world of fiscal dominance, de-dollarization pressure, and energy security premiums, real assets (gold, energy, critical minerals, real estate) perform a structural role that fixed income performed in the 1990s-2010s period. The "60/40" portfolio — 60% equities, 40% bonds — is structurally broken for this macro regime.

**Principle 5 — Track every call, grade every falsification, update every thesis.** The investment log is not administrative overhead — it is the mechanism for improving the framework over time. Lessons 123 and 129 demonstrated this with the Hormuz Scorecard. This discipline must extend to every position.

## V. The Investment Thesis in One Page

For the CEO's records — the core thesis that underlies the entire portfolio, compressed to its essence:

**We are in a structural break from the 2010s investment environment.** The three conditions that made a 60/40 portfolio optimal — low and declining inflation, falling interest rates enabling duration extension, and peaceful globalization enabling supply chain efficiency — are gone. They have been replaced by:

1. **Structural inflation pressure** (fiscal dominance, energy security premium, reshoring costs)
2. **Higher structural interest rates** (term premium restoration, debt ceiling politics, foreign creditor caution)
3. **Geopolitical fragmentation** (bloc competition replacing globalization)
4. **Rearmament** (the largest peacetime defense build in modern history)
5. **Energy security premium** (the end of energy-as-commodity, the beginning of energy-as-strategic-asset)

The portfolio that performs in this environment is not the portfolio that outperformed 2010-2021. It holds more real assets. It is long defense. It is short long-duration bonds. It has no passive faith that China-linked supply chains will remain stable. It uses geopolitical frameworks as a primary input into asset allocation decisions, not as a qualitative overlay on a quantitative model.

The thesis will be wrong in specific positions. The Hormuz call (Lesson 98-99) was partially wrong — the structural resolution came faster than anticipated. The bond vigilante timing (Lesson 149) was early. This is expected. The framework improves through error, not despite it.

**The one sentence thesis:** *Geopolitical fragmentation, fiscal dominance, and the great-power competition militarization supercycle represent the three most powerful structural forces in the investment landscape over a 5-10 year horizon — and most portfolios are positioned as if none of them are happening.*
