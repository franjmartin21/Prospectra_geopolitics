## Part II: Historical Grounding — The Four Phases of the US-China Trade War

### Phase 1: First Salvo (2018–2019, Trump 1.0)

The Trump administration launched the first major trade offensive in March 2018, imposing 25% tariffs on $50B of Chinese goods under Section 301 (intellectual property theft). China retaliated symmetrically. Escalation continued through mid-2019, reaching $360B of US tariffs on Chinese goods.

**Investment lesson from Phase 1:** Markets initially sold off (panic phase), then recovered as a partial deal appeared likely. The "Phase 1 deal" of January 2020 was hailed as resolution. It was not. It was a pause.

**Key misread that most investors made in 2018-2020:** They priced this as a bilateral dispute between two leaders (Trump and Xi) that would be resolved when political incentives aligned. They missed the bipartisan structural shift in Washington — from engagement to strategic competition — that had begun well before 2018 and would outlast any administration.

---

### Phase 2: The Biden Consolidation (2021–2024)

Biden did not reverse Trump's tariffs. He added to them strategically:
- **CHIPS and Science Act (2022):** $52B in semiconductor manufacturing subsidies + 25% investment tax credit. The first explicit industrial policy to counter Chinese technology dominance.
- **Inflation Reduction Act (2022):** $370B in clean energy investment — simultaneously a domestic climate bill and a strategic play to capture critical mineral and EV supply chains before China.
- **Export controls (October 2022 + 2023):** The most sweeping technology denial measures in US history. Blocked advanced semiconductor chips (below 16nm), chip manufacturing equipment (ASML, KLA, Applied Materials), and AI accelerators (Nvidia A100/H100 class) from export to China.
- **Entity list expansion:** Added hundreds of Chinese firms to the restricted commerce list.

**The structural signal most investors missed:** When a Democratic administration not only maintained but amplified Republican tariffs and added unprecedented technology controls, the bipartisan consensus on strategic competition with China was confirmed. This was not a Trump policy. This was US strategic doctrine.

---

### Phase 3: Trump 2.0 Escalation (January 2025 – Present)

Trump returned with an explicit "economic nationalism" mandate. The escalation has been more sweeping:
- **IEEPA tariffs (April 2025):** Invoked emergency powers to impose broad tariffs on nearly all Chinese imports. Initially 145%, later modulated.
- **Supreme Court IEEPA ruling (2026):** Court struck down the IEEPA tariff authority as exceeding statutory limits, forcing the administration to revert to Section 301 authorities.
- **Current posture (June 2026):** 30% total tariffs on Chinese goods (20% "fentanyl tariffs" + 10% "reciprocal" tariff under Section 301). USTR proposing additional 12.5% tariff on forced labor grounds. 60-day pause on higher tariffs announced June 11 — negotiations ongoing.

**China's structural response (the move most investors are underpricing):**
- In June 2026, China introduced **outbound investment laws** designed to prevent firms from easily exiting Chinese supply chain contracts. This is Beijing using its regulatory architecture to create exit costs — making "China Plus One" strategies more expensive and slower.
- Simultaneously, China is accelerating its own friend-shoring: deepening supply chain ties with Russia, Central Asia, Africa, and ASEAN nations that remain neutral in the US-China rivalry.

### Phase 4: The Structure Underneath the Negotiations (2026 and Beyond)

Here is the investment-critical insight: *the trade war headline is the surface. The technology war is the structure.*

No trade deal — including any deal that might emerge from the June 2026 negotiations — has or will address:
- Export controls on advanced semiconductors and manufacturing equipment
- Restrictions on US investment in Chinese technology sectors
- China's export controls on critical minerals (gallium, germanium, graphite, rare earths)
- CHIPS Act domestic semiconductor manufacturing mandates
- Friend-shoring requirements embedded in US federal procurement

These measures are now statutory, bureaucratically embedded, and politically irreversible. Even a comprehensive trade deal would require years of implementation and could be reversed by the next administration. The technology denial architecture cannot be negotiated away in any 60-day window.

**The five-year investment thesis rests on this asymmetry:** Tariff rates will fluctuate (cyclical risk); technology decoupling will deepen (structural opportunity).

## Part III: Geopolitical Significance Assessment (Step 2)

**Is this structural or cyclical?**

Definitively structural. The evidence:
1. **Bipartisan consensus:** Every major trade and technology control measure since 2022 has had bipartisan congressional support. This is not a partisan policy.
2. **US national security doctrine:** The 2022 and 2025 National Security Strategies both explicitly name China as the "pacing threat" requiring technological superiority maintenance.
3. **China's own strategic posture:** China's dual circulation strategy, the "Made in China 2025" upgrade (now "Made in China 2035"), and the June 2026 outbound investment laws are not defensive reactions — they are an independent strategic program that would exist regardless of US trade policy.
4. **Allied convergence:** EU, Japan, South Korea, Netherlands have all implemented or are implementing export controls on advanced semiconductors aligned with US policy. This is no longer unilateral US pressure — it is a coordinated Western technology denial architecture.

**Does this change the hierarchy of power?**

Yes, potentially decisively. The specific question is whether China can achieve technological self-sufficiency in semiconductors — the industrial backbone of 21st-century economic and military power — before the export controls create an insurmountable gap. This is the strategic race that defines the next decade.

**The Thucydides Trap application (Lesson 62):** The US-China rivalry is a textbook power transition scenario. The trade war is one visible manifestation. The semiconductor war is the more consequential one. Rising powers historically challenge incumbent powers through economic competition before (or instead of) military confrontation. China is doing this precisely — building economic leverage while avoiding direct military conflict.

**What are the second-order effects?**

| First-Order (already priced) | Second-Order (pricing in progress) | Third-Order (not yet priced) |
|---|---|---|
| US-China trade volumes down 50% | Supply chain diversification to Vietnam, India, Mexico | New technology standards competition (5G, AI, quantum) replacing old supply chains |
| Tariff cost pass-through to US consumers | CHIPS Act fab construction (TSMC Arizona, Intel Ohio, Micron Idaho) | Bifurcated global technology ecosystem — products, standards, AI models split by bloc |
| Chinese export controls on gallium/germanium | Non-Chinese rare earth development (MP Materials, Australia) | New reserve currency architecture as technology blocs consolidate |
| Semiconductor stock repricing (Nvidia export control impact) | Indian and Vietnamese manufacturing ecosystems maturing | Whether China can produce competitive 7nm chips domestically by 2028-2030 |

## Part VI: CEO Directional Views — Five Positions with Explicit Theses

### View 1: Long Critical Mineral Independence (STRUCTURAL — 3-7 years)
**Thesis:** China's export controls on gallium, germanium, graphite, and rare earths are the mirror image of the semiconductor export controls the West has placed on China. This is a structural materials shortage for Western defense and technology industries that will not resolve without 3-7 years of mine and processing plant development outside China.

**Instruments:** MP Materials (MP) — the only fully integrated rare earth miner/processor in the Western hemisphere. Lynas Rare Earths (LYC.AX) — Australian processor with US DoD contracts. Energy Fuels (UUUU) — expanding into rare earth extraction as a by-product of uranium mining.

**Horizon:** 3-5 years
**Size:** Core position
**Falsifying condition:** China reverses export controls as part of a comprehensive trade agreement AND new non-Chinese processing capacity comes online from multiple sources, eliminating the supply gap before Western processors can scale

---

### View 2: Long TSMC (TSM) via Friend-Shoring Premium (STRUCTURAL — 2-5 years)
**Thesis:** TSMC is the unavoidable chokepoint of global semiconductor supply. Its Arizona expansion (N4 process, ramping 2025-2027) makes it simultaneously a geopolitical hedge AND a capacity play. The friend-shoring premium — the valuation premium for geopolitically safe manufacturing — is not yet fully priced.

**Instruments:** TSM (ADR), or TSM common on TWSE for the pure play
**Horizon:** 2-5 years
**Falsifying condition:** Taiwan Strait military conflict that destroys or forces evacuation of TSMC Hsinchu operations (tail risk hedge required — see Lesson 88); OR Arizona fab cost overruns make the US expansion economically unviable at scale

---

### View 3: Long India as the Structural Winner of Decoupling (STRUCTURAL — 5-10 years)
**Thesis:** Established in Lesson 34 and Lesson 100. India is the largest single beneficiary of the "China Plus One" strategy. It has the labor force, domestic market, neutral geopolitical positioning, and English-language technical workforce that makes it the preferred manufacturing and service destination for supply chains exiting China.

**New addition from June 2026 data:** With China actively blocking supply chain exits through its new outbound investment laws, the premium on alternative supply chain locations (that are *not* subject to Chinese regulatory control) has increased.

**Instruments:** INDA ETF for broad exposure; Infosys (INFY) for the services supply chain thesis; L&T (engineering/infrastructure construction that builds the new India manufacturing base)
**Horizon:** 5-10 years
**Size:** Largest individual country allocation in the portfolio
**Falsifying condition:** Major domestic political instability, border conflict with China, or India pivoting away from neutral positioning toward either US or China bloc alignment

---

### View 4: Long Vietnam Infrastructure / Short China Manufacturing Equities (RELATIVE VALUE — 2-4 years)
**Thesis:** Vietnam is absorbing the labor-intensive manufacturing that is leaving China fastest (textiles, electronics assembly, furniture). Its stock market is still fundamentally valued as a frontier market rather than an emerging market, despite its supply chain integration with global tech companies (Samsung, Apple, Intel all have major operations there).

**Instruments:** VNM ETF (Vietnam); selectively, MCHI (MSCI China ETF) short as the relative value expression of manufacturing share loss
**Horizon:** 2-4 years
**Falsifying condition:** Vietnam-China political crisis (Vietnam walks a tight geopolitical line given its South China Sea disputes and economic dependence on China); OR China's domestic consumption market proves strong enough that manufacturing export dependence is replaced by domestic-demand-driven growth

---

### View 5: Long the Bifurcation Premium on Domestic Chinese Technology (CONTRARIAN — 3-5 years, high risk)
**Thesis:** This is the contrarian thesis most Western investors are unprepared for. China's technology sector has been subject to maximum negative sentiment in Western markets. But China's response to the technology denial architecture has been a massive, state-backed acceleration of domestic semiconductor design (Huawei HiSilicon, CXMT for DRAM, SMIC for logic), AI infrastructure (Baidu's Ernie, domestic compute clusters), and clean energy technology (CATL's global dominance in battery storage).

The market is pricing these as permanently impaired. The reality: for the Chinese domestic market (1.4 billion people and the world's largest manufacturing base), the question of "can Chinese AI chips compete with Nvidia globally" is irrelevant. The question is "can Chinese AI infrastructure meet Chinese domestic demand?" — and the answer is yes, within 3-5 years at a quality threshold sufficient for most industrial applications.

**Instruments:** This requires direct access to Hong Kong / mainland Chinese markets. KWEB ETF (Chinese internet — the best liquid proxy for domestic tech); CXSE (WisdomTree China ex-State-Owned Enterprises — focuses on private sector Chinese tech)
**Horizon:** 3-5 years; requires explicit willingness to hold through continued Western regulatory headwinds
**Risk rating:** HIGH — political, regulatory, and delisting risks are real and require a smaller position size
**Falsifying condition:** Chinese semiconductor domestic capability chronically fails to meet domestic demand requirements AND Chinese government pivots away from state technology support (extremely low probability)

The trade war headline generates noise. The structural decoupling generates signal.

The investment question is not "will the 60-day tariff pause produce a deal?" That question has the wrong time horizon. The investment question is: **"In a world where the US and China are building parallel technology, manufacturing, and energy architectures — who builds the infrastructure, who processes the materials, who writes the software, and who finances the construction?"**

The answers: India and Vietnam for manufacturing; Western defense/tech for chips and AI; non-Chinese miners for materials; domestic Chinese champions for the Chinese market. These are the structural positions. The trade deal headlines are the noise that creates entry points.
