## Opening Question

*You closed your first institutional client. The contract is signed. The wire is coming. The six months of outreach, calls, sample reports, and pricing negotiations are behind you.*

*Now the most dangerous moment in the Prospectra client lifecycle begins — and most institutional research firms don't know it.*

*Before reading on: What is the primary reason that an institutional research client who genuinely valued a product at the point of signing does not renew 12 months later? Assume the research quality remained consistent. Assume the price didn't change. Assume no relationship breakdown occurred. What is the structural failure that produces a non-renewal from a client who was a genuine believer at the time of signing?*

The answer is not dissatisfaction. It is **non-embeddedness** — the research was consumed passively but never integrated into the institution's actual investment decision process. The client read the reports but did not change how they made decisions. When renewal came, the honest internal evaluation was: "We can justify not renewing this because nothing in our process breaks without it." That evaluation happens in 10 minutes and ends an otherwise successful client relationship.

The antidote is not better reports. It is a deliberate, structured onboarding process designed to create integration — the point at which the GRI framework is not a subscription a client reads, but a tool embedded in their investment workflow.

## I. The Churn Anatomy — What Actually Goes Wrong

To design an onboarding architecture that prevents churn, you need to understand how churn happens at institutional research firms. The pattern is consistent across the category.

### The Passive Consumption Trap

Most institutional research products are delivered, consumed passively, and evaluated at renewal based on a vague impression of value. The client received the weekly reports. They read some of them. They found them interesting. They may have even forwarded one to a colleague. But at no point in the 12 months did the research explicitly change a position, inform a portfolio decision, or become cited in an investment committee memo.

When renewal arrives, the procurement team asks the portfolio team: "Did you use this?" The honest answer is: "We received it. We read it. But we can't point to a decision it drove." The product is cut. The research firm is surprised because the client never complained.

This is the structural failure. The research firm was producing signal. The client was not consuming it as signal — they were consuming it as content. Content is optional. Signal is essential.

### The Integration Gap

The gap between "content consumption" and "signal integration" is the gap the onboarding architecture must close. Integration requires three things:

1. **The client knows which GRI signals are specifically relevant to their portfolio** — not generically relevant, but tied to actual positions or risk exposures they are currently managing
2. **The GRI output is connected to an internal workflow** — a standing agenda item in the investment committee, a portfolio review template that includes GRI scores, a risk monitoring protocol that references the framework
3. **At least one decision has been made in which the GRI framework played a visible, cited role** — a position sized differently because of a GRI signal, a country weight adjusted because of a regime shift detection, a risk committee discussion framed using GRI country scores

If by the end of Month 3 the client has all three, they will renew. If they have none of them at Month 11, they will not.

## II. The 90-Day Activation Framework

The Prospectra onboarding architecture is structured around 90 days, organized into three phases of 30 days each. Each phase has a specific objective, a defined set of deliverables, and a measurable success condition.

### Phase 1 (Days 1–30): Portfolio Mapping and GRI Calibration

**Objective:** The client understands which countries, regions, and themes in the GRI framework are most relevant to their specific portfolio — and the Prospectra team understands their portfolio deeply enough to make the research specifically useful.

**Deliverables:**

1. **Portfolio Intake Session (Day 1–7):** A 60-minute structured call (not a product walkthrough — a listening session) in which the Prospectra CEO maps the client's portfolio exposure by country, sector, and asset class. This session produces a **Client Portfolio Map** — a one-page document listing the 10–15 GRI-tracked countries and themes most directly relevant to this client's positions.

2. **Calibrated GRI Output (Day 7–14):** The first post-signing GRI output is delivered with a client-specific overlay — for each country in the client's Portfolio Map, the GRI score and regime status is highlighted and connected to their specific exposure. This is not the standard weekly output with their name on it. It is a customized analytical brief that proves the framework is specifically relevant to them, not generically relevant.

3. **Onboarding Check-In Call (Day 28):** A 30-minute call with the primary contact to confirm: (a) the Portfolio Map is accurate, (b) the calibrated output was useful, (c) there are any portfolio changes or new focus areas to incorporate. This call should not be a support call or a sales call. It is a calibration checkpoint — "Is this hitting the right signals for you?"

**Phase 1 Success Condition:** The client's primary contact can name, without prompting, two or three GRI signals that are currently relevant to positions they are managing. If they can do this, the research has begun to map onto their decision process. If they cannot, Phase 2 is at risk.

---

### Phase 2 (Days 31–60): Workflow Integration

**Objective:** The GRI framework becomes connected to an internal institutional workflow — a meeting, a template, a protocol — that ensures the research is consumed systematically rather than optionally.

**Deliverables:**

1. **Integration Proposal (Day 35):** A one-page document — delivered by email, not a call — that proposes specifically how the GRI output could be incorporated into the client's existing investment process. Not a generic suggestion. A tailored one, based on what was learned in the Portfolio Intake Session.

   Examples of integration proposals:
   - "For the monthly Investment Committee risk review: we recommend adding a GRI Country Scorecard for your five highest EM-exposure positions as a standing agenda item. We can prepare this in the format your committee uses."
   - "For your weekly portfolio review: we recommend flagging any GRI regime shift signal above threshold 0.7 for positions in that country as a standing review trigger. We can alert you directly by email when a signal crosses threshold."
   - "For your annual strategic asset allocation review: we recommend incorporating the GRI 12-month outlook for each country in your allocation model as a risk input. We can prepare an SAA-ready country risk summary format."

2. **One Integration Event (Day 45–60):** Work with the client to execute one of the proposed integrations — ideally, preparing a GRI-formatted brief for one investment committee meeting, one portfolio review, or one risk report during this period. This does not have to be elaborate. One two-page GRI Country Scorecard used in one meeting is sufficient. The goal is the first instance of the research being used in a real decision context.

3. **Month 2 Check-In Call (Day 55):** 20 minutes. One question: "Did the [format/brief/scorecard] work for the meeting — and what would make it more useful next time?"

**Phase 2 Success Condition:** At least one integration point exists — the GRI framework appears in a meeting agenda, a risk template, or a workflow document at the client institution. It does not have to be deeply embedded. It has to exist.

---

### Phase 3 (Days 61–90): Proof of Value and Expansion Seeding

**Objective:** The client has a first-person, citable experience of GRI-informed analysis contributing to an investment decision — and the Prospectra team understands whether the client is a candidate for tier expansion or deeper workflow integration.

**Deliverables:**

1. **Quarterly GRI Review (Day 75–90):** A 45-minute call presenting the quarter's key regime shift signals in the client's tracked countries, with explicit portfolio implication commentary for their specific exposures. This is not a summary of what was delivered. It is a forward-looking analytical brief: "Here is what changed in your portfolio's risk environment over the past 90 days, here is what we are watching, and here is the directional view on each major signal."

2. **Case Study Moment (Day 75):** If a GRI signal fired during the onboarding period that was relevant to a position the client held — even if the position decision predated the signal — prepare a one-page retrospective showing the signal timing, the investment implication as Prospectra framed it, and the subsequent market outcome. This is not a self-promotional exercise. It is an analytical audit that demonstrates the framework's real-time relevance. Even one case study delivered in the first 90 days dramatically increases the probability of the client incorporating GRI into future decision rationale.

3. **90-Day Onboarding Report (Day 88–90):** A brief, structured document delivered to the client — not a deck, not a pitch, just a 2-page operational summary — covering: (a) the key GRI signals relevant to their portfolio that fired in the first 90 days, (b) the integration touchpoints completed, (c) the Prospectra recommendation for Phase 2 of the engagement (which might be a tier upgrade, a deeper integration format, or simply continuation at the current tier).

**Phase 3 Success Condition:** The client's primary contact has at least one specific example they can cite — to their colleagues, to their investment committee, to a renewal decision-maker — of GRI analysis informing a portfolio discussion. This example is what prevents the "we can't point to a decision it drove" response at renewal.

## III. The Engagement Ladder — From Passive Reader to Embedded Partner

Beyond the 90-day framework, the long-term account management architecture follows an engagement ladder — a progression of integration depth that both measures client health and creates natural expansion opportunities.

| Level | Description | Renewal Risk | Expansion Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Passive Consumer** | Receives and reads reports; no workflow integration | Very High | Must be moved to Level 2 within 60 days of signing |
| **2 — Active Reader** | Engages with specific signals; asks follow-up questions; forwards content internally | Medium | First integration point is achievable — pursue it |
| **3 — Workflow User** | GRI framework incorporated into at least one standing meeting or review process | Low | Watch for expansion into additional countries or asset classes |
| **4 — Decision Integrator** | GRI signals cited in investment committee discussions or position rationale | Very Low | Expansion conversation is appropriate; reference client candidacy |
| **5 — Embedded Partner** | GRI framework is part of the institution's documented risk management process | Negligible | Tier upgrade, custom deliverables, and strategic partnership pricing |

**The goal of onboarding is to move every new client from Level 1 to at least Level 3 within 90 days.** The difference between Level 2 and Level 3 is a single integration event — one meeting, one template, one standing agenda item. That step is within the Prospectra team's control. It does not require the client to do something difficult. It requires a specific, tailored proposal and the execution of one customized deliverable.

## IV. The Reference Client Program — Turning Clients Into Distribution

A well-executed onboarding architecture produces a secondary dividend beyond renewal: reference clients. In institutional sales, a warm introduction from a peer institution is worth approximately 20 cold outreach sequences. A reference client who will take a five-minute call on behalf of Prospectra — "Yes, we use this, here is what we got out of it" — is the most efficient distribution asset an early-stage institutional research firm can have.

**The conditions for a reference client:**
- They reached Level 4 or Level 5 on the engagement ladder
- They have at least one citable example of GRI analysis informing a real decision
- The relationship is personal — they know the CEO, not just the product
- They were explicitly asked, and said yes

**How to build the reference client network:**
1. Identify at Month 3 which clients have reached Level 3+
2. At the Month 3 Quarterly Review call, ask directly: "Is there anyone in your network at a similar institution who you think would find this useful? I'd value an introduction — and if you're willing to be a reference when we're in conversations with peers, that would mean a lot."
3. Build a reference client matrix: client name, institution type, regions/themes they can speak to, willingness to take introductory calls

**Target: 3 reference clients by end of Year 1.** Three well-placed references at a family office, an endowment, and a macro hedge fund are sufficient to generate 20+ warm introduction conversations — which, at a 25% response rate, produces 5 qualified discovery calls. That is meaningful pipeline from zero incremental outreach cost.

## V. Historical Parallel — How Bloomberg Built Stickiness at Institutional Scale

The challenge Prospectra faces in moving clients from passive consumption to embedded integration is not new. Bloomberg solved this problem in the 1990s at massive scale — and the lessons from their model are directly instructive.

When Bloomberg terminals first entered institutional trading desks, the adoption challenge was not awareness. The challenge was embeddedness. A trader who learned to do their analysis in Bloomberg — who learned the keyboard shortcuts, who built their custom screens, who wrote their own functions — became radically price-insensitive. Bloomberg could charge $24,000 per terminal per year because the switching cost was not the money. The switching cost was the embedded workflow: six months of custom screens, historical data queries, and analytical shortcuts that lived only in Bloomberg and nowhere else.

Bloomberg engineered this embeddedness deliberately. Their account managers were trained not to sell terminals — they were trained to teach traders how to use specific Bloomberg functions that were most relevant to that trader's specific workflow. The more workflow-specific the learning, the more embedded the terminal. The first Bloomberg sales rep who walked onto a fixed income trading desk and spent an hour teaching a trader how to build a custom yield curve in Bloomberg was not selling a subscription. They were building a switching cost.

The Prospectra parallel: every time a client's portfolio review meeting includes a GRI Country Scorecard, the client is building workflow-specific knowledge of how to interpret GRI signals in the context of their portfolio. That knowledge is not transferable to a generic geopolitical news service. It is Prospectra-specific. It creates the same switching cost dynamic at a small scale that Bloomberg built at an enormous one.

**The insight:** Embeddedness is not an outcome of good research. It is an outcome of deliberate teaching — showing clients how to use the framework in their specific context, rather than delivering the framework and hoping they figure out how to apply it.

## VI. The Databricks Angle

Client success management is a data problem, and the Prospectra onboarding architecture generates data that can be tracked and analyzed.

**Client Health Score Pipeline:**

| Component | Data Source | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Report open rate | Email delivery tracking (SendGrid/Mailgun) | Passive engagement — are they reading the reports? |
| Response latency | Email thread timestamps | Active engagement — how quickly do they reply to check-ins? |
| GRI signal relevance score | Portfolio Map x current GRI output | Are we delivering the right signals for their portfolio? |
| Integration milestone status | Manual CRM entry | What level on the engagement ladder are they on? |
| Case study count | Account notes | How many citable GRI-decision moments exist for this client? |

**The Prospectra Client Health Score:** A composite score (0–100) for each client, updated weekly, combining the above inputs into a renewal risk indicator. Any client below 60 at Month 9 should trigger a direct outreach from the CEO — not a support email, but a substantive analytical call that reconnects the research to their current portfolio priorities.

The Databricks implementation: a Delta Lake table with one row per client per week, populated via a combination of automated inputs (email tracking API) and manual inputs (integration milestone updates from the CEO). A simple SQL model computes the health score. A Databricks job flags clients below threshold every Monday morning alongside the GRI weekly output.

**This closes the loop between the analytical platform and the client success function.** The same infrastructure that produces the GRI output also monitors whether clients are deriving value from it — making the Prospectra data platform both the product and the retention engine.

## Investment Implications

The onboarding architecture is not just an operational framework — it illuminates a core investment principle relevant to evaluating institutional research and data businesses:

**The terminal value of an institutional research business is determined primarily by its embedded client percentage, not its subscriber count.** A firm with 200 subscribers at Level 1–2 is a much weaker business than a firm with 50 subscribers at Level 4–5. The first has high churn risk, weak pricing power, and low barriers to displacement. The second has near-zero churn, strong pricing power, and a moat built from workflow integration rather than product quality.

This applies directly to evaluating firms like FactSet, Verisk, Wood Mackenzie, and MSCI as portfolio holdings. What separates them from weaker peers is not the quality of their data. It is the depth of workflow embedding: FactSet's portfolio management templates, Verisk's underwriting models, MSCI's index integration into institutional mandates. Each of these integrations makes the data product progressively harder to replace, compounding the switching cost over time.

**For Prospectra:** Building Level 4–5 clients is not just a success metric — it is the primary value creation activity in Year 1. A portfolio of 10 embedded clients is worth more strategically than 40 passive subscribers, because embedded clients generate references, case studies, testimonials, and co-development opportunities that passive subscribers never provide.

*Asset class implication:* Data infrastructure businesses with embedded institutional workflows — specifically those where the switching cost is workflow-specific rather than data-specific — are among the most defensible compounders in the technology sector. The key underwriting variable is not subscriber count or revenue growth rate; it is the percentage of revenue derived from clients who would face meaningful operational disruption if they cancelled.
