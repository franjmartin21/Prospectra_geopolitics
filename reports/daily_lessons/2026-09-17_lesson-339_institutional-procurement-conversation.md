## I. The PM/CFO Gap — Two Different Decisions

### The PM's Decision (Analytical Legitimacy)

The PM — the portfolio manager, analyst, risk officer, or investment director who first encounters Prospectra — is making an analytical judgment:

- Does this framework make analytical sense?
- Is the GRI methodology coherent and defensible?
- Have the signals been right? What's the track record?
- Does this add something I don't already have?

The PM evaluates Prospectra the way they evaluate any research input: does it produce signal or noise? The PM conversation is the one Prospectra has been optimizing for in its public publishing, methodology document, and advisory board construction. The PM is the champion. The PM is not the buyer.

### The CFO's Decision (Contractual Risk Management)

The CFO is not evaluating the methodology. The CFO is evaluating the vendor relationship:

- **Is this vendor legally structured in a way that creates liability for us?** (Answer: the legal architecture from Lesson 338 addresses this)
- **What exactly are we paying for, and how is it defined?** (Answer: subscription agreement terms)
- **If this vendor's analysis is wrong and we lose money, what is our recourse?** (Answer: none — and the subscription agreement must say this explicitly)
- **Is there a multi-year commitment or can we cancel?** (Answer: cancel provisions must be clear)
- **Does this price scale with users, or is it per-seat?** (Answer: Prospectra must decide its licensing model)
- **Who else at our institution will have access?** (Answer: the user count and access architecture matter to compliance)
- **Is there a discount for multi-year commitment or multi-seat access?** (Answer: Prospectra must have a defensible answer)
- **What happens to our data if we cancel — does Prospectra retain anything?** (Answer: data handling provisions in the subscription agreement)

The CFO conversation is procedural, contractual, and risk-focused. It is not asking whether the GRI model is good. It is asking whether the vendor relationship is appropriately structured. The CFO has seen research vendors come and go. They have seen research vendors whose analysis looked compelling but whose contractual terms were sloppy, whose data handling was ambiguous, or whose pricing was designed to trap customers in escalating commitments. The CFO's instinct is not hostile — it is professional skepticism.

**The implication for Prospectra:** Every piece of institutional credibility infrastructure (the methodology document, the advisory board, the legal architecture, the subscription agreement) has two audiences simultaneously — the PM, who reads it for analytical credibility, and the CFO, who reads it for contractual clarity. Prospectra's institutional package must satisfy both.

## II. The CFO's Seven Standard Questions — And Prospectra's Prepared Answers

These are the questions that appear, in some form, in virtually every institutional procurement process for a research service below $50,000/year. Prospectra must have a prepared answer to all seven before the first institutional contract is sent.

### Question 1: "What are we paying for, precisely?"

**What they want:** A clear scope definition — not "geopolitical intelligence" but a specific description of what the subscription delivers: frequency, format, coverage, access channels.

**Prospectra's prepared answer:**
*"The Prospectra Institutional Research Subscription delivers: (1) one weekly geopolitical intelligence briefing covering 5 geopolitical events with directional investment implications, distributed every Monday by 8:00 AM Eastern; (2) the Geopolitical Risk Index, updated weekly for 10 covered countries and regions; (3) ad hoc signal alerts when GRI readings cross defined threshold levels; (4) access to the full briefing archive (52 weeks rolling); and (5) an annual subscriber methodology review call with the CEO. All content is delivered via encrypted email distribution and available in the subscriber portal."*

This answer is specific, deliverable-based, and leaves no ambiguity. The CFO can match it against the subscription price and make a judgment about value.

### Question 2: "What is the pricing structure — per-seat, per-entity, or flat subscription?"

This question is operationally significant. The wrong answer creates either (a) a price that doesn't scale with multi-user access, leaving Prospectra undermonetized, or (b) a per-seat price that creates friction in institutional procurement where access is distributed across a team.

**The recommended structure for Prospectra at this stage:**

| Tier | Description | Price | Users Covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual | Single named user; personal account | $6,000/year | 1 |
| Team | Up to 5 named users; same entity | $12,000/year | Up to 5 |
| Institutional | Up to 15 named users; same entity | $18,000/year | Up to 15 |
| Enterprise | Unlimited users; single legal entity | $28,000/year | Unlimited |

**The structural logic:**
- The jump from Individual to Team ($6,000 to $12,000) is a 2× price for 5× users — a large discount that incentivizes upgrade without feeling punitive
- The jump from Team to Institutional ($12,000 to $18,000) is a 50% increase for 3× users — a smaller discount that reflects the diminishing marginal value of additional seats as distribution widens
- Enterprise pricing ($28,000) is for institutions where firm-wide distribution is a selling point (a research committee that circulates briefings across the portfolio team) — rare at this stage, but the pricing architecture should accommodate it

**What Prospectra should NOT do:** price per named seat at $2,000/user. This creates procurement friction every time the subscribing institution adds a user. Tier-based pricing removes this friction and simplifies the renewal conversation.

### Question 3: "Is there a multi-year discount?"

This question is a trap if answered without a policy. A CFO who negotiates a 10% multi-year discount establishes that Prospectra's pricing is negotiable — which means every subsequent institutional prospect will open by asking for a discount.

**The recommended position:**

- No automatic multi-year discount. Published prices are annual.
- One specific exception: a 2-year commitment earns a 5% discount on Year 2 only (not Year 1). This rewards commitment without establishing a general negotiability pattern.
- Communicate the exception as a policy, not a negotiation: "We offer a one-time 5% discount on Year 2 for subscribers who commit to a 2-year term at signing. This is the only pricing accommodation we make, and it's applied uniformly."

**The psychological logic:** Communicating this as a policy rather than a concession removes the negotiation dynamic. The CFO gets a discount; the CFO does not get the sense that pushing harder would yield more. This preserves pricing integrity for future renewals and future institutional prospects.

### Question 4: "If your analysis is wrong and we lose money, what is our recourse?"

The honest answer is: none. The subscription agreement must say so explicitly. But the framing matters.

**Prospectra's prepared answer:**
*"Prospectra provides research and analysis, not investment advice. Our subscription agreement makes clear that the analytical framework we publish is one input into institutional decision-making — not a basis for action without independent analysis by your investment team. Historical analytical performance is documented in our signal log, which is publicly accessible. We have never claimed that our GRI signals translate mechanically into investment outcomes; the framework is a structured lens, not a trading signal. The subscription agreement limits Prospectra's liability to fees paid in the 12 months preceding any claim. This is the standard structure for all research service vendors."*

This answer is honest, is legally accurate, and frames the research positioning as a feature rather than a limitation. The disclaimer architecture from Lesson 338 is the foundation.

### Question 5: "What is the cancellation policy?"

**The recommended position:**
- Annual subscriptions: cancel with 30 days written notice before the annual renewal date for no further obligation; no mid-year cancellation refund
- Month-to-month (Individual tier only, by exception): cancel with 30 days notice at any time

**Why no mid-year refund:** Research services are consumed in real time — a subscriber who cancels mid-year has already received the analysis delivered. The policy is not punitive; it is the standard structure for research services and should be stated clearly in the subscription agreement.

### Question 6: "Who owns the research output — can we republish internally?"

**The recommended position:**
- Subscribers have the right to circulate Prospectra briefings internally within their subscribing entity (all named users on the account) for internal analytical purposes
- Subscribers do not have the right to republish externally, distribute to entities not party to the subscription, or use Prospectra content in client-facing communications without explicit written consent
- Prospectra retains all IP in the methodology, the GRI formula, and the briefing content

The internal circulation right is important: institutions need to be able to share research briefings with their investment committee. Restricting this would be operationally impractical and would create friction in the procurement process. The restriction on external distribution protects Prospectra's IP and prevents subscribers from effectively building competing services on Prospectra's analytical output.

### Question 7: "What happens to our data if we cancel?"

**Prospectra's answer is simple:** Prospectra does not store subscriber data beyond the information required to manage the subscription relationship (name, email, entity, billing contact). There is no "subscriber data" that belongs to the subscriber — they are the customer for analytical content, not a data provider. The briefings and GRI data they received remain theirs for their own use; Prospectra retains the analytical IP.

This answer is easy and should be stated clearly in the subscription agreement. It removes a standard procurement concern before it generates friction.

## III. The Multi-Stakeholder Sales Conversation — Sequencing the PM and CFO

Understanding that the PM and CFO are different decision-makers, with different criteria, the institutional sales process has a specific sequence:

### Stage 1: PM Qualification (Weeks 1–3)

Goal: determine whether the PM is genuinely interested and whether the institution is a qualified prospect (research budget, appropriate AUM, investment style compatible with geopolitical risk analysis).

**What to do:** Have the analytical conversation. Send the methodology document. Share the signal log. Answer questions about the GRI framework. Let the PM make the analytical judgment.

**What NOT to do:** Jump to pricing before the PM is sold. A PM who hasn't decided whether they want the product will not advocate for it in the internal procurement process. The PM must be a champion before the CFO process starts.

### Stage 2: The PM-to-CFO Handoff

When the PM says: "I'd like us to subscribe — what's the process?" — this is the handoff moment. The PM is about to go to the CFO. This is the moment to prepare the PM as an internal advocate.

**What to provide the PM at this moment:**
1. The pricing sheet (all four tiers, clearly formatted)
2. A one-page "Compliance FAQ" document (from Lesson 338 Action 3)
3. A copy of the subscription agreement (for CFO and legal review)
4. The methodology document (already provided; confirm the PM has it)
5. A one-paragraph description of Prospectra for the PM to include in their internal recommendation: "Prospectra is a geopolitical intelligence subscription service providing weekly geopolitical risk analysis with directional investment implications across energy, currencies, and emerging market equities. The service is structured as a research product, not investment advice, and is appropriate for use as an analytical input alongside other research tools. The methodology has been independently reviewed and the signal track record is publicly documented."

**Why this paragraph matters:** The PM will be asked to explain Prospectra to the CFO and potentially to the investment committee. Without a prepared description, the PM will improvise — and an improvised description may either oversell (creating compliance concerns) or undersell (making the service sound unimportant). Providing the language gives the PM the words to make the case correctly.

### Stage 3: The CFO Conversation

The CFO conversation rarely involves Prospectra directly. It happens between the PM and the CFO, using the documentation Prospectra provided. The CFO will have one or more of the Seven Standard Questions (Section II). The PM needs to have answers, or the CFO sends the PM back to get answers from Prospectra.

**Prospectra's role at Stage 3:** Be immediately responsive. When the PM comes back with a CFO question — "The CFO is asking about the cancellation policy" — answer within the same business day with a clear, documented response. A slow or unclear response at Stage 3 is one of the most common reasons institutional sales stall. The CFO has other things to do; a vendor who takes three days to answer a simple contractual question signals operational immaturity.

### Stage 4: Contract and Onboarding (Weeks 4–6 from initial contact)

The subscription agreement is sent, signed, and returned. Billing is established. The subscriber is onboarded: confirmed user list, portal access enabled, first weekly briefing delivered to all named users.

**The onboarding call:** For institutional subscribers at Team tier and above, Prospectra schedules a 30-minute onboarding call with the primary contact (usually the PM who championed the subscription). Agenda: walk through the briefing format, confirm how the GRI scores should be interpreted, answer any initial questions. This call serves two functions: (1) ensures the subscriber understands the product correctly, preventing early dissatisfaction from misaligned expectations; (2) begins the personal relationship that drives renewal 12 months later.

## IV. The Enterprise Exception — When to Offer a Custom Arrangement

For most institutional prospects, the four-tier pricing structure handles the conversation. There is one scenario where a custom arrangement may be appropriate: an institutional prospect who has a specific, named use case that doesn't fit the standard product.

**Example scenarios that might warrant custom discussion:**
- A sovereign wealth fund wants to embed the GRI scores into their internal risk management system as a data feed (API access rather than weekly PDF briefings)
- A university endowment wants to license the methodology and co-brand an academic research project using the Prospectra GRI framework
- An asset manager wants a bespoke country coverage set (GRI scores for 20 specific countries they operate in, rather than the standard 10)

**The rule for custom arrangements:**

Engage in custom discussions only when: (a) the institution has established that they are a serious, qualified prospect at Institutional or Enterprise tier; and (b) the customization is architectural (data access, coverage scope, co-branding) rather than merely pricing (wanting a discount).

Custom pricing discussions that are merely discount negotiations should be redirected to the standard structure. Custom architecture discussions that add genuine value to the relationship (and create pricing justification above published rates) are worth the conversation.

**The minimum enterprise custom price:** $35,000/year. Any custom arrangement that requires Prospectra to do meaningfully more work (expanded coverage, API access, co-branding) should price above the $28,000 enterprise tier. Pricing below this for custom arrangements signals that Prospectra undervalues the additional work.

## V. Historical Case — How Comparable Research Services Have Navigated Multi-Stakeholder Procurement

### Eurasia Group — Institutional Procurement at Scale

Eurasia Group's institutional clients include sovereign wealth funds, tier-1 asset managers, and multinational corporations. Their product line ranges from the annual Top Risks report (available at $3,000–$5,000/year for single-user access) to bespoke analytical engagements ($50,000–$500,000+) for clients who want tailored geopolitical analysis integrated into their strategic planning.

**What Eurasia Group has learned:** The transition from "subscriptions" to "advisory relationships" happens when the institutional client has internal demand for geopolitical intelligence that exceeds what a standardized subscription product can serve. The standard subscription converts the PM. The advisory relationship converts the C-suite. Eurasia Group's revenue concentration is in the advisory relationship tier — custom engagements with named institutional clients — not in subscription volume.

**The Prospectra implication:** The four-tier subscription architecture is the right structure for Year 1. It creates the customer base and the track record. But Prospectra should watch for the signal that an institutional client is generating internal demand that exceeds the subscription product — because that signal is the moment to begin a bespoke advisory conversation at a materially higher price point.

### Oxford Economics — The Multi-Year Subscription Model

Oxford Economics, which provides macroeconomic forecasting to institutional clients, has built a multi-year subscription structure that generates high renewal rates. Their standard institutional subscription (database access + quarterly briefings) is annual with automatic renewal unless canceled 90 days in advance. The 90-day cancellation notice window is significantly longer than Prospectra's proposed 30-day window.

**What the 90-day window achieves:** It forces the renewal conversation to happen 3 months before the end of the subscription period — when both the PM (who values the service) and the CFO (who is reviewing vendor contracts) are engaged on the renewal simultaneously. By the time the 90-day window opens, Oxford Economics has already been in contact with the client about next year's coverage expansion, new data releases, and any pricing adjustments. The renewal conversation happens on Oxford Economics' timeline, not the client's.

**The Prospectra option:** A 60-day cancellation notice window (rather than 30) would give Prospectra the same structural advantage — the renewal conversation happens 60 days before expiration, when Prospectra can actively engage. This is worth considering as institutional subscription volume grows.

### BCA Research — Single-Price, No Negotiation

BCA Research, a Canadian macro research firm with 80+ years of institutional history, maintains a strict no-negotiation pricing policy. Their published prices are their prices. Institutional clients who push back on pricing are told: "We offer the same terms to all subscribers. Our analytical track record speaks for itself."

The no-negotiation policy has a specific effect: it filters the prospect pool to institutions who have already decided Prospectra's value is worth the published price. Clients who are primarily motivated by getting a discount are not the highest-value clients. The policy also preserves pricing integrity across the subscriber base — a discounted subscriber who learns that a peer institution pays full price becomes a dissatisfied subscriber.

**The Prospectra implication:** The no-negotiation stance, combined with the one specific exception (2-year commitment = 5% discount on Year 2), should be communicated as policy at the beginning of the pricing conversation — not as a defensive response to a request for a discount.

## VI. Investment Implications

### For Prospectra's Revenue Architecture

The multi-stakeholder pricing model has a direct impact on revenue concentration:

| Scenario | Subscribers | Blended ACV | Annual Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scenario A — Individual dominant | 8 Individual, 2 Team | $7,000 avg | $56,000 ARR |
| Scenario B — Institutional uplift | 5 Individual, 3 Team, 2 Institutional | $9,800 avg | $98,000 ARR |
| Scenario C — Enterprise anchor | 4 Individual, 2 Team, 2 Institutional, 1 Enterprise | $11,600 avg | $139,200 ARR |

The gap between Scenario A and Scenario C is primarily driven by the two Institutional and one Enterprise subscribers — five contracts representing $84,000 of the $139,200 in Scenario C. This is classic B2B revenue concentration: a small number of high-ACV institutional relationships drive the majority of revenue.

**The implication:** Prospectra's sales architecture should allocate disproportionate time and attention to the institutional tier, even when institutional sales cycles are 3–4× longer than individual sales. One institutional conversion at $18,000/year requires the same weekly publishing infrastructure as one individual conversion at $6,000/year — but produces 3× the revenue.

### For Understanding Research Service Equity Valuations

Research service companies that successfully institutionalize their subscriber base — transitioning from individual/retail subscribers to institutional contracts with multi-year commitments — typically re-rate at higher EV/Revenue multiples. The market for geopolitical intelligence services has historically valued institutional revenue at 4–6× annual revenue vs. 2–3× for retail subscription revenue, primarily due to lower churn, longer contract duration, and higher switching costs.

Understanding this dynamic has direct implications for how Prospectra should think about its future equity story if it reaches a commercialization stage: institutional revenue concentration is not just a cash flow benefit, it is a valuation multiplier.
