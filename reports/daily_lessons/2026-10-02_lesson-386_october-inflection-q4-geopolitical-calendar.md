## The Core Concept: Event-Driven vs. Structural Investing

There's a tension at the heart of geopolitical investing that the curriculum has circled for months:

**Structural investors** (our stated identity) hold positions over 6-18 months because geopolitical cycles unfold slowly. They buy European defense in May and expect to be right by the following spring.

**Event-driven investors** trade around specific catalysts — an election result, a central bank meeting, a sanctions announcement.

The truth is that the best structural investors are also event-aware. They don't *trade* events, but they use them as **thesis checkpoints**. An election confirms or breaks the structural story. An FOMC meeting reveals whether the monetary constraint they expected is materializing. A failed ceasefire signals that the conflict premium will persist longer than priced.

**The Q4 2026 calendar is therefore not a trading calendar — it's a thesis validation calendar.**

For each event cluster, the question is: *Does the outcome tighten or loosen my conviction in the structural position?*

## The Q4 2026 Event Map

### OCTOBER 2026

**1. FOMC Meeting — October 28-29**

This is the first FOMC under Warsh's full control with the market having fully processed his Jackson Hole framework. The key question: does Warsh deliver on the "geopolitically constrained Fed" doctrine, or does strong US growth data give him cover to stay higher for longer?

*Thesis check:* Our structural view is fiscal dominance eventually wins — inflation remains structurally elevated, real rates compress over time, dollar weakens vs. hard assets. If Warsh holds rates and signals prolonged constraint, this thesis *strengthens* for the medium term. If he pivots dovish due to growth slowdown, it *accelerates* dollar weakness — also constructive for our commodity/EM positions but for different reasons.

*Watch: 10-year Treasury yield reaction, USDX move, gold/oil response.*

**2. US Midterm Elections — November 3 (but campaign enters final sprint in October)**

The October pre-election environment matters: polling shifts, policy announcements, and any October surprises can move markets before the actual event. 2026 midterms carry unusual weight because:
- Defense spending authorization bills move faster with or without House majority
- Tariff escalation authority is tied to executive action, but Congressional composition affects China policy credibility
- Infrastructure/industrial policy (IRA-adjacent) is partly in play

*Thesis check:* European defense rearmament thesis is relatively insulated from US midterms. Critical minerals thesis depends partly on US IRA policy continuity. A House flip toward gridlock slightly *weakens* the fiscal dominance thesis (less spending) but could *strengthen* it perversely (debt ceiling theatrics → fiscal credibility crisis → dollar weakness).

**3. China Q3 GDP Release (mid-October)**

China typically releases Q3 GDP in mid-October. Given the structural fragility thesis (demographics, property overhang, export overcapacity), the Q3 number will be watched for signs of stimulus effectiveness and whether deflation is deepening.

*Thesis check:* Our view is China managed decline, not hard landing. A weaker-than-expected Q3 print (+3% handle or below) with PBoC non-response would signal the hard landing scenario is gaining probability. That's *bearish* for copper and Australian miners, *bearish* for EM Asia exporters, *neutral to bullish* for US Treasuries.

### NOVEMBER 2026

**4. US Midterm Elections — November 3**

The outcome that matters most for our portfolio:
- **Divided government (most likely scenario):** Structural neutral. Markets price relief that neither extreme governs. Dollar marginally weaker. Status quo for defense and critical minerals policy.
- **Full Republican sweep:** Tariff escalation risk rises. China decoupling accelerates. Dollar temporarily stronger before fiscal concerns reassert. Defense authorization easier to pass.
- **Democratic sweep (lowest probability):** IRA reinforcement. Clean energy supply chain investment accelerates. Copper, lithium, rare earths *bullish*. Domestic defense spend likely maintained.

*Portfolio implication:* The election is an input, not a trigger. Don't trade it; use the result to recalibrate 12-month conviction on specific sector tilts.

**5. OPEC+ Meeting (likely early December, but decisions form in November)**

After the Hormuz episode, OPEC+ cohesion is under maximum test. Saudi Arabia is managing the tension between wanting higher prices (Vision 2030 fiscal needs) and not giving market share to US shale at $90+/bbl.

*Watch in November:* Any pre-meeting signals about production quotas, compliance disputes (UAE/Iraq overcompliance history), or bilateral oil deals that could destabilize the cartel.

*Thesis check:* Our oil range view is $75-95/bbl with geopolitical tail risk upward. An OPEC+ production cut extension supports the floor. A surprise supply increase would force a thesis revision.

**6. G20 Summit**

In 2026 the G20 is hosted by South Africa. The summit will carry unusual weight given:
- Post-Hormuz geopolitical tensions
- Warsh-era dollar diplomacy
- Critical minerals supply chain negotiations
- BRICS+ parallel track

*Watch:* Any joint communiqué language on trade, sanctions, or financial architecture. The signal-to-noise ratio is low, but the side-meeting diplomacy (bilateral US-China, US-Saudi, India-EU) matters.

---

### DECEMBER 2026

**7. Year-End Dollar Liquidity Dynamics**

Every December, year-end balance sheet constraints create predictable dollar funding pressure. In 2026, with US fiscal expansion and higher Treasury issuance, the year-end squeeze may be sharper than usual. This is a structural/cyclical overlay on our theses, not a new catalyst.

*Watch:* Repo rate spikes, FX swap basis widening, EM currency pressure. A sharper-than-usual year-end squeeze is *bearish* for EM FX in the short term but irrelevant to 12-month structural positions.

**8. Iran Nuclear Talks — Ongoing Decision Window**

The most binary event in the Q4 calendar. A successful nuclear framework (P5+1 successor deal) would release approximately 1.5-2 mbpd of Iranian crude over 12-18 months, fundamentally altering the oil supply picture. A breakdown accelerates the nuclear threshold scenario.

*Thesis check:* This is the key stress test for the energy thesis. We hold the view that no deal gets done before end-2026 given domestic Iranian political constraints and Israeli red lines. If that view is wrong, Brent rerates down toward $70 and the Hormuz premium evaporates.

## Investment Implications

**Asset classes to watch closely against this calendar:**

**1. Brent Crude** — Every single Q4 event affects it. The range $75-95 holds unless: Iran deal (breaks floor) or OPEC+ fracture (breaks floor) or new Hormuz incident (spikes through ceiling). At $80-85, the risk-reward still favors holding energy exposure into Q4.

**2. EUR/USD** — Warsh holds + fiscal expansion narrative = dollar under mild pressure. European rearmament fiscal spending = slightly EUR positive. Target range 1.10-1.15 over 12 months. October FOMC is the key calibration event.

**3. European Defense Equities (RHEINMETALL, SAAB, LEONARDO, THALES)** — The thesis is not event-dependent; it's structural. But November US midterms are the one event that could create short-term volatility (if NATO commitment signals change). Treat any October-November weakness as entry points, not thesis breaks.

**4. Copper / Critical Minerals** — The key Q4 risk is China GDP surprise. A sub-4% Q3 print with weak industrial data would compress copper temporarily. Our view: any China-driven pullback in copper below $9,000/mt is a structural buying opportunity on the energy transition demand thesis. The 2-3 year structural demand outlook doesn't change because China slows for one quarter.

**5. Gold** — The thesis is fiscal dominance + reserve diversification + EM central bank buying. None of these are Q4 event-sensitive. Gold is the portfolio position most insulated from the Q4 calendar. Hold through all events.
