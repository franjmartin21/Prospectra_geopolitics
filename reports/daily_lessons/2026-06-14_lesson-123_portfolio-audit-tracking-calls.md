## Opening Framing Question

Here is the uncomfortable truth about investment frameworks: almost every analyst who holds a well-constructed thesis can sound right for months before the market decides. The Hormuz thesis sounded right from April through June. The uranium thesis sounds right today. The gold thesis has been right for 14 months and counting.

**But being right is not the same as being right *for the right reasons*.**

A portfolio audit is not a performance review. It is a framework diagnostic. The question is not "did this position make money?" The question is: "Did the thesis predict what actually happened? And if it did — was that because the framework was correct, or because we got lucky on a correlated factor we didn't model?"

A framework that makes money because of factors it didn't analyze is not a validated framework. It is a framework waiting to fail.

**This lesson exists to separate the signal from the narrative. We review 13 positions. We score each one honestly. We update the framework where the evidence demands it.**

---

## Section 1: The Audit Framework — How We Score Our Calls

We evaluate each position on three axes:

**Axis 1: Thesis Confirmation (T)**
- T+ = core thesis is confirmed by market behavior and/or real-world events
- T0 = thesis is neither confirmed nor denied (still open, inconclusive)
- T- = core thesis is contradicted by market behavior or events

**Axis 2: Return Attribution (R)**
- R+ = position has generated positive returns (or is on track to)
- R0 = flat / inconclusive
- R- = negative returns (or tracking to loss)

**Axis 3: Framework Score (F)**
- F+ = we predicted the *mechanism* correctly, not just the direction
- F0 = direction correct but mechanism partially missed
- F- = direction correct but for the wrong reasons (lucky); OR direction wrong but framework reason was sound (unlucky)

The goal is F+ across positions. R+ without F+ is dangerous — it rewards analytical sloppiness. F+ without R+ is invaluable — it means the framework works but timing was off, which is fixable.

## Section 5: Framework Lessons — The Audit's Deliverables

**Five framework improvements this audit generates:**

**1. Sector bifurcation before any underweight call.** Any future European sector underweight must first segment defense-adjacent from non-defense. Similarly, any EM equity call must segment commodity-producing from commodity-importing sub-sectors. Blanket sector calls missed intra-sector divergence.

**2. AI infrastructure is now a standard commodity demand input.** Copper, rare earths, power infrastructure, cooling water — these all have AI data center demand as a material and growing factor. Omitting it in commodity thesis construction is a systematic bias going forward.

**3. Nuclear-tier geopolitical events require 30-day monitoring, not 90-day.** India-Pakistan escalated from routine to nuclear signaling in under 2 weeks. Position monitoring intervals must be calibrated to event speed, not portfolio administrative convenience.

**4. Entry discipline is the framework's most valuable property.** The tactical crude short is the proof case. A thesis without an entry trigger is an opinion. An entry trigger prevents the worst version of being right — entering a position that reverses before it works.

**5. Track record compounds. Don't stop auditing.** This is audit #1 over 10 weeks of active positions. The framework improves *proportionally* to the honesty of these reviews. The temptation is to focus on the wins (gold, European defense) and explain away the misses (industrials bifurcation). That temptation is the enemy of a compounding framework.
