## CORE CONCEPT: THE BINARY EVENT PROTOCOL

A **binary event** is a known, dated, discrete geopolitical decision point where the outcome has two plausible states and the market will move materially in one direction within 24-48 hours of resolution.

Binary events are distinct from:
- **Continuous signals** (GDELT event intensity trending — act gradually over weeks)
- **Ambiguous events** (diplomacy, ceasefires — outcome probability diffuse and shifting)
- **Tail events** (true black swans — no known timing, asymmetric sizing required)

Binary events are the most actionable class of geopolitical signal — and the most dangerous if handled incorrectly.

### The Four Failure Modes

**Failure Mode 1 — Averaging into the binary:** Adding position size as the deadline approaches because "conviction is high." If the event resolves against you, you've added at the worst moment. The binary outcome doesn't care about your conviction.

**Failure Mode 2 — Treating the market's pre-positioning as signal:** If the market drops oil 15% ahead of a peace talk, that isn't evidence a deal is coming — it's evidence other participants are also uncertain and speculating. The June 2026 MOU speculation pattern repeated twice this year. Each time, oil dropped 15-20% on talk of talks. Each time, we correctly identified it as speculation and held. The discipline that produced those correct calls is the same discipline that applies to November 10.

**Failure Mode 3 — Sizing for certainty you don't have:** A binary event is 50/50 until it isn't. Even a "90% likely" outcome fails 10% of the time. Position sizing for the event should reflect the actual probability distribution, not your preferred scenario.

**Failure Mode 4 — Ignoring the event entirely:** "I'm a long-horizon investor; I don't trade around events." Correct — but a binary event can permanently alter the structure of a thesis. If China reinstates rare earth export controls tomorrow, that is not a 24-hour event — it is a structural supply-shock that shifts the medium-term supply curve. Ignoring it is not discipline; it is wilful blindness.

This matrix is the most important thing to build before a binary event. It tells you which positions benefit from which outcomes, which are exposed to which outcome, and where you have concentration risk (positions that all move the same direction under the same negative scenario).

### Step 3 — Decide on Pre-Event Sizing

**Three legitimate postures:**

**A — Hold and don't size around the event.** Valid when the binary doesn't change your 12–24 month thesis and the event is binary on timing, not direction. The European defense position fits this: a Ukraine peace deal doesn't close the structural rearmament thesis; it just pauses a catalyst. Hold.

**B — Add before the event in your favored outcome.** Valid only when: (1) you have genuine informational or analytical edge, (2) the add is sized for the probability of being wrong, not the probability of being right. For rare earth miners, the CEO's current directional view is that Chinese controls will reinstate — the structural case for it is too clear for Beijing to retreat without a face-saving APEC framework. But the APEC meeting is November 18-19, and a bilateral Trump-Xi call could change the calculus. Sizing the add: starter position (1-2%) with the right to add post-event confirmation.

**C — Reduce pre-event and re-enter post-confirmation.** Valid when the binary is genuinely 50/50, the downside scenario is damaging, and the premium for holding through the event exceeds the cost of re-entering post-resolution. For oil/tankers, the Iran talks are roughly 50/50 on near-term direction. The structural thesis doesn't change either way. Consider reducing 5-10% going into November 10, re-adding if talks break down.

### Step 4 — Set the Post-Event Response Rule in Advance

This is the most important step, and the one most investors skip. Before the event: write down what you will do in each outcome. Not "reassess on the data" — a specific action with a specific size and a specific trigger.

## HISTORICAL GROUNDING: THE CAMBO MINERAL PRECEDENT (2010-2012)

In 2010, China halted rare earth exports to Japan for 40 days following the Senkaku boat collision incident. The halt was not formally announced — it was enacted through regulatory obstruction of export licenses. Japanese manufacturers (Toyota, Hitachi, Shin-Etsu Chemical) scrambled for supply. Within six months, Japan had initiated a $1.2 billion emergency program to develop domestic rare earth alternatives and diversify sourcing to Australia, Kazakhstan, and Vietnam.

The price of dysprosium — used in permanent magnets — went from $1,400/kg in early 2010 to $2,900/kg by mid-2011. Molycorp (US), which had been dormant, was relisted on the NYSE at $14 in July 2010 and reached $77 by April 2011 — a 450% move driven entirely by the geopolitical supply shock.

**What investors who held Molycorp at $14 knew:** China had used rare earths as a weapon once. They would use them again. The structural case was clear and the entry was cheap. What they got wrong (Molycorp filed for bankruptcy in 2015): they assumed the geopolitical premium was permanent without monitoring whether the supply diversification programs actually produced viable alternatives. The geopolitical insight was right; the exit discipline was absent.

**The lesson for today:** The entry thesis (Chinese controls as supply weaponization) is established. The exit discipline is what this project must build. The kill condition for rare earth miners is not "Chinese controls lifted in perpetuity" — it is "credible non-Chinese processing capacity online, covering >30% of global heavy rare earth demand." That is a 7-10 year thesis, not a 7-10 day one.
