## Opening Question

*You've built the pitch. You've survived due diligence. The investor calls on a Thursday afternoon and says:*

*"We'd like to invest. I'll send over a term sheet tomorrow."*

**Before you open that document — what should you already know about what it contains?**

Most founders open their first term sheet and focus on two numbers: the valuation and the check size. These matter. But they are not the only terms that will govern your business for the next 3–5 years. Some of the most consequential provisions are buried in sections with names like "protective provisions," "pro-rata rights," and "liquidation preference" — terms that seem technical and inert until the moment they determine whether you make money from your company or not.

The founders who negotiate well are not the ones with the best lawyers. They are the ones who understand the economics before the lawyers arrive.

## Part I — The Two Instruments: SAFE vs. Priced Round

### The SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)

A SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) is the dominant instrument for pre-seed and many seed rounds in the US, popularized by Y Combinator in 2013. It is not equity — it is a promise of equity, converted at a future priced round.

**How it works:**
- Investor writes a check today.
- No shares are issued now.
- At the next priced round (typically a Series A), the SAFE converts into equity, usually at a discount to the price paid by Series A investors.

**Key parameters:**
- **Valuation cap:** The maximum valuation at which the SAFE converts. If your cap is $5M and the Series A prices you at $15M, the SAFE holder converts as if the price were $5M — giving them 3x the share count of Series A investors for the same dollar. This is how early investors are compensated for taking early risk.
- **Discount:** An alternative (or additional) mechanism — the SAFE converts at, say, 80% of the Series A price (a 20% discount).
- **MFN (Most Favored Nation):** A clause saying that if you issue a future SAFE with better terms, the current SAFE holder automatically gets those better terms. Protect yourself from issuing multiple SAFEs at progressively better terms without realizing you're re-pricing all prior holders.

**For Prospectra at the current stage:** A SAFE is almost certainly the right instrument. It is fast, cheap, and standard. Legal costs for a SAFE round are $2,000–$5,000 vs. $15,000–$30,000 for a priced round. The typical Prospectra seed raise — $250K–$500K — does not justify the complexity of a priced round.

### The Priced Round

A priced round issues actual shares at a defined price per share, establishing a formal company valuation. It requires:
- A Certificate of Incorporation amendment
- A stock purchase agreement
- A voting agreement
- An investors' rights agreement
- A right of first refusal and co-sale agreement
- An information rights schedule

This is the right instrument for a Series A ($2M+), where investors need formal governance rights and you need a clean cap table. At seed stage, it is premature and expensive.

**Rule of thumb:** Raise your first $500K on SAFEs. Raise everything after on priced rounds.

## Part II — The Economics: Valuation, Dilution, and the Cap Table

### Pre-Money vs. Post-Money Valuation

This is the most commonly misunderstood term in early-stage funding.

- **Pre-money valuation:** What the company is worth *before* the investment.
- **Post-money valuation:** What the company is worth *after* the investment.

**Example:**
- Investor offers $500K on a $4M pre-money valuation.
- Post-money = $4M + $500K = $4.5M
- Investor owns $500K / $4.5M = 11.1% of the company.
- Founders own 88.9% (before any option pool).

**YC's post-money SAFEs** price on post-money valuation, not pre-money — meaning the cap in the SAFE is the *post-money* cap. This makes the math simpler but gives investors slightly more ownership for a given number. Read your SAFE carefully to know which convention it uses.

### The Option Pool Shuffle

This is one of the most important and least understood dynamics in seed term sheets.

Before a priced round, investors will require you to create or expand an option pool for future employees. The question is: is the option pool carved out of pre-money or post-money?

**Investor-standard:** The option pool is carved out of pre-money, which means it dilutes existing founders, not the new investor.

**Example of the shuffle:**
- Investor wants 20% of the company post-money.
- They require a 15% option pool.
- If the option pool is pre-money, founders are diluted by *both* the investor (20%) and the option pool (15%), leaving founders with 65%.
- If post-money, founders would retain more.

**For SAFEs:** This issue doesn't arise at SAFE stage because no shares are issued. It becomes real at the priced round. Know it before it surprises you.

### Dilution Math for Prospectra

If Prospectra raises:
- $300K on a $3M post-money SAFE cap
- Then raises a Series A at $10M pre-money ($2M round, $12M post-money)

The SAFE converts: $300K / $3M cap = 10% of the company.
Series A investor gets: $2M / $12M = 16.7%.
Option pool (15%, pre-money): 15%.
Founders retain: 100% - 10% - 16.7% - 15% = **58.3%**

That is a realistic scenario. It means the founders still control the company economically and have a clean cap table at Series A.

## Part III — Governance Terms That Matter

### Board Seats

At seed stage, most SAFE rounds give investors no board seat — the board remains founder-controlled. At a priced round, investors will typically negotiate one seat.

**Prospectra guidance:** Do not give a board seat to a seed investor unless they are a genuine domain expert who adds operational value. A $200K check does not entitle a passive investor to govern your company. If they insist, a "board observer" role (attends but doesn't vote) is a reasonable compromise.

### Protective Provisions

Protective provisions give investors veto rights over specific major decisions. Standard provisions require investor approval for:
- Selling the company
- Taking on debt above a threshold
- Issuing new shares
- Changing the certificate of incorporation
- Paying dividends

These are standard and acceptable. Watch for non-standard provisions that give investors veto rights over hiring decisions, product launches, or pricing — these are operational overreach and should be pushed back.

### Pro-Rata Rights

A pro-rata right gives an investor the right to participate in future rounds to maintain their ownership percentage. If an investor owns 10% after the seed round and Prospectra raises a Series A, the pro-rata right lets them invest enough to remain at 10%.

**Why this matters:** Pro-rata rights can be valuable to investors (they protect against dilution in a breakout company) and constraining to founders (they limit how much of the next round you can sell to new, higher-value investors). For seed rounds, pro-rata rights are standard and you should accept them. The key is ensuring they have a time limit — typically lapsing after 18–24 months or after the Series A.

### Information Rights

Investors with information rights receive regular financial reporting — typically monthly or quarterly financials and an annual audit. For SAFEs, these are rarely formalized. For priced rounds, a standard information rights package includes:
- Monthly management accounts within 30 days
- Annual audited financials within 120 days
- Annual budget for investor review (not approval)

This is not onerous. Build the discipline of regular investor updates regardless — it is good governance and it keeps investors engaged for the next round.

## Part IV — Negotiation Framework: What to Push Back On

Not all term sheet terms are equal. Here is a prioritized framework:

### Non-Negotiable (Accept Standard Terms)
- Pro-rata rights at seed stage
- Information rights (standard package)
- Standard protective provisions
- SAFE valuation cap at market rate

### Negotiate Firmly
- **Board seats:** Push for observer status at seed; full board seat only for a lead investor with genuine domain value.
- **Option pool timing:** At priced round, push for option pool to be carved post-money.
- **Valuation cap:** The single most important economic term. Don't accept a cap below your evidence-based valuation.

### Red Lines — Walk Away If Demanded
- Founder vesting cliff longer than 1 year (standard is 4-year vest, 1-year cliff)
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution (vs. weighted-average — dramatically punishes founders in a down round)
- Drag-along provisions that allow a small minority investor to force a sale
- Non-compete terms that apply post-company

### The Relationship Rule

Negotiation is not a transaction — it is the first stress test of a long-term relationship. Push hard on terms that matter economically. Let go of terms that are symbolic. Investors who try to win every point at seed stage are signaling how they will behave when things get hard. That is information.

## Part V — Closing Mechanics

Once terms are agreed, the closing process for a SAFE round is:

1. **Term sheet signed (non-binding)** — 1 day
2. **SAFE documents drafted and reviewed** — 3–5 days (use YC's standard SAFE template)
3. **Legal review** — 3–7 days ($2,000–$4,000 in legal fees)
4. **Wire transfer received** — closing is complete
5. **Cap table updated** (pending equity, not yet issued)

**Total timeline from term sheet to close:** 2–3 weeks for a single investor. For a round with 4–6 investors, plan 6–8 weeks as each SAFE is negotiated separately.

**Prospectra action items before first term sheet arrives:**
- [ ] Open a business bank account separate from personal accounts
- [ ] Establish Delaware C-corp (Prospectra Inc. is already done — confirm it is in good standing)
- [ ] Create a preliminary cap table in Carta or equivalent
- [ ] Have a standard YC SAFE template reviewed by a startup lawyer once

## Investment Implications

The structure of your early financing has direct implications for long-term investor returns and founder economics:

**SAFE vs. Priced Round:**
- SAFEs defer valuation negotiation — appropriate when traction is early and valuation is speculative. For Prospectra at $47K ARR, a SAFE is structurally appropriate.
- Priced rounds lock in valuation — appropriate when you have enough evidence that the number is defensible and you need formal governance structure.

**Valuation anchor effect:**
- The cap you set on your SAFE becomes the floor expectation for your Series A. Set it too low and you disadvantage yourself; set it too high and you create a down-round risk. For Prospectra, a $3M–$5M post-money cap is defensible given $47K ARR with institutional clients.

**Dilution math is compounding:**
- Every point of dilution at seed is worth multiples at exit. A founder who dilutes 25% at seed vs. 20% has given away five percentage points of a company that might be worth $50M–$200M at exit. That is $2.5M–$10M in wealth. These numbers justify careful negotiation.
