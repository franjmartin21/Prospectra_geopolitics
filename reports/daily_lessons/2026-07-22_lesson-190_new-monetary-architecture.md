## Opening Framing Question

*If every major geopolitical actor — China, Russia, the Gulf states, India, Brazil — agrees the dollar system is unfair and wants to reduce dependence on it, why is the dollar still 57% of global reserves after 25 years of de-dollarization rhetoric?*

The answer to that question is the whole lesson. And buried inside it is the most important insight for investors: **the transition is real but slow, and the journey is more investable than the destination.**

---

## I. The Problem Everyone Agrees On, and Why They Can't Solve It

Lesson 189 established the structural case for dollar decline: the US fiscal trajectory, the weaponization of sanctions (specifically the 2022 Russian reserves freeze), and the emergence of alternative trade settlement. The question Lesson 190 addresses is harder: **what actually replaces it?**

Start with a brutal historical fact. The dollar replaced sterling as the world's reserve currency — and it took roughly **50 years** (1920–1970) even with two World Wars, the collapse of the British Empire, and Marshall Plan diplomacy actively accelerating the shift. The dollar's current rivals have none of those forcing functions working in their favor. What they do have is:

1. A shared grievance (sanctions weaponization)
2. Rising trade volumes conducted outside the dollar zone
3. An accelerating accumulation of gold as the reserve asset of last resort
4. Early-stage payment infrastructure being built outside SWIFT

But grievance without a credible alternative is just noise. The question is whether the alternatives are becoming credible.

## II. The Four Candidate Replacement Mechanisms

### Candidate 1: The SDR — The IMF's Non-Answer

The IMF's Special Drawing Right is technically a basket reserve asset (USD 43%, EUR 29%, CNY 12%, JPY 8%, GBP 8%). In theory, a reformed SDR could serve as a global reserve anchor — this was actually the Keynes vision at Bretton Woods (he called his version the "Bancor").

**Why it won't work:**
- SDRs can only be held and transacted by central banks and the IMF — no private sector use
- You cannot invoice a commodity contract in SDRs
- Creating enough SDRs to meaningfully replace dollar reserves requires US Congressional approval (the US holds veto power at the IMF)
- The US will never vote to dilute its own monetary privilege

The SDR is a diplomatic fig leaf, not a monetary architecture. Cross it off.

### Candidate 2: The RMB — The Heir Apparent That Isn't (Yet)

China's renminbi is the most obvious candidate — the world's second-largest economy, largest trade nation, largest commodity importer. In theory, commodity markets should price in RMB, and RMB should be flowing into global reserve portfolios.

**What's actually happened:** The RMB's share of global reserves is approximately 2.4% as of Q4 2025. China's trade share is roughly 15% of global commerce. The gap between trade weight and reserve weight is the story.

The structural barriers:
- **Capital account inconvertibility:** China does not allow free capital flows. You cannot hold RMB assets without accepting that Beijing can freeze your access. After watching Russia's reserves frozen, no sovereign wants a currency where capital account closure is a policy option.
- **Lack of deep, liquid bond markets:** Reserve currencies need to offer massive quantities of safe, liquid assets. The US Treasury market is $27 trillion. China's government bond market, for foreign holders, is a fraction of that size with convertibility constraints.
- **Trust deficit:** Reserves are the ultimate savings of last resort. Sovereigns require predictability of property rights, rule of law, and no political risk to their holdings. China doesn't offer this.

The RMB is a **regional trade currency**, not a global reserve currency. It will grow slowly in trade invoice share, particularly in Belt and Road corridor economies. It is not a dollar replacement on any 10-year horizon.

### Candidate 3: Gold — The Ancient Answer Making a Modern Comeback

This is where the real structural shift is happening, and it's happening quietly.

**The data:**
- Central banks globally purchased a net **1,237 tonnes of gold in 2025** — the third consecutive year above the 1,000-tonne threshold
- BRICS+ nations now hold 17.4% of global gold reserves, up from 11.2% in 2019
- China officially added to its gold reserves every month for 18+ months through 2024-2025
- Poland, the Czech Republic, Turkey, India, and a dozen EM central banks have been systematic buyers

**Why now?** The Russian sanctions of 2022 were the inflection point. When the G7 froze $300 billion in Russian FX reserves, every non-allied central bank in the world asked the same question: *what happens to our dollar reserves if we end up on the wrong side of a future sanctions decision?* Gold doesn't have a counterparty. Gold can't be frozen. Gold settles in physical delivery.

This is not a conspiracy theory about a gold standard return. It is rational sovereign risk management by countries who watched their peer's reserves disappear overnight.

The implication: **gold is the reserve diversification trade of the 2020s**. Not as crisis insurance. As monetary architecture.

### Candidate 4: mBridge and the CBDC Payment Rails — The Plumbing Revolution

The most underappreciated development in de-dollarization is happening not in reserve composition but in **payment infrastructure**.

The mBridge project (originally developed under BIS Innovation Hub) is a multi-CBDC platform allowing central banks to settle cross-border transactions in their own digital currencies, bypassing SWIFT entirely. As of 2026, it includes:
- China (digital yuan / e-CNY)
- Saudi Arabia (joined 2024)
- UAE
- Thailand
- Hong Kong

**Why this matters more than the BRICS currency talk:** A BRICS currency requires harmonized monetary policy across economies with wildly divergent fiscal situations (China, Russia, Brazil, India, South Africa) — it's nearly impossible politically. A payment rail requires only technical interoperability. You don't need to agree on monetary policy to agree on a settlement mechanism.

mBridge doesn't replace the dollar as a store of value. It replaces SWIFT as a transaction mechanism. The distinction is critical:

- Dollar as **transaction currency** (medium of exchange): *can* be disrupted by mBridge
- Dollar as **store of value** and **safe asset** (unit of account and reserve): much harder to displace

What mBridge ultimately enables is commodity trade — particularly oil, gas, and minerals — settled without touching the US financial system. If the Gulf states can invoice Saudi oil to China in digital yuan, settled through mBridge, with those yuan then used to purchase Chinese manufactured goods, a complete bilateral trade circuit exists that bypasses the dollar entirely. That circuit doesn't need to be the dominant one to matter for price discovery.

## III. What the COFER Data Is Actually Telling Us

Here is the nuanced reading that the headlines get wrong:

The dollar's share of global reserves fell to **~57%** in Q3 2025, the lowest since 1995, down from 71% in 1999. This sounds dramatic. But look underneath:

- In Q2 2025, **92% of the reported decline** in dollar reserve share was due to **exchange rate valuation effects** — the dollar weakened, making dollar-denominated reserves worth less in SDR terms, reducing the measured share. Active portfolio shifts were minimal.
- Adjusted for constant exchange rates, the dollar's reserve share edged down only 0.12% in that quarter.

**The honest read:** De-dollarization in reserve portfolios is happening at a pace of roughly **0.5–1% per year** in structural terms. At that pace, the dollar remains above 50% of global reserves through 2035. This is not a crisis — it is a multi-decade erosion.

The **gold accumulation** trend is more structurally significant than the headline reserve share numbers, because gold buying doesn't show up as "anti-dollar" in COFER data — it shows up as a different asset class entirely. The real de-dollarization is happening in *what sovereigns buy instead of more dollars*, not in them selling the dollars they already have.

## IV. The Realistic Timeline and Its Investment Meaning

| Phase | Timeline | What Happens | Investment Signal |
|-------|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Slow erosion** | 2025–2030 | Dollar reserve share drifts from 57% → 50%. Gold accumulation continues at 800–1,200 T/year. mBridge expands. Commodity pricing experiments in non-dollar currencies. | Long gold, long gold miners, long commodity-exporting EM sovereigns |
| **Fragmentation** | 2030–2040 | Two or three regional monetary zones solidify: dollar zone, RMB zone, neutral zone. Bilateral swap lines replace multilateral reserve pooling for many EM economies. Commodity contracts increasingly priced in regional currencies. | EM differentiation premium; currency basket exposure; reduce long-duration USD bonds |
| **Post-dollar order** | 2040+ | No single currency holds >40% of global reserves. Settlement infrastructure fully multi-polar. Gold as the settlement backstop between blocs. | Structural gold overweight; commodity supercycle premium; US asset discount |

**Key caveat:** A US fiscal crisis, a major war, or a SWIFT exclusion of a large EM economy could compress this timeline dramatically. Tail risks are asymmetric toward faster transition, not slower.

## V. Investment Implications — The Transition Portfolio

**The core investor insight:** You are not positioning for the *end state* of a post-dollar world. You are positioning for the **transition premium** — the mispricing that occurs when a 25-year structural shift is underway but not yet fully priced.

### Long Positions (6–18 month horizon, with structural multi-year thesis)

1. **Gold (GLD, physical, or royalty companies like WPM, RGLD):**
   - Thesis: Central bank demand structurally elevated. 800–1,200 T/year of sovereign buying regardless of price sensitivity. ETF demand episodic; sovereign demand is structural.
   - Target: $3,500–4,000 range in 2026-2027 if current accumulation pace holds.
   - Wrong if: Dollar rallies sharply on US fiscal consolidation and Fed credibility restored (low probability given current trajectory).

2. **Gold miners (GDX, GDXJ, individual producers):**
   - Thesis: Operational leverage to gold price. Miners lagged gold in the 2023-2025 run. Catch-up trade.
   - Risk: Cost inflation, operational execution, geopolitical risk of mine locations.

3. **Commodity-exporting EM sovereigns with current account surpluses:**
   - Brazil (BRL, IBOV): commodity exporter, active BRICS+ de-dollarization participant
   - Indonesia (IDR): nickel dominance, ASEAN hedge
   - Saudi Arabia (via Aramco or USD-denominated Saudi bonds): straddling both systems
   - India (INR, SENSEX): beneficiary of friend-shoring and non-aligned status

4. **Euro (EUR):**
   - If dollar loses reserve share, euro is the primary beneficiary — it's the second reserve currency and has deep, convertible bond markets. European rearmament creates fiscal spend; ECB managing inflation trajectory.

### Short / Underweight (structural headwinds)

1. **Long-duration US Treasuries (TLT):**
   - If the marginal buyer (foreign central banks) is gradually reducing purchases in favor of gold, the demand function for USTs structurally weakens. Supply is simultaneously exploding due to US fiscal deficits. Classic recipe for duration underperformance over 5–10 year horizon.

2. **USD-only revenue multinationals with large EM exposure:**
   - As EM trade increasingly settles in non-dollar, USD revenue recognition for companies with EM-heavy customer bases faces structural FX headwinds.

### Watch Indicators (signals to monitor in Databricks)

- **IMF COFER quarterly data:** Rate of change in dollar reserve share, adjusted for FX valuation effects
- **World Gold Council central bank survey:** Monthly purchase pace by sovereign
- **BIS data on currency composition of trade invoicing** (released annually)
- **mBridge transaction volumes** (when disclosed)
- **US 10Y real yield vs. gold price:** If this correlation breaks (gold rising despite high real yields), the de-dollarization premium is being priced

## VII. The Signal vs. Noise Verdict

**Signal:**
- Central bank gold accumulation (1,000+ T/year, third consecutive year) — structural, not speculative
- Dollar reserve share below 57%, lowest since 1995 — slow structural erosion confirmed
- mBridge Saudi Arabia participation — payment rail advancing beyond pilot stage

**Noise:**
- BRICS currency launch announcements — no credible design for common monetary policy
- "Dollar collapse" predictions — not supported by COFER data adjusted for FX effects
- RMB displacing dollar on any 10-year horizon — capital account convertibility remains the hard blocker

**CEO Verdict:** The new monetary architecture is being built in three layers simultaneously: (1) gold as the sovereign savings layer, (2) bilateral CBDCs/payment rails as the transaction layer, and (3) a slow drift in trade invoicing away from exclusive dollar use. None of these, alone or together, constitutes a dollar *collapse*. What they constitute is a 20-year **structural discount** on dollar hegemony. Invest accordingly — and build the infrastructure to measure it.
