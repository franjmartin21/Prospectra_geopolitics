## Opening Question

*You entered a copper long six weeks ago. Your thesis: a regional conflict in the Democratic Republic of Congo would disrupt artisanal production, tighten the global refined copper market, and push spot prices toward $12,000/tonne over 12 months.*

*Copper is up 8%. Your entry thesis is playing out. But two things have happened since you entered:*
*1. China's PMI came in at 48.1 — contraction territory, implying weakening demand.*
*2. A ceasefire was announced in the affected region — though conflict observers say it is unlikely to hold.*

**Do you exit? Reduce? Hold? Add?**

Most geopolitical investors make this decision by instinct. The mistake is not the decision itself — it's making it without a protocol. Without a protocol, every update becomes noise that overrides the thesis. You either exit too early on irrelevant data, or hold too long when the structural case has genuinely broken.

This lesson builds the protocol.

## I. The Five Stages of a Geopolitical Trade

Every geopolitical position passes through five stages. Knowing which stage you're in changes how you respond to new information.

### Stage 1: Pre-Entry — Thesis Construction
*(Covered in Lesson 287)*

The thesis exists in writing. You know:
- The geopolitical mechanism (what forces the change)
- The asset channel (how the mechanism reaches price)
- The instrument (how you express it)
- The entry level and sizing logic
- The invalidation conditions (what kills the thesis)
- The time horizon (when you expect it to play out)

If any of these are missing at entry, you don't have a thesis — you have a hunch.

### Stage 2: Position Live — Early Phase (Weeks 1–4)
This is the most dangerous period. The position is new, the P&L is volatile relative to conviction, and every piece of contrary data feels threatening.

**Protocol for Early Phase:**
- Separate P&L noise from thesis noise. A 5% drawdown in week 2 is not a thesis signal. It is market microstructure.
- Monitor **leading indicators**, not price. You entered because of a mechanism. Has the mechanism strengthened, weakened, or been invalidated? Price follows mechanism; mechanism does not follow price.
- Expect the market to test your conviction. Geopolitical trades often move against thesis temporarily as other traders take profits from the initial move.

**The Early Phase Question:**
*"Has anything happened that I did not anticipate in my thesis document — and if so, does it strengthen or weaken the core mechanism?"*

### Stage 3: Position Live — Mid Phase (Weeks 4–12)
The thesis has been tested. Either the trade is working (price is moving in your direction) or it's range-bound while the geopolitical cycle develops. This is where most investors make the critical mistake: they stop monitoring the thesis and start monitoring only the P&L.

**Protocol for Mid Phase:**
- **Quarterly thesis review**: Re-read your original thesis document. Score each assumption: still valid / weakened / invalidated.
- **Signal reinforcement check**: Are new geopolitical developments confirming or contradicting the structural case? The DRC ceasefire in the opening example is a *confirming ambiguity* — a ceasefire that observers expect to fail is not an invalidation; it is the market offering you a temporary re-entry opportunity if you reduced.
- **Position sizing review**: If the thesis has strengthened materially, the original sizing may be too small. The geopolitical risk premium has expanded — do you capture more of it?

**The Mid Phase Question:**
*"If I saw this thesis for the first time today, with current information, would I put the trade on? At what size?"*

If yes at same size: hold.
If yes at larger size: consider adding.
If yes at smaller size: reduce.
If no: exit.

### Stage 4: Position Live — Late Phase (Approaching Time Horizon)
The time horizon is a soft constraint, not a hard stop — but it is a mandatory review trigger. By the end of your defined horizon, the market should have priced most of the geopolitical development you anticipated. If it hasn't, ask why:

**Three explanations for late-phase underperformance:**

1. **The mechanism worked but the market didn't price it**: This happens when the geopolitical development was real but the transmission to the specific asset was weaker than modeled. Lesson: model the transmission more rigorously next time.

2. **The mechanism didn't work**: The conflict de-escalated, the election produced an unexpected result, the supply disruption was absorbed. This is a legitimate thesis failure. Exit, document the error.

3. **The mechanism worked but the timeframe was wrong**: The structural case is intact — the horizon was too short. This is where long-horizon investing's real discipline matters: can you extend the position given current P&L, risk budget, and opportunity cost? If yes, formalize the extension and reset the clock.

### Stage 5: Exit — The Structured Close
Most investors treat the exit as a spontaneous decision. This is a category error. The exit is the final step of the thesis.

**The Geopolitical Exit Checklist:**

☐ **Invalidation reached?** One or more of your pre-defined invalidation conditions has been triggered. Exit regardless of P&L.

☐ **Thesis complete?** The geopolitical mechanism has fully played out. The market has priced it. There is no more edge in the position. Exit into strength.

☐ **Time horizon expired?** You have reached the defined horizon without thesis fulfillment. Evaluate extension (Stage 4 logic) or exit.

☐ **Risk budget exhausted?** The position has hit your maximum drawdown threshold. Exit and reevaluate with fresh analysis.

☐ **Better opportunity identified?** A higher-conviction position requires capital reallocation. This is a legitimate exit reason — not emotion, but capital efficiency.

**The exit document** is written before you close the position. Two paragraphs: what happened, and what you learned.

## II. The Geopolitical Thesis Update Protocol

When new information arrives, you face a classification problem: is this information *relevant to my thesis* or *noise that feels relevant*?

### The Three-Question Filter:

**1. Does this update change the geopolitical mechanism, or just the timing?**

The DRC ceasefire: does it eliminate the supply disruption risk (mechanism change) or delay it (timing change)? If observers agree it won't hold, it's a timing change. The mechanism is intact. Do not exit.

**2. Does this update change the asset channel?**

A China PMI miss: does it fundamentally weaken copper demand enough to overwhelm the supply disruption thesis, or is it a cyclical wobble inside a structural supply constraint? You need to quantify: how much does the supply disruption tighten the market, and how much does the demand miss loosen it? If supply impact > demand impact, thesis intact.

**3. Does this update change what you would have done at entry?**

This is the most important question. It bypasses anchoring bias. You are not asking "should I stay in this position?" — a question contaminated by your current P&L. You are asking "would I enter this trade today?" Clean slate. No sunk cost.

## III. The Post-Mortem — Building the Learning Machine

The post-mortem is the highest-leverage activity in geopolitical investing. It is also the most neglected.

Most investors review losing trades superficially ("the thesis was wrong") and don't review winning trades at all. This is backward. A winning trade with a flawed process can destroy a framework; a losing trade with a correct process provides the foundation for a better system.

### The Six-Question Post-Mortem:

1. **Was the geopolitical mechanism correctly identified?** Did the disruption/election/escalation I anticipated actually occur?

2. **Was the asset channel correctly modeled?** Did the mechanism transmit to the asset price through the pathway I expected?

3. **Was the timing approximately right?** Did the thesis play out within my defined horizon, or was I early or late? How much did timing matter to the outcome?

4. **Did I manage the position correctly?** Did I update or exit at the right moments? Were my decisions protocol-driven or emotional?

5. **What did I miss?** What information was available that I did not incorporate? What structural factor was I wrong about?

6. **What would I change about the process?** One concrete improvement to the thesis construction, monitoring, or exit protocol.

**The post-mortem lives in the investment log** (`reports/investment_log.md`). Every closed position gets a six-question post-mortem within two weeks of closing. This is non-negotiable. Without post-mortems, you iterate on opinions — not evidence.

## V. Investment Implications

### The Position Management Edge

Most geopolitical edge is lost not in the thesis but in the management. Research consistently shows that systematic exit rules outperform discretionary exits — even when discretionary exits are made by analysts with superior information.

**Why:** Information asymmetry at entry (you have better geopolitical analysis than the market) does not persist at exit. By the time the thesis has matured, the information advantage has spread. The exit edge comes from discipline, not information.

**Implication for our framework:** Define your exit conditions at entry, write them down, and commit to them. The post-mortem process creates the feedback loop that makes each subsequent thesis more precisely calibrated.

The lifecycle management protocol maps directly to a Databricks pipeline:

**Pipeline: Thesis Monitor**

```
Input: Investment log (position list, thesis assumptions, invalidation conditions)
Data sources: 
  - GDELT event stream (geopolitical mechanism tracking)
  - FRED (macro leading indicators)
  - Yahoo Finance (price action, volume anomalies)
  - News sentiment (qualitative thesis reinforcement/contradiction)

Processing:
  - For each live position, score each thesis assumption: 0 (invalidated) → 1 (intact) → 2 (strengthened)
  - Aggregate to a Thesis Health Score (0–10)
  - Flag for human review when: Health Score drops 2+ points from prior week OR any single assumption drops to 0

Output: 
  - Weekly Thesis Health Report per position
  - Alert: positions requiring immediate review
  - Quarterly post-mortem trigger when position approaches time horizon
```

**Relevant datasets:**
- GDELT GKG for geopolitical mechanism tracking
- FRED API for macro leading indicator monitoring
- Yahoo Finance / yfinance for price and volatility monitoring

**Feature engineering ideas:**
- Thesis decay score: rate of change of assumption scores over time
- Confirmation ratio: % of new information that supports vs. contradicts the thesis
- Time-in-trade efficiency: P&L per unit of time relative to original horizon
