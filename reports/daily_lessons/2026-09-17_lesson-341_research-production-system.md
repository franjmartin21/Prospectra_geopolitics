## I. The Editorial Calendar: Your Publication Commitment Is a Legal Obligation

When Prospectra's institutional subscription agreement specifies delivery of "weekly geopolitical intelligence briefings and daily lessons," that language is not aspirational. It is the scope of services the client paid for. Missing a publication without notice is a material breach of contract — a fact most early-stage research firms discover when a CFO's legal team marks it up on renewal.

**The editorial calendar is not a productivity tool. It is a contractual architecture.**

### What Goes on the Calendar

The minimum publication cadence for an institutional research subscription:

| Publication | Frequency | Core Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Briefing | Every Monday by 8am client time | 5 geopolitical events, GRI update, Signal vs. Noise verdict, CEO Portfolio Note |
| Alert Memo | As-needed, max 24h after trigger event | One-page rapid analysis when a threshold event occurs |
| Monthly Themes Report | First week of each month | Structural thesis review — what changed and what didn't |
| Quarterly GRI Deep-Dive | Every quarter | Full GRI methodology review, historical score comparison, forward scenarios |

The alert memo is the most frequently underestimated commitment. Institutional clients are not paying for research that arrives on a schedule while the world moves. They are paying for an analytical intelligence function. When the Treasury sanctions drop at 11pm, the question your client is asking at 6am is not "will you cover this next Monday?" It is "can you get me a view today?"

**This is the commitment that separates a $2,400/year newsletter from an $18,000/year institutional intelligence subscription.** The price difference is almost entirely the responsiveness premium.

## II. The Tiered Urgency Framework

Not every geopolitical event deserves an alert memo. The analytical discipline of *not* publishing on every event is as important as the discipline of publishing on the right ones. Institutional clients learn to trust research shops that demonstrate selectivity. The shop that publishes an alert on every tariff announcement, every diplomatic statement, every central bank minute eventually produces the boy-who-cried-wolf problem — clients stop reading because the signal-to-noise ratio has collapsed.

Prospectra's internal triage framework for incoming events:

### Tier 1 — Publish Within 24 Hours (Alert Memo Required)
**Threshold:** The event materially changes Prospectra's existing analytical thesis on a region, sector, or asset class with active GRI coverage.

Examples:
- A sanctions package affecting a country with a GRI score published in the last 30 days
- A surprise central bank rate decision in an EM country covered in an active thesis
- A military escalation crossing a threshold previously defined as a scenario trigger (e.g., confirmed kinetic engagement in a previously tension-only situation)
- An election result that diverges from Prospectra's forecasted scenario

**The test:** Would an institutional client who acts on Prospectra's research be materially harmed by not receiving this analysis today? If yes, publish. If no, hold for the weekly briefing.

### Tier 2 — Include in Next Weekly Briefing (No Alert Required)
**Threshold:** The event is relevant to existing coverage and worth analytical commentary, but does not change the active thesis.

Examples:
- Diplomatic statements consistent with Prospectra's forecast
- Commodity price moves explained by previously analyzed supply dynamics
- Political developments in countries outside active GRI coverage

**The test:** Is this new information, or is this the expected consequence of information we already had? If expected, fold it into the weekly briefing. If new, consider Tier 1.

### Tier 3 — Log and Monitor (Do Not Publish)
**Threshold:** The event is potentially relevant but requires more data before analytical judgment is possible.

Examples:
- Initial reports of a developing situation with conflicting sourcing
- Market moves whose geopolitical cause is unclear
- Geopolitical events in regions outside Prospectra's current coverage scope

**The discipline here is critical:** Publishing preliminary analysis that you subsequently have to retract destroys institutional credibility faster than not publishing. Institutional clients understand that analysis takes time. They do not forgive confident calls that turn out to be based on early, inaccurate reporting.

## III. Sourcing Architecture: Where the Analysis Comes From

The research production system is only as good as the inputs flowing into it. Most early-stage research shops have the same problem: their sourcing is ad hoc. The founder reads whatever surfaces in their feed, synthesizes it into analysis, and publishes it. This works for a newsletter. It does not work for an institutional subscription where the client's legal team just agreed to a 12-month contract on the basis of a methodology document describing a systematic analytical process.

**The sourcing architecture must be intentional, documented, and defensible.**

### Primary Sources (Must Be Reviewed Daily)

| Source Category | Specific Sources | What You're Looking For |
|---|---|---|
| Official government communications | US State Department, Treasury OFAC, EU External Action Service, UK FCDO | Sanctions, diplomatic statements, policy shifts |
| Multilateral bodies | UN Security Council, IMF Article IV consultations, World Bank country assessments | Structural risk assessments |
| Central banks | Fed, ECB, major EM central bank communications | Policy signals and geopolitical commentary in minutes |
| Treaty organizations | NATO communiqués, ASEAN statements, SCO declarations | Alliance architecture shifts |

### Secondary Sources (Reviewed for Signal, Not as Primary Authority)

| Source Category | What You're Looking For |
|---|---|
| Major financial press | Event awareness — NOT the interpretation (that's Prospectra's value-add) |
| Regional specialist outlets | Country-specific depth unavailable in major Western press |
| Academic geopolitical analysis | Structural frameworks to test against current events |
| GDELT event data | Quantitative event frequency and tone shifts (feeds GRI directly) |

### The Sourcing Discipline

The critical discipline is separating *what sources you use* from *how you interpret them.* The institutional subscriber is not paying for a summary of Reuters and the FT. They can read those themselves. They are paying for Prospectra's *analytical framework applied to those events* — the GRI score update, the second-order effects analysis, the asset class implications.

This means the sourcing process and the analysis process must be explicitly separated in the production workflow. Read first. Collect facts. Then analyze separately. Never synthesize while you source — you will unconsciously filter the sourcing to fit the thesis you're already forming.

## IV. The Production Workflow — A Weekly Briefing in Seven Steps

### Monday Briefing (published 8am, example workflow for Sunday-to-Monday production):

**Step 1 — Friday Event Scan (Friday, 30 minutes)**
Review the Tier 1/2/3 triage log for the week. Identify the five candidates for the Monday briefing. Flag anything that might escalate to a Tier 1 alert over the weekend.

**Step 2 — Weekend Monitor (Saturday-Sunday, async)**
Set up news alerts for any Tier 1 threshold events. If a threshold event triggers, write the alert memo immediately regardless of day. Do not wait for Monday.

**Step 3 — Sunday Afternoon: GRI Score Review (1 hour)**
Pull the GDELT pipeline output. Review event frequency, tone, and goldstein score shifts for all covered countries in the past 7 days. Identify GRI score changes ≥ 5 points (threshold for mandatory commentary in the weekly briefing). Identify score changes ≥ 10 points (threshold for Signal vs. Noise declaration).

**Step 4 — Sunday Evening: Briefing Draft (2–3 hours)**
Write the five events in the Signal vs. Noise format: What happened → Geopolitical significance → Investment implications → Directional view. Do not edit while writing. Finish the draft first.

**Step 5 — Monday Morning: Analytical Review (45 minutes)**
Read the draft cold. Ask: Is the thesis defensible? Have I made any claims that are not supported by sourced evidence? Is the Signal vs. Noise verdict internally consistent with the investment implications section? Revise.

**Step 6 — CEO Portfolio Note (15 minutes)**
The Portfolio Note is written last because it must synthesize the full briefing into a single actionable view. It should reflect what changed this week versus the prior week's note. A Portfolio Note that could have been written without reading the briefing is not doing its job.

**Step 7 — Delivery (8am)**
Format, subject line, send. The institutional client's macro analyst reads this before the market opens. The analytical value peaks at delivery time and decays over the following hours as the market processes the same information.

## V. Managing Analytical Independence Under Client Pressure

The 6am email from the macro analyst asking about the sanctions announcement is not a research request. It is a pressure event.

The pressure is structural: the client paid $18,000/year. They are asking a question. The natural human response is to answer it — immediately, verbosely, with as much conviction as possible, because that feels like demonstrating value.

**This is the trap.**

Rapid, unstructured responses to client questions erode analytical credibility faster than any publication failure. The analyst email that says "yes, this definitely changes our Russia GRI by X points" — written at 6:52am before you have had time to read the sanctions document, model the implications, or check the Tier 1 triage criteria — is the most dangerous response Prospectra can send.

**The protocol:**

When a client asks a question that requires analytical judgment:

1. Acknowledge immediately (within 1 hour): *"Thank you for flagging. We're reviewing the sanctions announcement against our current Russia coverage. I'll have a formal view to you by [specific time]."*

2. Analyze using the Tier 1 criteria. Is this a GRI score-changing event? If yes, write the alert memo. If no, confirm to the client when this will be covered in the weekly briefing.

3. Send the formal response at the committed time.

This protocol does three things: (a) it demonstrates responsiveness without sacrificing analytical integrity, (b) it trains the client to expect structured responses rather than reactive commentary, and (c) it creates a record of the analytical process that protects Prospectra if the client later disputes a call.

**What you never do:** Give a verbal or email opinion on a geopolitical event before you have applied your own analytical framework to it. That is not research. That is being a source. And it carries all the liability of being a source with none of the protection of being a publisher.

## Investment Implications

The research production system is Prospectra's operational moat. Analytical insight is valuable but replicable. The *system* that produces insight reliably, on schedule, with documented sourcing and consistent analytical frameworks is the asset that scales and compounds.

**The institutional investor parallel:** Think of Prospectra's research production system the way you would think about a fund manager's investment process. Two funds can have the same analytical framework. The fund that outperforms consistently over a decade is the one with the better process — the one that prevents the analyst from making emotional decisions at 6am when a Treasury sanctions announcement drops. The process is the edge.

**The direct market implication:** Research businesses with systematic, documented production processes command higher acquisition multiples than those dependent on a single analyst's judgment. Bloomberg acquired Merrill Lynch's research assets in 2013 not primarily for the analysts but for the coverage universe, the methodology documentation, and the client data. The business you are building in Lessons 325–341 is either an analyst-dependent cottage shop or an institutional intelligence platform. The production system is what separates them.

**Pipeline: The Editorial Production Dashboard**

The research production system generates internal operational data that should flow into Databricks as a management tool:

```
Table: editorial_calendar
- publication_id, type (briefing/alert/monthly/quarterly), scheduled_date
- actual_publish_date, delay_minutes, trigger_event_id (nullable)
- tier_classification (1/2/3), client_inquiries_received, alert_memo_sent (boolean)

Table: sourcing_log
- event_id, event_date, source_category, source_name, tier_classification
- analysis_initiated_date, analysis_completed_date, published (boolean)

Table: client_inquiry_log  
- client_id, inquiry_date, inquiry_type, acknowledgment_minutes, response_hours
- formal_response_sent (boolean), publication_triggered (boolean)
```

**The analytical value of this data:** After 12 months of operation, the `sourcing_log` and `editorial_calendar` tables will tell you which event types most reliably trigger Tier 1 alerts — the events where Prospectra's analytical value is highest. That becomes the input for your GRI coverage expansion decisions. You are not guessing which geopolitical risk categories matter to institutional investors. You are measuring it.

**The pipeline architecture:** Connect the editorial calendar to the GDELT event feed. When a geopolitical event in a covered country registers a Goldstein score shift > 2 standard deviations from its 30-day mean, automatically create a draft Tier 1 entry in the `sourcing_log` table and trigger an alert in the analyst workflow. The triage decision — Tier 1, 2, or 3 — stays human. The monitoring and flagging is automated.
