## Section 2: The Concept — Repricing vs. New Information

These are two different categories of market-moving events, and conflating them is a specific, nameable analytical error:

- **New information** changes your assessment of the probability distribution of future states of the world. A surprise CPI print, an unexpected FOMC dot, a ceasefire signing — these add a fact you didn't have before.
- **Repricing** is the market's price catching up to information it already had but had not yet fully believed, sized, or acted on. No new fact is added; the market simply moves toward consistency with a known signal.

The canonical historical case is the 2013 "Taper Tantrum." Fed Chair Bernanke's May 22, 2013 testimony mentioning that the Fed *could* slow asset purchases "in the next few meetings" was not new information in the strict sense — tapering had been discussed in FOMC minutes and Fed commentary for months, and the Fed's own reaction function implied it was coming once the labor market improved. What changed was that markets, which had been pricing extended QE as the default, suddenly recognized the path was real and repriced violently: the 10-year Treasury yield rose roughly 100 basis points over the following three months, and EM currencies and gold — the two asset classes most levered to the "Fed stays easy forever" assumption — were hit hardest. The lesson the field drew was not "the Fed surprised markets" (it largely hadn't, on the facts) but "markets had been underpricing a well-telegraphed signal, and recognition itself is repriceable."

That is structurally the same mechanism running this week. The Fed told the market on June 17 that 9 of 18 participants see a hike. The market initially treated that as low-probability noise (29% by June 18) — plausible, since dots are not commitments and Warsh is a new, unproven chair. Over the following week, continued hawkish Fedspeak and the absence of any walk-back caused the market to take the dots more seriously, and EM-FX-and-gold-sensitive positioning (this project's exact exposure set) repriced accordingly.

**Why this distinction matters for this project's discipline:** PROJECT_FOUNDATION.md's investment thesis explicitly commits to reasoning that doesn't react to price moves emotionally or without a structural framework. A repricing event is real and should absolutely be used to grade existing positions and kill-switch legs that are written around objectively observable variables (Brent level, TIPS yield, DXY level) — those triggers don't care *why* the price moved. But a repricing event should not, by itself, generate a *new* thesis or a new Investment Log entry with new reasoning, because no new causal fact has actually arrived. The discipline is: grade the existing framework against the new price; don't write a new framework to fit the new price.

## Section 3: Grading Four Open Positions Against This Week's Move

**Gold — kill switch: Brent <$75 AND 10Y TIPS real yield >3% for 60 days, simultaneously.**
Brent leg: now confirmed below $75 for two consecutive sessions (more solid than yesterday's contested read). TIPS leg: unchanged at 2.25–2.27%, no clock started. Neither leg fires the kill switch, and the conjunctive (AND) structure means a moving Brent leg alone cannot close the position regardless of how far it moves — this is the same discipline L134 applied to the energy position one day ago.

But there is a genuine framework question worth flagging rather than resolving today: gold just broke a major level ($4,000) and the dollar just hit a 15-month high, and the proximate mechanism is rate-hike-odds repricing acting through the dollar — not through the TIPS real yield specifically. The kill switch was written around one causal channel (real yields crowding out a non-yielding asset). It is currently silent on a second, related but distinct channel (nominal dollar strength driven by rate-hike *expectations*, independent of where the real yield itself sits today). **This is not a reason to act now** — the position is HIGH conviction and the kill switch has not fired — but it is a legitimate candidate for the next monthly framework audit: should the gold kill switch have a DXY leg, or is the current TIPS-only specification deliberately narrow and correct?

**Long TIPS — structural inflation hedge (no tactical stop; structural stop: defense spending <3% GDP AND Brent <$70 AND 24 consecutive months at/below 2% CPI, all three).**
The May CPI print (4.2% YoY, energy-driven) and this week's hawkish repricing both *reinforce* the structural inflation thesis — none of the three structural stop conditions are remotely close to triggering. But there's a distinction worth holding precisely: the thesis being right and the instrument being safe are not the same claim. TIPS as an instrument carries real-rate duration risk. If the Fed actually delivers a September hike rather than just talking about one, real yields could move up sharply on the announcement, which would pressure TIPS *prices* even as the inflation-protection mechanism does exactly what it's supposed to do over the structural horizon. Nothing to action today — real yields haven't moved yet — but worth flagging explicitly so a near-term price dip on instrument duration isn't mistaken for thesis failure if and when it happens.

**Tail hedge — EM bond volatility / BOJ carry-unwind risk (2–3% of portfolio, insurance, not a thesis).**
This is the position most directly validated by this week's move. The hedge exists because a widening Fed-BOJ rate gap fuels the yen carry trade that underlies large EM bond positions, and a BOJ surprise unwind is the systemic tail risk. A market now pricing ~70% odds of further Fed tightening (versus 29% a week ago) is pricing a *wider*, not narrower, rate differential — the exact condition that makes the carry trade larger and a future unwind more violent. No sizing change (the 2–3% ceiling is a discipline, not a forecast), but this week's repricing is the clearest single update yet that the risk this hedge insures against has grown, not shrunk.

**EM underweight — Turkey (TUR) and South Korea (EWY), vs. neutral EM benchmark.**
This position now has two of its own drivers pulling in opposite directions, and that tension should be named rather than smoothed over. Falling Brent (now confirmed sub-$75, heading toward the stop-loss's $80 sustained-breach test) *helps* both economies' energy import bill — an argument for closing or reducing the underweight. A 15-month-high dollar *hurts* both currencies directly — an argument for holding or adding. The position's actual stop-loss (Brent <$80 sustained 6 weeks → reduce 50%) is a pure price-level trigger that doesn't adjudicate this tension; it will fire on the price test alone if the clock starts at Friday's confirmed close, regardless of which causal story you believe. That's a feature, not a bug, of writing triggers on observable prices rather than on narratives — but it means the position could close on the Brent leg even while the dollar leg is actively reinforcing the original thesis. Flag for the next portfolio review; no action today.

## Key Concepts Covered

- Repricing vs. new information as distinct categories of market-moving events — a price move can be large and real without any new fact having arrived
- The 2013 Taper Tantrum as the historical template: a well-telegraphed Fed signal (tapering) that markets initially underpriced, then repriced violently over months, hitting EM currencies and gold hardest — the same two asset classes in motion this week
- Kill-switch mechanism specificity: a trigger written around one causal channel (TIPS real yield) can be silent on a position moving through a different, related channel (dollar strength via rate-hike odds) — worth auditing periodically, not reflexively patching
- Thesis-correctness vs. instrument-price-risk: a structural inflation thesis being validated by data does not protect an interest-rate-sensitive instrument (TIPS) from near-term price pain if real yields move on the way to that validation
- Naming genuine tension between a position's own drivers (Brent down helps Turkey/Korea's import bill, dollar up hurts their currencies) rather than resolving it by picking the conclusion you prefer
