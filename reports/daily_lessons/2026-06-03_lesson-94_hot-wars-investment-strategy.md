## Core Concept: The Five Phases of War Economics

Hot wars — direct military conflicts between states or involving major powers — move through a predictable market anatomy. Not identical each time. But structurally coherent. Understanding the phases is the difference between reacting emotionally and investing systematically.

### Phase 1 — Pre-War Tension (Weeks to Months Before Outbreak)

**What happens in markets:** Gradual risk-off. EM currencies in the conflict zone weaken. Safe havens (gold, USD, CHF, JPY) bid up. Oil implied volatility rises before spot prices move. Defense contractors begin outperforming quietly. Credit spreads in exposed sovereigns widen. Bonds in the conflict zone sell off.

**The key signal most investors miss:** The VIX is still calm. Equities are still near highs. The market is *telling you* it doesn't believe war is imminent — which is exactly when the signal-to-noise ratio is highest for a framework-driven investor.

**In the Iran case:** U.S.-Israeli tensions escalated through January-February 2026. GDELT event data would have shown a dramatic spike in hostile military action codes (CAMEO event categories 17-20) involving Iran, Israel, and the U.S. Oil options markets began pricing Hormuz tail risk. But broad equity markets remained within 3% of all-time highs through February 25. The market was not priced for war.

### Phase 2 — Outbreak Shock (Day 0 to Day 7)

**What happens in markets:** Violent, reflexive repricing. The first 72 hours are dominated by fear and position unwinding, not fundamental analysis. Energy spikes (immediately and sharply). Defense stocks rally. EM equities selling hit the most exposed geographies first. Safe havens surge. The knee-jerk reaction is often *over*-correct — oil opens at a risk premium that assumes the worst-case scenario (complete Hormuz closure) even when the probability is uncertain.

**February 28–March 5, 2026:**
- Brent crude: +15% to $83/barrel within one week
- Oil subsequently surged past $100/barrel as Hormuz closure confirmed
- Gold to $5,400/oz (+2% in days)
- Northrop Grumman: +6%, RTX: +4.7%, Lockheed Martin: +3.37% in early sessions
- Broad equities: initial -4 to -6% before partial recovery

**The investor trap in Phase 2:** Panic-selling equities and panic-buying oil at the top of the spike. By the time most retail and institutional investors reacted, the best entry points were already gone. The framework investor acted in Phase 1.

### Phase 3 — Early War Period (Week 2 to Month 3)

**What happens in markets:** Volatility remains elevated but markets begin functioning on war-adjusted fundamentals. Several dynamics define this phase:

1. **Supply disruption math gets priced:** How much oil is actually off the market? Which routes are viable alternatives? What strategic reserves will governments release? The IEA coordinates releases. Saudi Arabia signals spare capacity. Markets begin differentiating between panic price and equilibrium price under sustained disruption.

2. **Inflation transmission:** Oil at $100+ begins showing up in CPI expectations. Breakevens rise. Central banks face the stagflation dilemma — tighten (crushes growth) or accommodate (lets inflation run). Bond markets price this ambiguity as steepening yield curves.

3. **Sector rotation accelerates:** Defense, energy, materials, and "war economy" industrials outperform. Consumer discretionary, technology (rate-sensitive), and international airlines (fuel costs) underperform.

4. **EM divergence:** Energy-exporting EMs (Gulf states, Norway, Kazakhstan, Nigeria, Brazil) benefit from price windfall. Energy-importing EMs (India, Turkey, most of Southeast Asia) face a current account shock and currency pressure.

**March–April 2026:** Oil averaged near $90-100/barrel. Energy sector ETFs outperformed the S&P 500 by 15-20 percentage points YTD through Q1. Defense allocations returned 10-15% in the same period. India's rupee came under pressure. GCC sovereigns quietly banked windfall revenues.

### Phase 4 — Ceasefire / Stalemate Uncertainty (Month 3 to Resolution)

**What happens in markets:** Oscillation around ceasefire news flow. Each ceasefire rumor produces a temporary risk-on relief trade. Each failure or resumption re-prices risk back up. This phase is the most dangerous for active traders — and paradoxically the most interesting for long-horizon investors who can look through the noise.

**The structural question:** Will the supply disruption produce *permanent* market structure changes, or will pre-war equilibrium be restored?

**April 8, 2026 ceasefire announcement:** Oil prices eased. Risk-on equities bounced. But — critically — Hormuz traffic remained far below pre-war levels. The UN reported that despite the ceasefire, supply chains worldwide were still "throttled." Markets correctly began pricing a *partial* restoration scenario rather than a full snap-back.

**Brent price path post-ceasefire:**
- Expected average: $86/barrel for full year 2026
- Expected drop to $70/barrel in 2027 as supply stabilizes
- But: continued U.S. strikes in Iran as recently as May 28, 2026 revived Hormuz disruption fears — suggesting the ceasefire is fragile

**The investor lesson in Phase 4:** Don't trade ceasefire news. Position around the *structural* question. The structural question here: Does the 2026 Iran war accelerate the energy transition (more renewables, more urgency to reduce Hormuz dependency) or does it reinforce the case for energy security spending (domestic production, LNG infrastructure, nuclear)? The answer is almost certainly *both* — but the timing of which dominates determines the relative trade.

### Phase 5 — Resolution and Long Tail (Year 1+ Post-Conflict)

**What happens in markets:** Mean reversion in the acute risk premium. Energy prices eventually normalize as supply routes are restored or rerouted. But structural changes persist:

1. **Defense spending floors are raised** — permanently. Countries that were shocked by the conflict don't reduce military budgets to pre-war levels.
2. **Energy security becomes infrastructure policy** — new LNG terminals, pipelines, and domestic production receive political capital that outlasts the conflict.
3. **Geopolitical risk premium is repriced** — the "unthinkable" happened; markets assign higher probability to tail scenarios going forward. This produces a lasting risk premium in energy, gold, and defense.
4. **Regime change in the conflict zone** — the political environment changes. Investment access, contracts, and sovereign risk are repriced.

## Historical Grounding: The War-Market Pattern Repeats

The 2026 Iran conflict is the latest iteration of a pattern that has played out consistently across modern history:

| Conflict | Key Market Signal | Duration of Shock | Long-Term Structural Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gulf War 1991 | Oil spike (+100%), reversed quickly | 6 months | U.S. dominance in Gulf cemented; oil recovered |
| Iraq War 2003 | Oil rose steadily ($25 → $55 by 2005) | Years | No supply disruption; geopolitical risk repriced upward |
| Russia-Ukraine 2022 | European energy crisis; gas 10x | 18+ months | European LNG terminal boom; USD strength; defense rearmament |
| U.S.-Iran 2026 | Largest oil supply disruption in history | Active | TBD — but energy security investment surge assured |

**The pattern:** The initial shock overestimates the immediate disruption. Markets then underprice the structural change. The long-horizon investor profits from the second mispricing, not the first.

**The 1973 Oil Embargo parallel:** The most relevant historical precedent. After the 1973 embargo, oil prices never returned to pre-embargo levels on an inflation-adjusted basis. The structural shift — OPEC's demonstrated willingness to weaponize supply — was permanent. The analogous question for 2026: Has Iran's closure of Hormuz permanently raised the structural risk premium on Gulf oil in a way that changes energy infrastructure investment globally? Almost certainly yes.

## Investment Implications

### 1. Energy — Structurally Bullish, Tactically Oscillating

**Long-horizon thesis:** The 2026 Iran war is an accelerant for energy security investment globally. This means:
- **Upstream oil & gas** in non-Hormuz jurisdictions benefits (U.S. shale, North Sea, Brazil, West Africa)
- **LNG infrastructure** gets an unprecedented political mandate — Europe and Asia both want to reduce Hormuz/Gulf dependency
- **Nuclear** gets rehabilitated — France's nuclear model is being re-examined; the U.S. is approving new reactor licenses
- **Uranium** is the quietest major beneficiary: nuclear renaissance + energy security = structural demand increase

**Near-term caution:** Oil at $86/barrel is not the $100+ spike — the trade of buying the acute spike is over. The thesis now is owning energy security infrastructure, not crude oil itself.

**Specific views:**
- `+` LNG producers and infrastructure (Cheniere, NextDecade, GazTransport & Technigaz)
- `+` Uranium/nuclear (Cameco, Kazatomprom, NuScale, Centrus)
- `+` Non-Hormuz upstream (Petrobras, Equinor, Permian Basin E&Ps)
- `neutral/+` Integrated majors (Shell, BP — Hormuz exposure balanced by price benefit)
- `-` Asia/India downstream refiners (margin compression from sustained high crude)

### 2. Defense — Multi-Year Secular Trend

**Thesis:** Defense budgets globally are being reset to higher permanent baselines. This is not a cycle — it is a structural shift. The 2026 Iran war, Russia's ongoing Ukraine campaign, and China's Taiwan posture have collectively ended the post-Cold War peace dividend. NATO members are moving toward 3-4% of GDP defense spending. The Middle East is rearming. Taiwan is accelerating its defense buildup.

**This is not a short-term trade. This is a 5-10 year secular theme.**

**Specific views:**
- `+` U.S. defense primes: Northrop Grumman (NOC), Lockheed Martin (LMT), RTX Corporation (RTX)
- `+` European defense: Rheinmetall (RHM), BAE Systems (BA.L), Leonardo (LDOF)
- `+` Cybersecurity (warfare increasingly has a cyber dimension): CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks
- `+` Drone and autonomous systems manufacturers
- `+` Shipbuilding (naval buildup): Huntington Ingalls, Austal

### 3. Gold & Hard Assets — Structural Floor Raised

**Thesis:** The 2026 Iran war has permanently raised the floor on gold's geopolitical risk premium. Central banks — already accelerating gold purchases since 2022 — have further reason to hold reserves outside of dollar-denominated paper assets. Gold at $5,400+ reflects both a geopolitical premium AND ongoing de-dollarization. The question is not "is gold expensive" but "has the regime changed."

Our view: yes, the regime has changed. Gold is structurally supported in the $4,500-$6,000 range for the foreseeable future, with upside if dollar hegemony further erodes.

**Other hard assets:** Copper (critical for both the energy transition AND military systems), aluminum (aerospace/defense demand), and rare earths (all have China-dependency exposure that the Iran war makes more salient to policymakers).

### 4. Bonds & Inflation — Stagflation Risk Not Fully Priced

**Thesis:** Oil at $86/barrel average for 2026 is an inflation shock. Central banks face the 1970s dilemma: the energy-driven inflation is supply-side (can't be solved by raising rates without crushing growth). The Fed will be reluctant to tighten aggressively into a war economy. This means real rates remain compressed, yield curves stay steep or steeper, and TIPS are preferable to nominal Treasuries.

**Risk:** If the conflict escalates again (the May 28 U.S. strikes are a warning sign), oil could re-spike to $100+, forcing central banks into a harder choice. Watch the ceasefire negotiations closely.

### 5. Equities — Sector Rotation Is the Story

Not "buy or sell equities." The question is *where* in equities:
- `+` Defense, energy, materials, industrials with defense exposure
- `+` U.S. domestic economy (less exposed to global supply chains)
- `-` Consumer discretionary (fuel cost transmission)
- `-` International airlines, shipping companies (cost structure)
- `-` Rate-sensitive growth/tech (if inflation forces tighter policy)
- `neutral/+` Healthcare (defensive, not war-related)

## Signal vs. Noise — The 2026 Iran War Edition

**Signal:** The structural repricing of energy security, defense spending, and geopolitical risk premium. This is lasting. The investment implications will play out over 5-10 years.

**Noise:** Every ceasefire headline, every Hormuz "partial reopening" story, every negotiation update. These move markets intraday and cause whipsawing in oil prices. They are not investment-relevant for a long-horizon framework.

**CEO Portfolio Note:** If you are not already overweight energy infrastructure, defense, gold, and commodities — the entry point is not as good as February 27 was, but the thesis has *more* confirmation than it did then. The error is not missing the initial spike. The error is deciding that because you missed the spike, the trade is over. The structural trade has years to run.

## Databricks Angle

**Dataset:** GDELT Project — specifically the **CAMEO event codes** for hostile military action (codes 17-20: Exhibit military posture, Use conventional military force, Fight, Use unconventional mass violence). These codes, aggregated by country dyad, produce a **pre-war escalation signal** with days to weeks of lead time before conflict outbreak.

**Pipeline idea — "Pre-War Tension Index":**
1. Ingest daily GDELT event data for country dyads with known geopolitical tensions
2. Compute a rolling 30-day hostile event ratio (hostile events / total events) for each dyad
3. Set alert thresholds based on historical conflict onset patterns (pre-Gulf War, pre-Ukraine, pre-Iran war)
4. Cross-reference with oil options implied volatility (Yahoo Finance) and gold price momentum
5. Output: a composite "conflict onset probability" score — if threshold crossed, trigger portfolio alert

**The 2026 Iran case as backtesting ground:** The GDELT signal almost certainly lit up in January-February 2026 before the February 28 outbreak. This is your validation dataset. Build the pipeline, backtest against the Iran war, and you have a live signal for the next conflict.
