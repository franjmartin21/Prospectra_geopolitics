## I. What Is a Geopolitical Regime?

A **geopolitical regime** is not a government. It is the *operating system* of international relations — the set of:

1. **Power distributions** — who holds military, economic, and institutional dominance
2. **Rules** — what treaties, norms, and institutions govern state behavior
3. **Default assumptions** — what market participants take for granted (dollar is risk-free, US security guarantee is credible, WTO rules apply, supply chains are resilient)

Geopolitical regimes persist for **decades**. Individual events — wars, elections, summits, sanctions — occur *within* regimes. Most of the time, even dramatic events don't change the regime. They are processed by the existing system and absorbed.

**Regime changes are rare. Events are common. The most expensive mistake in geopolitical investing is mistaking a big event for a regime change — or missing a regime change because it happened gradually.**

### The Regime vs. Event Matrix

| | High Visibility | Low Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Event (regime-consistent)** | Iran war, COVID, Brexit vote | Tariff adjustment, summit communiqué |
| **Regime change (rare)** | Fall of Berlin Wall, Nixon shock | Dollar share of reserves falling quietly; WTO appellate body paralysis |

The pattern: **regime changes often begin invisibly and become obvious only in retrospect**. Events that look like regime changes (9/11, COVID) often turn out to be regime-consistent shocks absorbed by existing institutions. The actual regime changes (Bretton Woods collapse, Cold War end) were often announced by small, technical decisions that the press barely covered.

## II. Historical Grounding — Three Clean Regime Transitions

### Case 1: The Nixon Shock (August 15, 1971) — End of Bretton Woods

**The regime being ended:** Dollar-gold convertibility. Every major currency pegged to the dollar; the dollar pegged to gold at $35/oz. The US as underwriter of global monetary stability.

**The event:** Nixon, in a Sunday TV address, announced the US was suspending dollar-gold convertibility. No negotiation. No multilateral agreement. A unilateral decision that ended a 27-year monetary regime in 15 minutes.

**What it looked like in real time:**
- Markets panicked, then rallied ("Nixon shock" was followed by a short-term equity rally as dollar devaluation boosted US exporters)
- Gold was artificially fixed at $35/oz through 1974 — even as the regime that anchored that price was gone
- The full implications didn't play out for years: the 1973 oil shock, the 1970s stagflation, the gold surge to $850/oz by 1980

**Investor lesson:** The regime changed in August 1971. But the full market repricing took 9 years. Investors who recognized the regime change in 1971-1972 and positioned in hard assets (gold, commodities, real estate) captured the full return. Investors who interpreted the 1971 equity rally as confirmation that "everything was fine" suffered through the worst decade for US equity real returns since the 1930s.

**Regime change signals that were visible in 1971:**
- US running persistent current account deficits for the first time since WWII (1971 was the first year the US ran a trade deficit in the 20th century)
- French and German central banks demanding gold conversion of dollar reserves (the "run on the bank" that forced Nixon's hand)
- Dollar share of global trade financing had already begun declining from 1968 peak

### Case 2: The Cold War's End (1989–1991) — The Unipolar Moment

**The regime being ended:** Bipolar world order. Two nuclear superpowers, two alliance blocs, two economic systems competing for global dominance. Every regional conflict proxied through the superpower lens.

**The event:** The Berlin Wall fell November 9, 1989. The USSR formally dissolved December 25, 1991.

**What it looked like in real time:**
- The immediate investment implication was obvious: long European equities, especially German (DAX rallied 30% in 1989), long Eastern European restructuring plays
- Less obvious: the "peace dividend" — Western defense spending fell sharply throughout the 1990s
- Even less obvious: the unipolar moment meant US assets would command a structural premium that had never existed before. The 1990s bull market was partly a "unipolar dividend" — the US as the sole superpower, free to focus on economic growth rather than military competition

**Regime change signals that were visible before 1989:**
- Soviet economic growth stagnation from the mid-1970s onward (CIA estimates were too optimistic)
- Afghanistan as the "Soviet Vietnam" (1979–1989)
- Poland's Solidarity movement surviving martial law (1981–1983)
- Gorbachev's rhetoric signals: "glasnost" and "perestroika" were observable from 1985

The Cold War's end was visible years before 1989 to analysts tracking structural indicators. The event (Wall falling) was the signal flare — not the regime change itself.

### Case 3: The Multipolar Emergence (2016–2022) — The Regime We're Living In

**The regime being ended:** Unipolar US dominance, liberal international order, rules-based trade, dollar primacy as unquestioned.

**The events:** Brexit (June 2016), Trump 1.0 election (November 2016), US withdrawal from TPP (January 2017), trade war (2018), COVID supply chain crisis (2020), Russia sanctions/SWIFT exclusion (2022), US-Iran war (2026).

**The pattern:** No single event ended the unipolar moment. It was a **cascading erosion** — each event slightly more corrosive than the last, accumulating into a structural shift.

**What looks like noise but was signal:**
- US withdrawal from multilateral institutions (Paris, TPP, UNESCO, WHO — temporarily)
- WTO appellate body blocked from functioning (2019, still paralyzed)
- China's GDP (PPP) crossing US in 2016 — the structural parity event
- Russian gold accumulation (2014 onward) — a sovereign hedge against SWIFT exclusion risk, visible in BIS data
- Central bank gold buying (2018 onward) — reserve diversification before the dollar was weaponized against Russia

**What looked like signal but was noise:**
- Every Trump tweet
- Every summit communiqué
- Every tariff rollback
- The COVID equity crash (V-shaped recovery; regime intact)

The regime was changing throughout — but the individual events were often temporary. The structural indicators told the real story.

## III. The Six-Signal Regime Detection Framework

Based on the historical cases, six indicators distinguish regime change from tactical noise:

### Signal 1: Reserve Currency Share (Duration: Slow — Quarterly Data)
The dollar's share of global reserves has been declining since 2001. It is now at approximately 47%, down from 71% in 1999. This is a **structural regime indicator** — it moves slowly but directionally. A summit cannot reverse it. Only a credibility restoration event (new Bretton Woods-style agreement) could.

**Current reading:** Regime change in progress. Dollar hegemony eroding structurally. One summit cannot reverse 25 years of trend.

### Signal 2: Institutional Functionality (Duration: Slow — Annual)
The WTO appellate body has been non-functional since 2019. The IMF's quota reform — giving emerging economies more voting power — has been blocked by the US for years. UN Security Council veto gridlock. These are **institutional erosion signals** that indicate the rules-based order is losing its enforcement capacity.

**Current reading:** Institutional dysfunction persisting. Not reversed by any recent events.

### Signal 3: Defense Spending Trajectory (Duration: Medium — Annual)
NATO members' defense spending is rising sharply. Japan broke its 1% GDP cap. Taiwan is on a sustained buildup. This represents **sovereign hedging behavior** — states hedging against security guarantees becoming unreliable. You cannot hedge and trust simultaneously. The hedging tells you what states actually believe about regime stability.

**Current reading:** Global rearmament secular trend intact. Not reversed by Trump-Xi summit.

### Signal 4: Conflict Event Frequency (Duration: Fast — Daily/Weekly via GDELT)
GDELT tracks geopolitical event tone globally. In stable regimes, conflict event frequency and tone are mean-reverting. In transitional regimes, the baseline itself shifts upward — more frequent high-severity events, slower reversion to calm after each shock.

**Current reading:** Iran war, Ukraine, Taiwan strait tensions, Sahel coups — baseline conflict frequency is elevated versus 2010–2020 norms. Post-ceasefire calm is consistent with within-regime reversion; it does not indicate structural change.

### Signal 5: Capital Control and Parallel Infrastructure Buildout (Duration: Medium)
When states build alternatives to the dollar system (mBridge, CIPS, bilateral swap lines, gold reserves, RMB trade invoicing), they are making structural commitments that are costly to reverse. These are not tactical decisions; they require institutional investment, legal frameworks, and counterparty agreements.

**Current reading:** CIPS transaction volume growing 20%+/year. China-Gulf RMB oil settlements accelerating post-Iran war. mBridge expanding membership. This buildout is not stopped by a tariff truce.

### Signal 6: Elite Consensus Shifts (Duration: Medium — Detected through speeches, doctrine documents, and investment allocation data)
When major institutions — sovereign wealth funds, central banks, pension funds — start systematically diversifying away from US assets, it signals elite-level regime skepticism. This is different from retail sentiment; institutions are making multi-decade commitments.

**Current reading:** Central bank gold buying at multi-decade highs. Sovereign wealth funds in Gulf states diversifying into Asian assets. European pension funds building "geopolitical risk" allocations explicitly for the first time.

## IV. Applying the Framework to the Current Moment

### The Trump-Xi Summit (May 14, 2026) — Signal or Noise?

Run the six signals:

| Signal | Reading | Summit Changes It? |
|---|---|---|
| Reserve currency share | Dollar at 47%, declining trend | No |
| Institutional functionality | WTO broken, IMF constrained | No — requires years of reform |
| Defense spending | Global rearmament in progress | No — commitments are multi-year |
| Conflict event frequency | Elevated baseline | Marginally — short-term calm |
| Parallel infrastructure buildout | CIPS/RMB accelerating | No — structural commitment |
| Elite consensus | Diversifying away from US | No — portfolio allocations sticky |

**Verdict: The Trump-Xi summit is a within-regime tactical adjustment. It reduces short-term conflict event frequency and provides a temporary reprieve from tariff escalation. It does not reverse any of the six structural regime indicators.**

The framework produces a clear answer: **trade the rally, but don't reprice the structural positions.** The interregnum continues.

### The Iran Ceasefire — Regime Reversion or Durable Stability?

Oil at $100, down from $126. Hormuz traffic at 70% of pre-war levels, recovering. Ceasefire holding since April 8.

The six-signal check:
- Iran's nuclear program is destroyed — this is a durable change in the Middle East balance of power
- But the structural forces that created the conflict (US-Iran hostility, Chinese-Iranian economic integration, Gulf state hedging between blocs) are unchanged
- RMB oil settlement acceleration from the war period is not reversing

**Verdict:** The ceasefire is a real event with lasting strategic consequences (Iran's nuclear deterrent is gone for 5–10 years). But the broader regime dynamics are unchanged. The investment implication: oil below $100 is tactical reversion, not structural — the risk premium that drove oil to $126 has not been structurally eliminated, just temporarily reduced.

## V. Investment Implications — The Regime Detection Trade

The regime detection framework changes how you size positions and when you add or reduce:

### Rule 1: Use Event-Driven Rallies to Build Structural Positions
When a summit, ceasefire, or "positive development" temporarily reduces the geopolitical risk premium and causes risk assets to rally and structural hedges (gold, energy, defense) to pull back — **that is the entry window for long-horizon structural positions**, not the exit.

**Current application:** Post-Iran ceasefire, post-Trump-Xi summit pullback in gold and defense equities. If the six-signal framework says the regime is still in transition, these pullbacks are gifts. The market is pricing the event, not the regime.

### Rule 2: Distinguish Regime Signals from Event Noise in Your Databricks Pipeline
Every geopolitical signal needs to be classified: is it event-level (revertible) or regime-level (structural)? The Multipolarity Stress Index (MSI) introduced in Lesson 63 should weight signals by their regime vs. event classification — regime-level signals get higher weight and longer decay periods.

### Rule 3: Know What Would Falsify Your Regime View
A regime change from "multipolar interregnum" back to "stable unipolar order" would require:
- Dollar share of reserves stabilizing above 55% for 3+ consecutive years
- WTO appellate body restored and functioning
- Global defense spending as % of GDP declining back to 2015 levels
- China reversing RMB internationalization infrastructure
- US security guarantees credibly restored (verified through sovereign hedging reversal)

None of these conditions are being met. The Trump-Xi summit is not falsification; it is noise. **Know your falsification conditions in advance. Don't let market rallies move the goalposts.**

### Rule 4: Regime Transitions Have Duration — Calibrate Your Time Horizon
The 1919–1944 interregnum lasted 25 years. The post-Bretton-Woods commodity supercycle lasted from 1971 to ~1982 (11 years). The Cold War transition played out over 10 years (1989–2000).

**We are approximately 10 years into the current multipolar transition (2016–2026).** Historical precedent suggests 10–20 more years of transitional dynamics. This is not a trade. This is a structural portfolio orientation for the rest of the decade.

Characteristics:
- US remains the sole global military hegemon (Iran war proved this)
- US no longer the unquestioned economic hegemon (dollar share falling, institutions eroding)
- China capable of disrupting the US system but not replacing it
- Middle powers extracting rents from both sides (Gulf states, India, Vietnam, Mexico)

**Probability of regime reversal (return to stable unipolarity) in 5 years:** ~10%
- Would require: US domestic political consensus for global leadership, credible military retrenchment by China, dollar system reform, institutional repair
- None of these are likely on a 5-year horizon

**Probability of regime stabilization (stable multipolarity, new institutions) in 10 years:** ~35%
- Would require: G20 gaining enforcement capacity, US-China managed competition framework, WTO reform, IMF expansion
- Possible but requires political will that doesn't exist today

**Probability of continued interregnum (10 more years of contested, unstable multipolarity):** ~55%
- No dramatic collapse, but no resolution either. Periodic crises, structural volatility, competing blocs
- This is the base case. Position accordingly.

## VII. Databricks Angle

**Pipeline: Geopolitical Regime Classifier**

Build a machine learning classifier (or a simpler rules-based version) that labels the current geopolitical regime on a rolling 3-month basis.

**Inputs (features):**
- MSI score (Lesson 63) — composite index
- Dollar reserve share (BIS quarterly, interpolated monthly)
- GDELT global conflict tone 90-day moving average and standard deviation
- Defense spending YoY change (SIPRI, updated annually)
- Trade fragmentation index (bilateral trade flow deviation from trend — IMF DOTS)
- Central bank gold purchases (WGC quarterly data)

**Labels (target variable):**
- 0: Stable unipolar (2000–2008, calibrated historically)
- 1: Crisis within regime (2008–2009 GFC)
- 2: Transitional interregnum (2016–present)
- 3: Stable multipolar (no current historical example — future state)

**Use case:** The regime label becomes the master switch for the Investment Signal Generator. In regime 0 (stable unipolar), weight traditional risk factors heavily. In regime 2 (interregnum), weight geopolitical structural signals heavily. In regime 1 (within-regime crisis), apply crisis playbook (short duration, long vol, defensive equities).

**Dataset priorities:**
- BIS international reserve composition (most important single dataset)
- GDELT GKG for conflict tone at scale
- WGC (World Gold Council) central bank demand data
- IMF DOTS for trade fragmentation

**Engineering note:** The regime classifier's output should be stable (updating monthly or quarterly) — not reactive to individual events. If the classifier updates every time a summit happens, it's measuring noise, not structure. Use long-decay rolling averages as inputs to enforce structural sensitivity.
