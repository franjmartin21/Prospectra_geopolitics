**The 3.5-hour gap between PCE and Warsh's speech is the critical window:**

Between 8:30 AM Eastern (PCE release) and approximately 12:00 PM Eastern (Warsh's Mountain Time morning session), the market will price the PCE data. Gold, dollar, and rate futures will move. **This repricing is not the signal — it is the noise.** The reflexive market move on PCE will partially overshoot. The signal is Warsh's speech text, which was written before the PCE number was released but in full knowledge of the prior inflation trajectory.

**What to do in the 3.5-hour gap:**
1. Read the PCE release immediately. Calculate core PCE YoY and MoM.
2. Identify which scenario the PCE print supports (see table above).
3. **Do not reposition before the speech.** The market's initial reaction to PCE will be the wrong direction approximately 40% of the time in high-uncertainty environments. Wait for the speech.
4. Pre-set the position-level responses for each Warsh scenario (should already be done from Lesson 262's pre-commitment framework).
5. At speech release: read the text for the three key signals. Execute pre-committed response.

**The three key sentences to find in Warsh's text:**
1. **On current policy stance:** Any conditional framing of "the appropriate stance of policy" paired with an inflation level or labor market condition.
2. **On the September FOMC decision:** Any explicit reference to the timing of the next policy change, even oblique ("in coming meetings" vs. "in the near term" vs. "over time").
3. **On financial innovation:** Any signal about CBDCs, stablecoin regulation, or digital dollar — the symposium theme. A substantive announcement here would be Scenario D and would move fintech/crypto separately from the rate signal.

## Historical Grounding: What Investors Did Wrong in the Week Before Major Pivots

Three case studies — the same mistake, different names:

### 1. August 2022 (Pre-"Pain" Speech)
In the week before Powel's August 26, 2022 speech, consensus firmly expected a 50bp-step-down signal. The quiet week produced no counter-signals. Investors added duration and reduced hedges based on the consensus — **in the week when they should have been trimming risk, not adding it.** The 8-minute speech destroyed $3 trillion in market cap.

**The lesson:** A quiet week before a major speech is NOT permission to relax the hedges. It is the moment to confirm that the pre-commitment rules are in place and that the position is sized correctly for ALL scenarios, not just the consensus one.

### 2. December 2021 (Pre-Tapering Pivot Week)
In the week before the December 2021 FOMC meeting, inflation data had been consistently hot for months. The Fed's language had been shifting toward "expedited taper." Despite all signals pointing in one direction, a segment of the market continued to discount the pivot speed — because the prior quarter's "transitory" framing had become anchored in expectations.

**The lesson:** Assumption persistence — the same failure mode identified in Lesson 262's intelligence correction — operates in markets as much as in analysis. The market's prior assumption about Fed behavior resisted updating until the actual announcement forced repricing. Don't be the market.

### 3. September 2023 (Non-Event Week That Felt Like a Signal)
In the week before the September 2023 FOMC (which held rates steady after 11 consecutive hikes), oil prices rose sharply (Saudi-Russia supply cuts), creating a brief period where the market worried the Fed might hike again. Tuesday data (Consumer Confidence) showed weakness. Investors repositioned mid-week based on the oil-CPI-Fed narrative.

**The final outcome:** The Fed held, as expected. The mid-week repositioning was noise-chasing. Investors who held their pre-meeting stance outperformed.

**The lesson (directly applicable this week):** Oil price moves, Consumer Confidence prints, and jobless claims numbers are all inputs into the scenario probability revision framework — not standalone reasons to repositioning. Use them to update the probabilities. Only repposition if the probability shift crosses a pre-defined threshold. Write the threshold today.
