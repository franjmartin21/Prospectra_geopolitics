## Opening Question

*The GRI delta scan runs on Tuesday morning. Turkey's score has risen 18 points over 14 days — the third-highest move in the current scan across 40 countries. You've identified the trigger. You have a quantitative signal.*

**"What exactly do you do in the next 90 minutes — and how do you know when the analysis is complete enough to commit to a published thesis?"**

This question matters more than it appears. Most research products fail at the boundary between data and prose. The data says "something is moving in Turkey." The prose says "here is our directional view and why." Bridging that gap badly — by reverse-engineering a narrative from a pre-formed view, by stopping at the data without interrogating it, or by writing confidently about causation you've only established at the level of correlation — is the failure mode that produces a credible-looking track record with no genuine analytical value.

This lesson defines the analytical process for the 90-minute work block between "GRI delta identified" and "thesis written." It is the protocol that makes the signal selection rigorous rather than opportunistic.

---

## I. The Structure of the Problem

When a quantitative trigger fires — a country's GRI rises sharply — there are three possible explanations:

**Explanation 1: Noise.** The GRI moved because GDELT ingested a spike of news coverage that reflects media attention, not actual geopolitical deterioration. A regional conflict received a surge of international press. The GRI captured the coverage, not the reality.

**Explanation 2: Cyclical Event.** A genuine geopolitical event occurred — an election, a diplomatic row, a localized economic stress — but the event is bounded, predictable, and already priced. The market knows about it. There is no edge.

**Explanation 3: Structural Shift.** The GRI is capturing an early signal of a structural change in the country's geopolitical risk profile — a change that, once it becomes clear to market participants, will produce durable asset price movements that are not yet fully reflected in current pricing.

Only Explanation 3 produces a publishable signal. The 90-minute analytical process exists to distinguish between the three.

---

## II. The Five-Layer Analysis Stack

Work through the following five layers in order. Do not skip to Layer 4 (market pricing) before completing Layers 1–3. The sequence matters: it prevents reverse-engineering.

### Layer 1 — Source Interrogation (15 minutes)

**Question:** What events actually drove the GRI delta?

Pull the underlying GDELT event data for the country over the 14-day window. Identify the top 5–10 event types by count and tone contribution. Ask:

- Is the coverage concentrated in one news cycle, or distributed across multiple independent events?
- Are the source outlets domestic, regional, or international? A surge driven by international outlets on a short-duration story is more likely noise than a surge driven by regional outlets across two weeks.
- What is the event type distribution? GDELT's Conflict / Mediation / Accusation / Protest taxonomy tells you whether the signal is violent-event-driven or diplomatic-tension-driven.

**Output from Layer 1:** A judgment on whether the GRI delta reflects one media spike or a distributed underlying shift. If it's a single spike with no corroboration across event types, note "potential noise" and weight the signal accordingly.

---

### Layer 2 — Political Narrative Construction (20 minutes)

**Question:** Is there a coherent political story that explains the delta?

This is the layer where you read — but read with discipline. You are looking for a narrative that:

1. Existed before the GRI move (not a post-hoc rationalization)
2. Is structural (not a single event)
3. Has actors with defined interests, not just abstract forces

The discipline: write the narrative in 3 sentences before looking at market prices. Three sentences: (a) what changed in the country's political/security/economic landscape, (b) what actors drove the change and why, (c) what the directional trajectory of the change is. If you cannot write three coherent sentences — if the narrative is vague, circular, or requires speculative leaps — the signal is likely not ready.

**Output from Layer 2:** A 3-sentence political narrative. If you cannot complete it in 20 minutes, the country is not this week's signal.

---

### Layer 3 — Falsifiability Check (10 minutes)

**Question:** What would make this thesis wrong?

Before writing the investment thesis, state — in writing — the conditions that would falsify it. This is not optional. A thesis without falsifiability conditions is an opinion, not an analysis. Specifically:

- What is the counter-scenario? (e.g., "If the opposition coalition collapses before the election, the political deadlock thesis fails.")
- What early data would indicate the thesis is breaking down? (e.g., "If TRY/USD stabilizes above X over the following 10 trading days, the market is not pricing the political risk we're identifying.")
- What is the timeline for observing confirming or disconfirming evidence?

**Output from Layer 3:** 2–3 falsification conditions committed in writing before the thesis is drafted.

---

### Layer 4 — Market Pricing Analysis (20 minutes)

**Question:** Is the risk already priced?

Only now do you look at asset prices. The sequence matters. If you look at prices first, you will unconsciously anchor your thesis to what markets have already done. Look at prices last so the analytical judgment remains independent of recent market moves.

Examine:

- **Currency:** FX spot vs. 30-day and 90-day implied vol. Has the options market already bid up protection? If front-month implied vol has spiked, the event is pricing. If the vol curve is flat, the market is not hedging.
- **Sovereign spreads:** CDS or EMBI+ spread for the country. Has credit risk been repriced? Spread widening of >50bp in the past 14 days suggests the credit market is aware.
- **Equity (if applicable):** If there is a country ETF or liquid listed equity market, has there been unusual volume or a trend break?

**The key judgment:** Is current market pricing consistent with the geopolitical risk you've identified, or does a gap exist? A signal exists only when the analytical assessment of risk exceeds what market pricing implies.

**Output from Layer 4:** A one-sentence market pricing verdict. "Market is underpricing this risk because [X]" or "Market has already repriced; no edge here."

---

### Layer 5 — Thesis Formulation (25 minutes)

**Question:** What is the investment thesis in precise language?

If Layers 1–4 support a signal, write the thesis using this mandatory structure:

> **Country:** [X]
> **Risk identified:** [Specific political / geopolitical event or process]
> **Asset class:** [FX / Sovereign debt / Commodity / Regional equity index]
> **Directional view:** [Long / Short / Overweight / Underweight]
> **Specific instrument:** [Most liquid, most direct expression]
> **Rationale:** [2–3 sentences connecting the geopolitical risk to the asset price mechanism]
> **Falsification conditions:** [The conditions identified in Layer 3]
> **Timeframe:** [30 / 60 / 90 days]
> **Confidence level:** [High / Medium / Low, with explicit reasoning]

The confidence level is not a hedge. It is an analytical output. "High confidence" means Layers 1–3 all pointed in the same direction, the political narrative is coherent, and there is a clear price mechanism. "Low confidence" means one or more layers produced ambiguous evidence. Low confidence signals are still publishable — but the publication must disclose the ambiguity honestly. A research product that only publishes high-confidence signals is self-selecting into the easy calls and suppressing the difficult ones. Institutional reviewers know this.

## III. The Reverse-Engineering Trap

The failure mode worth naming explicitly: starting with the market view and working backward.

You notice TRY/USD has sold off 4% in two weeks. You look at the GRI scan. Turkey has moved. You decide Turkey is this week's signal. You write a political narrative that explains the FX move.

This is not analysis. It is rationalization. The GRI score is being used as a legitimizing device for a thesis that was generated from market data, not from geopolitical analysis. The problem is not that the thesis is wrong — it may well be directionally correct — but that the process cannot be systematized. If the selection process is "find a market move and find a GRI delta to justify it," the methodology is broken. At Signal #26, the track record will show win rates, but the analytical process will not be replicable, and no institutional client can rely on it.

**The discipline:** Run the five layers in order. Do not look at Layer 4 prices until Layer 3 is complete. Commit the Layer 3 falsification conditions before examining price data. The sequence is what makes the process systematic, not just the output.

## IV. Investment Implications

The analytical process described here is the foundation of a **systematic, auditable research product** — the exact product type that commands institutional pricing ($2,000–$10,000/month vs. $50–$100/month for typical Substack subscriptions).

What distinguishes institutional-grade research from general-audience commentary at the analytical level:

| **General Audience Research** | **Institutional-Grade Research** |
|---|---|
| Narrative-first, data-supporting | Data-first, narrative-constraining |
| Event-driven selection | Process-driven selection |
| Implicit falsifiability | Explicit falsifiability conditions |
| Prose outcome description | Scored, auditable outcome record |
| Confidence asserted | Confidence level disclosed and tracked |

The five-layer protocol — especially the falsifiability requirement and the sequencing of market pricing last — is what separates the two. The Prospectra publication commitment is not to write about geopolitical events. It is to run a disciplined process that produces directional investment theses with explicit conditions for being wrong.

## V. Case Study — Turkey, August 2018

To anchor the protocol with a real historical example:

**Layer 1:** GDELT event spike in July–August 2018, concentrated in diplomatic tension events (US–Turkey pastor dispute) and economic commentary. Distributed across 6 weeks, not a single media spike. Signal: genuine underlying shift.

**Layer 2 narrative (3 sentences):** The Erdogan government's confrontation with the Trump administration over Pastor Andrew Brunson, combined with an existing current account deficit financed by foreign borrowing, created a dual vulnerability — diplomatic and financial. The US threat of additional sanctions against a NATO member with significant USD-denominated private sector debt triggered capital flight dynamics that were structurally self-reinforcing. The trajectory was deteriorating: Erdogan had no incentive to capitulate ahead of elections, and the US had domestic political reasons to maintain pressure.

**Layer 3 (falsification):** If the Brunson case was resolved before US midterms, diplomatic pressure would lift and TRY would recover. If the CBRT raised rates aggressively (>500bp), market confidence in policy orthodoxy would stabilize the currency. Both conditions were observable in real time.

**Layer 4:** TRY/USD had already moved from 4.5 to 5.5 (22%) but 3-month implied vol was spiking to 30%+ — market was pricing uncertainty, not certainty of resolution. Sovereign CDS at 350bp; not yet at Argentina/Venezuela stress levels. Gap between geopolitical deterioration trajectory and market's implied terminal scenario.

**Layer 5 thesis:** Short TRY/USD (via NDF), 90-day horizon, high confidence. Currency would continue weakening until one of three falsification conditions resolved.

**Outcome:** TRY reached 7.1 before the Brunson release in October 2018 — a further 30% move from the Layer 4 assessment point. The analytical process worked because the falsification conditions were named and neither resolved in the short run.
