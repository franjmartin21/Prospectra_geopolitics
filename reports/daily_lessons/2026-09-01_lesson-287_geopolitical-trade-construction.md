## Opening Question

*You have processed a live geopolitical event through the 48-hour framework (Lesson 285). You have sourced, filtered, and layered your intelligence through the five-tier stack (Lesson 286). Your analysis says: this event creates a meaningful supply disruption risk in copper, a currency pressure on the Indonesian rupiah, and a tailwind for European defense contractors.*

**Now what?**

Most analysts stop here. They write a note. They update their view. They circulate an opinion.

But analysis is not investing. The gap between "I have a view" and "I have a position" is where most edge is lost — either through inaction, over-trading, wrong instrument selection, or failure to define what would make the thesis wrong before entering it.

This lesson is the bridge. It answers: *how do you convert geopolitical intelligence into a position with a defined thesis, sized correctly, in the right instrument, with an explicit exit condition?*

## I. The Four Failure Modes of Geopolitical Investing

Before the framework, understand the four ways analysts fail at the translation step:

### Failure Mode 1: The Pundit Trap
You have a brilliant geopolitical view. The trade never gets put on. Analysis becomes a substitute for action. Result: zero P&L, infinite opinions.

*Cause:* No decision trigger. "I'll wait for more confirmation" is not a process — it's anxiety dressed as discipline.

### Failure Mode 2: The Reactor
The event hits. You buy immediately. The market has already moved 3%. You're buying the noise, not the signal. You're the liquidity the smart money is selling into.

*Cause:* No pre-built framework distinguishing "event that requires immediate action" from "event that requires structured monitoring."

### Failure Mode 3: The Thesis Drift
You entered with a thesis ("copper tightens on Chile political risk"). The thesis plays out — copper up 15%. Then it reverses. But you've started reading copper through a new lens ("Fed pivot, China stimulus"). You stay in the trade. You lose the gain.

*Cause:* No original exit thesis. The position outlived its investment rationale.

### Failure Mode 4: The Wrong Instrument
You're right on the macro ("EM currencies weakest since 2015"). You express it through a single EM equity. The currency moves but the company hedges its exposure. You're right on the idea, wrong on the expression. Net P&L: zero.

*Cause:* No instrument selection discipline.

The framework below addresses all four.

## II. The Geopolitical Trade Construction Manual

### Step 1: Thesis Articulation — One Paragraph, One Sentence

Before any position is sized or any instrument selected, the thesis must be written in two forms:

**Long form (one paragraph):** Describe the geopolitical mechanism, the market dislocation it creates, and the asset class most directly exposed. This is for internal accountability.

**Short form (one sentence):** The falsifiable claim. This becomes your exit trigger.

**Example:**
- *Long:* "Chile's constitutional referendum has shifted political risk dramatically toward resource nationalism. Copper production could face supply disruption of 5–10% within 12–18 months as a new mining royalty regime is implemented. Copper is currently pricing a 3% supply risk premium, not 8–12%. This is a mispricing."
- *Short: "Copper moves to reflect a 8%+ supply disruption premium within 12 months, or this thesis is wrong."*

The short form is your exit thesis. If 12 months pass and copper has not incorporated that premium, the thesis failed — even if copper went up for other reasons.

### Step 2: The Three-Filter Check

Before proceeding to position sizing, the thesis must pass three filters:

**Filter 1 — Is the market already pricing it?**
Check the relevant asset against its 6-month price action. If it has already moved significantly in the direction of your thesis, you are *confirming*, not *discovering*. Confirmation has value but requires a different position — smaller, shorter horizon, with tighter stops.

**Filter 2 — Is there a counterfactual?**
Name the thing that would make you wrong, in the next sentence. Not "if the event doesn't happen" — that's too easy. What specific other force could overwhelm your thesis? (Example: Chile copper thesis is wrong if China demand collapses by 15%+ independently of supply.) If you cannot name a credible counterfactual, your thesis is probably too vague.

**Filter 3 — Is there a clean expression?**
Can you name a liquid instrument where the thesis is directly exposed? If you need three degrees of separation to get to the trade, the instrument is probably wrong. (Example: "I think Chilean political risk rises, so I'll buy CAT stock because they sell mining equipment to Chile" is three degrees of separation. Direct: short CLP, long copper via futures or ETF.)

### Step 3: Position Sizing — The Conviction Matrix

Geopolitical positions are sized on two axes: **thesis confidence** and **market crowding**.

```
                    LOW CROWDING    HIGH CROWDING
                   (few others      (consensus
                    have this view)  already on)
-------------------------------------------------
HIGH CONFIDENCE  |   CORE (3-5%)  |  TRIM (1-2%)  |
                 |                |               |
LOW CONFIDENCE   |  PROBE (0.5-1%)|   SKIP        |
-------------------------------------------------
```

*Portfolio % of total investable capital:*

- **CORE (3–5%):** High confidence, uncrowded. Rare. These are the thesis-defining positions.
- **TRIM (1–2%):** Right view, but if you can see it, so can everyone. Enter smaller; size up only when crowding reduces on a dip.
- **PROBE (0.5–1%):** Early, uncertain, but the optionality is worth the cost. This is an intelligence position — you're in the trade to force yourself to track it carefully.
- **SKIP:** If you're late *and* uncertain, there is no edge. Do not trade.

**Rule:** No single geopolitical position should exceed 5% of the portfolio unless it also appears in a macro overlay (i.e., a structural thesis that is multi-year, not event-driven). Even then, 8% is a hard ceiling.

### Step 4: Instrument Selection — The Proximity Principle

Select the instrument that sits closest to the geopolitical mechanism, with acceptable liquidity and manageable correlation to your other positions.

| Geopolitical Mechanism | Instrument Proximity Ranking |
|---|---|
| **Commodity supply disruption** | 1. Commodity futures 2. Commodity ETF 3. Producers (equities) |
| **Currency pressure (EM)** | 1. FX spot/forward 2. CDS (if available) 3. EM bond ETF |
| **Defense/conflict escalation** | 1. Defense sector ETF 2. Individual primes 3. Commodities consumed (steel, aluminum) |
| **Sanctions on energy exports** | 1. Energy futures 2. Freight/tanker equities 3. Replacing-supply-source equities |
| **Central bank policy divergence** | 1. Rate futures 2. FX carry pairs 3. Duration ETFs |
| **Supply chain regionalization** | 1. Industrial/capital goods sector 2. Logistics equities 3. EM manufacturing country ETF |

**Instrument selection rule:** If the cleanest expression is illiquid or unavailable in your account, use the *second closest* instrument — not a proxy that requires a narrative chain longer than two steps.

### Step 5: Staging — Enter in Three Tranches

Geopolitical theses take time to mature. Never enter a geopolitical position in a single tranche (the exception: if the event is immediately reflected in prices, you've missed tranche 1 — enter only 2 and 3).

- **Tranche 1 (50% of position):** Initial entry on thesis identification. Price is acceptable relative to your view.
- **Tranche 2 (30%):** Entered after first confirmation signal (event evolves as expected, additional data confirms mechanism, market has not overreacted).
- **Tranche 3 (20%):** Entered on re-test or secondary catalyst. Only if thesis still holds.

This tranche structure serves two purposes: it forces you to monitor the thesis actively (you have skin in the game from tranche 1) and it gives you a natural cost-averaging mechanism without chasing a moving price.

### Step 6: Exit Thesis — Write It Before You Enter

The exit thesis must be defined at entry. There are three legitimate exits:

1. **Thesis-correct exit:** The mispricing you identified has been incorporated. The market has moved to your target. Sell in full; do not let the trade drift into a new rationale.

2. **Thesis-wrong exit:** The falsifiable condition you named in Step 1 has been triggered. Close immediately. Do not extend the thesis. ("Copper failed to reflect the supply premium after 12 months despite no improvement in Chilean political risk — thesis failed.")

3. **Time-based exit:** The thesis required a specific timeframe. If the time passes without thesis maturation, reduce by 50% regardless. Re-evaluate. Do not hold indefinitely "because the fundamentals haven't changed." Markets don't have to agree with you.

**Anti-pattern:** "I'll hold until it works." This is not an exit thesis. This is hope.

## III. Case Application — Chile Copper Thesis (Live Build)

Let's apply the full framework:

**Thesis Statement (Long):**
Chile's second Boric-era government has accelerated the mining royalty reform, increasing effective tax rates on copper production by approximately 6–8 percentage points for mines producing over 50,000 metric tons/year. CODELCO (state producer) faces underfunding of maintenance CAPEX. At current copper prices, three major projects have negative NPV under the new regime. Market is pricing 3% supply disruption risk; the fundamental case suggests 10–15% is more realistic within an 18-month window.

**Thesis Statement (Short):**
"Copper prices incorporate a 10%+ supply risk premium by Q1 2027, or this thesis fails."

**Three-Filter Check:**
- *Already priced?* Copper has moved from $3.90 to $4.20 in the past 6 months (+8%) — partially reflecting this risk. But the *magnitude* of the risk is underpriced. A PROBE-to-TRIM entry, not a CORE.
- *Counterfactual:* Chinese construction demand collapse (-15%+) would overwhelm supply tightness. Track Caixin PMI and property starts monthly.
- *Clean expression:* Copper futures (CME) are the cleanest. HG (COMEX) front-month or a 6-month roll. Secondary: COPX (Global X Copper Miners ETF) — one degree of separation.

**Position Sizing:**
- Thesis confidence: Medium-High. Market crowding: Medium (Goldman and JPM have similar views).
- Verdict: TRIM (1.5% of portfolio).

**Instrument:** COMEX copper 6-month forward (primary); COPX as secondary if futures not accessible.

**Tranches:**
- T1 (50% = 0.75%): Enter now at $4.20.
- T2 (30% = 0.45%): Enter on next escalation signal (royalty legislation vote, CODELCO output data).
- T3 (20% = 0.30%): Enter on secondary confirmation (capex cuts announced by majors).

**Exit Thesis:**
- *Correct:* Copper reaches $5.20–5.50 and/or investment bank consensus incorporates 10%+ supply disruption. Exit in full.
- *Wrong:* 18 months pass without meaningful price move toward target AND China demand remains intact. Implies market sees something I don't — exit 75%, hold 25% monitor.
- *Time-based:* Q1 2027 checkpoint. Reassess full position regardless of P&L.

## IV. The Investment Log Entry — How This Gets Recorded

Every position constructed through this framework gets an entry in `reports/investment_log.md`:

```
DATE: 2026-09-01
THESIS: Chile mining royalty reform → copper supply disruption premium underpriced
ASSET: COMEX Copper (HG) 6-month forward; secondary COPX
POSITION SIZE: 1.5% (TRIM — medium-high confidence, medium crowding)
TIMEFRAME: 12–18 months (Q1 2027 primary checkpoint)
FALSIFIABLE CONDITION: Thesis fails if Q1 2027 arrives and copper has not incorporated 10%+ supply risk premium in the absence of China demand collapse
CURRENT STATUS: T1 entered 0.75%; T2/T3 pending confirmation signals
TRACKING SIGNALS: CODELCO quarterly output, Chilean mining royalty legislation, Caixin PMI, copper inventory (LME, SHFE)
```

This is not optional. Every recommendation without an investment log entry is analysis, not investing.
