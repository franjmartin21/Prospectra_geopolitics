## I. The Core Legal Distinction: Investment Adviser vs. Research Provider

### The Investment Advisers Act of 1940

In the United States, the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 defines an "investment adviser" as any person who:
1. **For compensation,**
2. **Engages in the business of**
3. **Providing advice or analysis concerning securities.**

All three elements must be present for registration to be required. This three-prong test is the central framework.

Prospectra provides geopolitical intelligence that has investment implications. Whether it must register as an investment adviser under the Act depends on how it structures its service, its language, and its contractual relationships.

### The Research Provider Exemption

The SEC has consistently recognized that publishers of research — including financial research — are not investment advisers when their publications:
1. Are **general in nature** — not tailored to the specific circumstances of individual clients
2. Are **widely distributed** — available to a subscription base rather than provided to a single client
3. Do **not purport to meet individual investment needs** — the research does not take into account the specific situation, risk tolerance, or portfolio of the subscriber

The **"publisher's exemption"** under the Investment Advisers Act (Section 202(a)(11)(D)) explicitly excludes from the definition of investment adviser "the publisher of any bona fide newspaper, news magazine or business or financial publication of general and regular circulation."

The SEC has applied this broadly to subscription research services — including quantitative research, factor models, and geopolitical risk scoring services — provided the service structure maintains the characteristics above.

**The practical implication:** Prospectra can publish geopolitical risk analysis, country scores, sector signals, and investment implications — and charge for access — without registering as an investment adviser, provided it is structured correctly.

## II. How Prospectra Must Structure Its Service to Stay Within the Exemption

The distinction is not merely semantic. It requires specific structural choices across four dimensions:

### 1. Language Architecture — What Prospectra Can and Cannot Say

**Permitted (research/analysis language):**
- "The Geopolitical Risk Index for Turkey has increased from 62 to 74. Historically, GRI readings above 70 have been associated with Turkish lira underperformance against the dollar over 6-month horizons."
- "The energy supply disruption risk score for the Strait of Hormuz is elevated. Investment implication: long Brent crude futures positions may have a favorable risk/reward in this environment."
- "We view defense sector equities in Europe as having positive structural tailwinds given NATO rearmament dynamics."

**Not permitted (investment advice language):**
- "You should allocate 5% of your portfolio to energy sector ETFs."
- "We recommend that you sell your exposure to Turkish equities."
- "Based on your risk profile, the appropriate position size for this signal is X."

The distinction is between **analysis about asset classes in general** and **personalized recommendations for a specific client's portfolio**. Prospectra publishes the former. It never touches the latter.

**The key test:** Could Prospectra publish the same content to 1,000 subscribers simultaneously without any customization? If yes, it is research. If it requires knowing the subscriber's individual circumstances, it is advice.

### 2. Disclaimer Architecture — What Must Appear on Every Publication

Every Prospectra publication, report, and subscriber communication must include a clear disclaimer. The following structure satisfies SEC guidance and matches the practices of established research services:

---

*"Prospectra Geopolitics & Investment provides geopolitical risk analysis and investment research for informational purposes only. This material does not constitute investment advice, a solicitation, or a recommendation to buy or sell any security or asset. Prospectra is not a registered investment adviser under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940. Subscribers should not rely on this material as the primary basis for any investment decision. All investment decisions involve risk; past analytical performance is not indicative of future results. Subscribers should consult with a licensed financial advisor regarding investment decisions specific to their circumstances."*

---

This disclaimer is not boilerplate to be dismissed. It is the legal boundary marker that keeps Prospectra outside the RIA registration requirement. It must appear:
- On every weekly briefing
- On every daily lesson delivered commercially
- On the Prospectra website and marketing materials
- In the subscription agreement signed by every subscriber
- In the footer of every email distribution

### 3. Contract Architecture — The Subscription Agreement Terms

The subscription agreement is the legal document that defines the relationship between Prospectra and each subscriber. It must contain:

**a) Research Product Definition Clause**
*"Prospectra provides geopolitical intelligence research and analysis as a subscription research product. The service is general in nature, is provided uniformly to all subscribers, and is not customized to the individual financial circumstances, investment objectives, or risk tolerance of any subscriber."*

**b) Non-Advisory Acknowledgment**
*"Subscriber acknowledges that Prospectra does not provide investment advice as defined under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, and that Prospectra is not registered as an investment adviser with the Securities and Exchange Commission or any state securities authority."*

**c) No Reliance Clause**
*"Subscriber agrees not to rely solely on Prospectra's geopolitical intelligence products in making investment decisions and acknowledges that such decisions should be made in consultation with a qualified financial professional who understands Subscriber's individual circumstances."*

**d) Track Record Disclaimer**
*"Historical GRI scores and investment implications contained in Prospectra's research are provided for analytical calibration purposes only and do not represent guaranteed outcomes. The investment implications stated in Prospectra's research are analytical observations about historical relationships between geopolitical conditions and asset class performance, not predictions of future performance."*

These four clauses, incorporated into a clean subscription agreement reviewed by a securities attorney (the cost: $1,500–$3,000 for a one-time legal review), provide the contractual foundation that satisfies most institutional compliance departments.

### 4. Subscriber Engagement Architecture — How Prospectra Interacts with Subscribers

The structural risks to the research product exemption typically arise not from published content but from subscriber interactions:

**High-risk interaction patterns to avoid:**
- A subscriber emails asking "Should I buy gold given your latest briefing?" and you respond with "Yes, I'd buy gold here."
- A subscriber asks "What allocation percentage do you recommend to energy sector ETFs?" and you respond with a specific allocation.
- A subscriber asks for a portfolio review and you provide one.

**Safe interaction patterns:**
- "The briefing addresses energy sector dynamics in Section III — the GRI score for Middle East instability rose 8 points this week and we discuss the historical correlation with Brent crude in detail there."
- "We're not able to provide personalized portfolio recommendations — that would require us to be registered as an investment adviser. What I can do is expand on the analytical framework we use to evaluate supply disruption risk."
- "Our analysis of defense sector tailwinds is in the European NATO Dynamics section — we discuss the structural drivers and historical precedents there, but specific allocation decisions are outside what we provide."

This framing is not evasive. It is accurate: Prospectra genuinely does not have the subscriber-specific knowledge required to make personalized recommendations. The legal structure and the analytical structure are aligned.

## III. The Historical Case — How Comparable Services Are Structured

### Stratfor (1996–present)
Stratfor explicitly structures itself as a geopolitical intelligence subscription service — not investment advice. Its subscriber terms contain language almost identical to the framework above. Stratfor's legal positioning: we provide analytical context. What you do with that context is your decision. This has never triggered SEC registration requirements in Stratfor's nearly 30-year history.

### Eurasia Group (1998–present)
Ian Bremmer's Eurasia Group sells geopolitical risk analysis to institutional investors, corporations, and governments. It explicitly does not register as an investment adviser. Its products — including the G-Zero World annual outlook, bespoke country briefings, and the Eurasia Group Top Risks report — are structured as research products. Its institutional clients use Eurasia Group analysis as one input into investment decisions; Eurasia Group does not make investment decisions on their behalf.

### Oxford Analytica (1975–present)
One of the oldest geopolitical risk research firms. Provides analysis to governments, central banks, sovereign wealth funds, and institutional investors. Not an RIA. Its products are research. Its subscription agreements contain standard research product disclaimers.

**The pattern:** Every established geopolitical intelligence service of any scale has navigated this question by the same means — structural discipline in language, comprehensive disclaimers, and subscription terms that define the service as research rather than advice.

### The Exception: PineBridge Investments' Political Risk Group
When investment advice is integrated with geopolitical analysis — PineBridge's political risk unit operates as part of a registered investment adviser, providing analysis that directly informs managed account allocations — the RIA registration applies. The difference is that PineBridge's political risk team provides analysis *to portfolio managers within the firm* who are making fiduciary allocation decisions. The analysis is an input to investment advice, not a standalone published research product.

**Prospectra's position:** Prospectra is Oxford Analytica, not PineBridge. It publishes research. It does not manage money.

This document does not go in the pitch deck. It goes in the due diligence package sent to compliance departments after an initial meeting. Its existence signals that Prospectra has thought about this question and has a prepared answer — which is, itself, an institutional credibility signal.

## V. Investment Implications

### For Prospectra's Own Architecture
The legal structure decision is effectively made: Prospectra is a research product, not an investment adviser. This is the correct structure at the current stage for three reasons:
1. RIA registration requires ongoing compliance infrastructure (Form ADV filings, compliance policies, potential state registration) that is disproportionate overhead for a pre-revenue startup
2. The research product structure aligns with the subscriber value proposition — subscribers want analytical insight, not someone to manage their money
3. The research product structure scales: Prospectra can add subscribers without proportional increase in regulatory burden

### If Prospectra Ever Evolves Toward Managed Accounts or Model Portfolios
The moment Prospectra moves from "we publish analysis" to "we manage a portfolio based on our analysis" — even informally, even as a demonstration account — the RIA question reopens. That is a deliberate strategic choice requiring regulatory preparation, not an informal product evolution. The line is: are you making investment decisions on behalf of a client? If yes, RIA registration applies. This constraint should inform future product decisions.

### For Understanding the Broader Geopolitical Intelligence Market
The research product structure is why the geopolitical intelligence industry operates as it does. Firms like Stratfor, Eurasia Group, and Oxford Analytica have enormous analytical scope and institutional client bases — but they are not regulated as financial advisers. This creates an interesting market dynamic: their institutional clients (hedge funds, SWFs, asset managers) are paying for input into decisions, and the intelligence firms have no legal accountability for whether the analysis leads to profitable decisions. This is both a protection and a constraint on product evolution.
