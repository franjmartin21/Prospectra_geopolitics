## Opening Question

*It is Month 10. You have a client who signed a 12-month Intelligence tier subscription. The onboarding went well — they reached Level 3 on the engagement ladder by Month 2 and have cited the GRI framework in two investment committee discussions. The relationship is good. The research has been strong.*

*The renewal conversation is in six weeks. The client's procurement team will ask the portfolio manager for a recommendation. The portfolio manager will think about it for ten minutes and say something to procurement.*

*Here is the question: What does that ten-minute internal conversation look like — and what determines whether it ends with "we should renew" or "let's evaluate this more carefully"?*

Sit with that for a moment before reading on.

The answer is not "how much they like the CEO" or "how good the research has been." Those factors exist. But the ten-minute internal evaluation follows a predictable structure. The portfolio manager asks themselves, implicitly or explicitly, three things:

1. **Can I point to something specific?** Not "did I find this useful" — but "can I name a decision, a discussion, or a position this changed?" If the answer is yes, the renewal is easy to justify internally.
2. **What breaks if we cancel?** If the GRI framework is embedded in a standing workflow, cancellation has a cost — the portfolio manager has to explain to the investment committee why the risk input disappeared from the agenda. If it is not embedded, cancellation has no operational consequence.
3. **Is the price proportionate to my ability to defend it?** Institutional subscription renewals require a procurement justification. A portfolio manager who can point to a decision and a workflow is in a strong position to defend the price. One who can only say "I found it useful" is in a weak position — because procurement will ask "useful enough to justify $X?" and the answer without specifics is uncertain.

The entire Prospectra renewal architecture is designed around making all three of these questions easy to answer affirmatively — before the procurement team ever asks them.

## I. The Renewal Conversation Is Won at Month 3, Not Month 11

The most common mistake institutional research firms make is treating the renewal as an event that happens at the end of the contract. It is not. The renewal decision is made continuously throughout the engagement — it is the accumulated answer to the question "is this worth what I am paying for it?" Most of that accumulation happens in the first 90 days.

This is why the 90-Day Activation Framework from Lesson 352 is not just an onboarding tool — it is the primary renewal architecture. A client who ends Month 3 at Level 4 on the engagement ladder (GRI framework cited in investment committee discussions) has effectively pre-made their renewal decision. They will renew unless something materially changes, because the framework has become an operational input they would have to consciously remove.

**The implication for the CEO:** Every action taken in the first 90 days of a client relationship is a renewal action. Every action taken after Month 9 is a negotiation recovery. The CEO's leverage over the renewal outcome is high early and falls sharply as the contract end approaches.

### The Renewal Decision Timeline

| Month | What Is Happening | CEO Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Onboarding. The client is forming their first impression of whether the framework is useful. | Execute the 90-Day Activation Framework. Get to Level 3 or above. Create at least one citable case study moment. |
| 4–6 | Steady state. The client is consuming the research as a routine. Passive consumption trap risk is highest here. | Monthly analytical check-in. Flag the most relevant GRI signals to their specific portfolio. Keep the research active and specific. |
| 7–9 | Mid-year assessment. Some institutions do informal mid-year budget reviews. The portfolio manager begins forming a renewal recommendation in their head. | Deliver the Mid-Year Review call (see below). Quantify value delivered. Identify the expansion opportunity. |
| 10 | Procurement cycle begins. The institutional procurement team starts asking for renewal justifications. | The portfolio manager already has their answer ready — or they don't. If they don't, the CEO is now in damage control, not renewal management. |
| 11–12 | Renewal negotiation, if any. | If the renewal architecture worked, there is no negotiation. There is a conversation about whether to expand. |

## II. The Three Renewal Anchors

Regardless of the client's tier, engagement level, or industry, there are three specific deliverables the CEO must complete before Month 10 to ensure the renewal is a continuation rather than a negotiation. Call these the **Three Renewal Anchors**.

### Anchor 1 — The Citable Case Study

The renewal anchor most under the CEO's direct control. In every client engagement, at least one GRI signal fires during the contract period that is relevant to a position the client is managing. The CEO's job is to identify that moment when it happens — not in retrospect at Month 11 — and build a one-page case study documenting:

- The signal: what the GRI framework flagged, when it flagged it, and the analytical rationale
- The portfolio relevance: which of the client's exposures were directly affected
- The market outcome: what happened to the relevant asset price, currency, or spread in the 30, 60, and 90 days following the signal

This case study is delivered to the client as a proactive analytical update — not as a sales document, but as a demonstration of real-time framework performance. It is the single most important piece of content the CEO can produce for a client, because it converts abstract usefulness ("I find this interesting") into concrete proof ("the framework identified something I should have been watching").

**The target:** At least one citable case study per client per 6-month period. Two is better. Three makes the renewal conversation a formality.

**Timing:** Deliver the first case study by Day 75 of onboarding (as specified in the 90-Day Activation Framework). The second, if a relevant signal fires in Months 4–9, is delivered within two weeks of the market outcome becoming observable.

---

### Anchor 2 — The Mid-Year Value Review

At the midpoint of the annual contract (Month 6, or a comparable natural review point), the CEO delivers a structured 30-minute call framed as a strategic review rather than a check-in. The structure:

**Part 1 — What We Delivered (10 minutes):**
A factual summary of the GRI analytical output relevant to the client's portfolio over the past 6 months: the country signals that fired, the regime transitions detected, the commodity pressure model readings that were relevant to their exposures. This is not a subjective argument for value — it is an objective enumeration of what was produced.

**Part 2 — What Changed in Your World (10 minutes):**
An open-ended conversation: "Looking at your portfolio now versus 6 months ago — what has changed in your risk environment, and where are you most focused?" This serves two purposes. First, it recalibrates the GRI output to the client's current portfolio priorities. Second, it identifies new countries, themes, or asset classes that have become relevant — which is the first signal of an expansion opportunity.

**Part 3 — What We Are Watching (10 minutes):**
A forward-looking analytical brief: "In the next 6 months, here are the three GRI signals we are monitoring most closely for your portfolio — and here is why they matter for your specific exposures." This positions the Prospectra research as proactive rather than reactive, and gives the client a concrete reason to stay engaged for the next 6 months.

**The Mid-Year Review produces three assets:**
1. A renewal justification the client can use internally ("our research provider did a mid-year review and here is what they flagged")
2. A recalibrated understanding of the client's current portfolio for more targeted research delivery
3. An expansion opportunity map — new countries or themes where Prospectra coverage is relevant but not yet being consumed

---

### Anchor 3 — The Expansion Conversation

Before the renewal conversation happens, there should be a conversation about going deeper. Not bigger — deeper. The expansion conversation is distinct from the renewal conversation, and it should happen before Month 10.

The expansion conversation is triggered by one of three observable signals:

1. **Coverage Gap:** The client's portfolio exposure has expanded into a country or region not currently covered by their subscription tier. The CEO sees this during the Mid-Year Review or through a signal that fires on a country the client mentioned but the GRI output did not flag as a primary focus.

2. **Integration Depth:** The client has reached Level 4 or 5 on the engagement ladder — the GRI framework is being cited in investment committee discussions. At this level, the client has demonstrated that they can use the research; the expansion question is whether they would benefit from a customized format (country deep-dive reports, quarterly scenario planning sessions, direct analytical briefings for the investment committee).

3. **Team Spread:** The client mentions that other portfolio managers or analysts at their institution are asking about the GRI output or requesting access to specific reports. This is the organic expansion signal — the research has spread beyond the primary contact.

**How to frame the expansion conversation:**
Not "would you like to upgrade?" but "I want to make sure we are delivering the right scope for your current exposure — based on what I saw in the Mid-Year Review, I think there may be a case for expanding the coverage we are providing. Let me show you what that would look like."

The expansion offer should be specific — not "upgrade to the Partnership tier" but "add these three country deep-dives to your current package because X, Y, and Z are the regions where your portfolio has the most currently unmonitored GRI exposure."

## III. The Tier Upgrade Architecture

Prospectra's product architecture (from earlier lessons) has three tiers: Signal, Intelligence, and Partnership. Most clients enter at Intelligence. The first expansion conversation, therefore, is about the path from Intelligence to Partnership — or, for clients who entered at Signal, the move to Intelligence.

### What Partnership Actually Means

The Partnership tier is not just "more research." It is a fundamentally different engagement model:

| Dimension | Intelligence | Partnership |
|---|---|---|
| GRI output format | Standard weekly report, portfolio-calibrated | Custom brief format, investment committee-ready |
| Research depth | Country-level GRI scores + regime signals | Country-level + sector-level + asset class-specific analysis |
| CEO access | Quarterly review calls | Monthly strategic calls + ad hoc analytical queries |
| Customization | Portfolio-calibrated delivery | Bespoke research on request |
| Price | $2,400–$8,400/year (institutional tier) | $15,000–$40,000/year |

The price jump is substantial. The value jump is also substantial — Partnership is not a 10% improvement in research quality; it is a shift from consuming signal to having a geopolitical research partner embedded in the investment process.

**The first Partnership client will not come from cold outreach.** It will come from an Intelligence client who reached Level 4–5, has a citable case study, participated in the Mid-Year Value Review, and received an expansion offer framed specifically around their portfolio's unmet analytical needs.

**Timeline for first Partnership client:** Late Year 1 or early Year 2, assuming the onboarding architecture is executed correctly for the first 5–10 Intelligence clients. The CEO's goal in Year 1 is not to close Partnership clients — it is to build the Intelligence client base that produces Partnership clients naturally.

## IV. The Renewal Conversation Script

When the renewal conversation happens — whether initiated by the client's procurement team or proactively opened by the CEO — it follows a specific structure. This is not a script in the sense of memorized lines. It is a structure that ensures the CEO controls the framing.

### The Three-Act Renewal Conversation

**Act 1 — Value Recall (Client Does the Talking):**
Open by asking the client to reflect, not by presenting a case: "Before we talk about the renewal, I'd like to hear from you — from where you sit, what has been most useful about the GRI research over the past year?"

This inverts the normal dynamic. In a standard renewal pitch, the vendor presents value and the client evaluates it. In the Prospectra architecture, the client articulates value themselves — which is far more persuasive than the CEO articulating it on their behalf. If the onboarding architecture worked, the client will cite specific signals, specific decisions, or specific workflow integrations. The CEO's job in Act 1 is to listen and take notes.

If the client struggles to articulate specific value, this is diagnostic — they are at Level 1 or 2 on the engagement ladder, and the renewal is at risk. The CEO should not proceed with the renewal pitch; instead, immediately schedule a dedicated analytical session to create a citable case study before the procurement deadline.

**Act 2 — Forward View (CEO Does the Talking):**
Once the client has reflected on the past year, pivot to the next 12 months: "Based on where your portfolio is positioned and what I see in the GRI pipeline — here are the three signals I am monitoring most closely for your exposures over the next 12 months."

This anchors the renewal decision to future value, not historical value. The client is not renewing for what they got. They are renewing for what they expect to get. By naming specific signals the CEO is already watching, the renewal becomes forward-looking rather than retrospective.

**Act 3 — Continuation or Expansion (Structural Close):**
Frame the renewal as the natural continuation of work in progress: "Given where we are and what we are watching together, the question for the next year is whether you want to continue at the current scope or expand coverage in [X, Y, Z areas I identified]."

This presents two options — continuation and expansion — neither of which is cancellation. The framing creates a decision about scope, not a decision about whether to renew. Procurement teams are sophisticated; this does not trick them. But it shapes the internal conversation the portfolio manager has with procurement, because they are now comparing "continue as is" versus "expand" rather than "renew or cancel."

## V. Historical Case Study — How McKinsey Builds Renewal-Proof Client Relationships

The Prospectra renewal architecture borrows from a model that has produced some of the highest renewal rates in professional services: the McKinsey engagement model.

McKinsey's renewal rates on large client accounts consistently exceed 85% over multi-year periods — despite charging fees that are easily justified as candidates for internal cost-cutting in any budget cycle. The reason is not the quality of the consulting work alone. It is a structural feature of how McKinsey embeds itself in client institutions.

**The McKinsey embeddedness mechanism:**
1. **Knowledge asymmetry:** McKinsey teams spend months inside a client institution, developing a deep institutional knowledge of the organization, its politics, its processes, and its specific strategic challenges. This knowledge is not transferable to a new consulting firm. If the client switches, they spend the first 3–6 months of any new engagement re-teaching the new firm what McKinsey already knows. The switching cost is the knowledge asymmetry, not the quality difference.

2. **The internal champion network:** McKinsey builds relationships not just with the senior executive who commissioned the engagement, but with the 3–5 internal executives who are most likely to sponsor future work. These "internal champions" become advocates for continuation — they have invested their own credibility in the McKinsey relationship and are naturally motivated to defend it at renewal.

3. **The pipeline conversation:** McKinsey is always proposing the next engagement before the current one closes. The renewal conversation does not end the relationship — it opens the conversation about what comes next. The client is never evaluating whether to renew; they are evaluating what scope to commission in the next phase.

**The Prospectra parallel:**
- Knowledge asymmetry = the Portfolio Map. The CEO's accumulated knowledge of the client's portfolio exposures, investment process, and GRI signal relevance is not transferable to a generic competitor. A new geopolitical research provider starts from zero; Prospectra starts from 12 months of portfolio-specific calibration.
- Internal champion network = the engagement ladder. A client at Level 4–5 has developed their own internal investment rationale for the GRI framework — they have cited it in investment committee discussions. They become internal advocates for renewal.
- Pipeline conversation = the expansion offer. The Prospectra CEO is always proposing the next scope of work — deeper coverage, additional countries, a new deliverable format — before the renewal conversation.

**The McKinsey model at small scale:** Prospectra cannot replicate McKinsey's institutional depth with a one-person team. But the structural logic transfers exactly: build asymmetric knowledge of the client's portfolio, cultivate internal champions by making specific portfolio managers look smart with the research, and always be proposing the next engagement before the current one ends.

## VI. The Databricks Angle

The renewal architecture is partially operationalizable in the Prospectra Databricks platform.

### The Renewal Risk Score

In addition to the Client Health Score introduced in Lesson 352, the Prospectra data platform should maintain a **Renewal Risk Score** — a composite metric that flags clients approaching renewal who may be at elevated churn risk.

**Renewal Risk Score components:**

| Signal | Weight | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement ladder level (as of Month 9) | 35% | Manual CRM entry — Level 1–5 |
| Case study count | 25% | Number of citable case studies delivered to client |
| Mid-Year Review completion | 20% | Binary — was the call completed before Month 7? |
| Response latency trend | 10% | Is the primary contact responding to check-ins faster or slower than in Month 1–3? |
| GRI signal relevance score | 10% | Portfolio Map x current GRI output — are the signals being delivered still relevant to their current exposures? |

A client approaching renewal (Month 9+) with a Renewal Risk Score below 65 triggers a CEO intervention: a direct analytical call scheduled immediately, focused on creating or refreshing a citable case study and reopening the expansion conversation.

**Databricks implementation:**
- Delta Lake table: `prospectra.clients.renewal_risk` — one row per client per week
- Automated inputs: email response latency from tracking API, GRI signal relevance from GRI pipeline cross-referenced with Portfolio Map
- Manual inputs: engagement level, case study count, review completion status (CRM integration or manual Delta entry)
- Monday job: flags clients with Renewal Risk Score below 65 and clients entering Month 9 of their contract

This creates a closed-loop system: the analytical platform (GRI pipeline) generates the product, and the operational platform (renewal risk model) monitors whether the product is driving the client outcomes that predict renewal. The CEO sees both in the same Monday morning dashboard.

## Investment Implications

The renewal architecture illuminates a core principle in evaluating subscription business quality: **net revenue retention (NRR) is a better quality signal than gross revenue retention.**

Gross retention measures whether clients stayed. Net retention measures whether clients stayed *and grew their spending*. A business with 90% gross retention and 85% NRR is a declining business masquerading as a stable one — its client base is churning faster than it appears because the surviving clients are spending less each year. A business with 90% gross retention and 115% NRR is a compounding machine — its surviving clients are deepening their engagement faster than new-client churn reduces the base.

The expansion architecture Prospectra is building — specifically the tier upgrade from Intelligence to Partnership — is the mechanism for NRR above 100%. If even 20% of Intelligence clients upgrade to Partnership each year, total revenue from the existing client base grows without adding a single new client. This is the economic model that separates high-quality subscription businesses from average ones.

**For evaluating portfolio companies:**
When underwriting a subscription business, the key question is not "what is their churn rate?" but "what is driving their NRR, and is that driver structural or cyclical?" FactSet's NRR has historically exceeded 105% because workflow embedding creates natural expansion — as portfolio teams grow, as data needs broaden, as new analytical requirements emerge, clients add seats and modules. That expansion is structural (driven by the client's growing need, not FactSet's sales effort) and is therefore more defensible than NRR driven by upsell campaigns.

*Directional view:* Long-horizon investors should pay a premium for subscription businesses where the expansion mechanism is structural (workflow embedding, platform dependency, data integration) rather than commercial (active upsell campaigns, promotional pricing for upgrades). The structural mechanism compounds; the commercial mechanism compresses under competition.

## Reflection Questions

**1. The Three Renewal Anchors assume the CEO can identify "citable case study moments" when they happen — a GRI signal fires, a market outcome follows, and the CEO connects the dots and documents the case. In practice, the CEO is also running GRI production, outbound sales, client check-ins, and Databricks build work. Design a monitoring protocol: what specific workflow ensures the CEO sees a GRI-relevant market outcome in the 30-60 day window after a signal fires, while the connection is still causal and fresh rather than retrospective and constructed?**

Think about: what data inputs, what alert thresholds, what calendar triggers create the conditions for proactive case study identification rather than Month 11 retrospective reconstruction?

**2. The three-act renewal conversation relies on the client being able to articulate specific value in Act 1. But some institutional clients — especially those with formal procurement processes — will not engage in open-ended value reflection with a vendor. The procurement team sends a standardized renewal questionnaire asking the portfolio manager to rate satisfaction on a 1–5 scale across a fixed set of dimensions. How does the CEO design a renewal architecture that works in both environments — the relational conversation with an engaged portfolio manager and the formal procurement questionnaire with a commoditizing institutional buyer?**

**3. The McKinsey model creates knowledge asymmetry as a renewal protection mechanism. But there is a risk: if the CEO holds all the institutional knowledge of the client's portfolio internally, and that knowledge is not documented in the Prospectra CRM or data platform, then the knowledge asymmetry is actually a single-point-of-failure — if the CEO is unavailable, the client relationship degrades rapidly. How should the Prospectra client knowledge architecture be designed to retain the competitive advantage of deep portfolio knowledge while ensuring the knowledge is not exclusively held by one person?**

This question matters most when Prospectra makes its first hire — because the new hire will immediately need access to every client's Portfolio Map, engagement history, and case study library to be effective.
