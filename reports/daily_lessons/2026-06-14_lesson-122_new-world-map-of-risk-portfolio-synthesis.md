## Section 2: The Correlation Matrix — How Crises Interact

This is the part most investors miss. Individual scenario analysis is necessary but not sufficient. The critical question is: **which crises amplify each other, and which offset?**

### High Positive Correlation (One crisis strengthens the other)

**Ukraine Escalation + Hormuz Tension:**
If Russia escalates to a major offensive in Ukraine, energy markets reprice risk globally. Iran reads reduced Western bandwidth as an opportunity to advance its nuclear program or support Houthi operations more aggressively. The energy risk premium spikes in both theaters simultaneously. If you are short energy as a ceasefire play in Ukraine, a simultaneous Hormuz incident detonates your position.

**Sahel Collapse + DRC Resource Wars:**
West African instability feeds Central African instability. Supply chain disruption in cobalt from DRC can compound with uranium supply disruption from Niger (Sahel) simultaneously, creating a critical mineral double-shock.

**North Korea Provocation + Taiwan Strait Tension:**
Pyongyang's tactical provocations (missile tests, nuclear signaling) serve Beijing's interests by consuming US and South Korean military bandwidth. When North Korea escalates, the probability of opportunistic Chinese pressure on Taiwan rises. These two theaters are strategically coupled.

### Low/Negative Correlation (Crises that offset each other)

**Ukraine Ceasefire + Middle East Escalation:**
A Ukraine ceasefire frees US diplomatic and military bandwidth for Middle East engagement — potentially stabilizing or potentially escalating depending on how bandwidth is deployed. These two theaters partially compete for US attention, meaning a resolution in one can redirect pressure to the other.

**India-Pakistan Stability + China-Taiwan Tension:**
A stable India-Pakistan relationship frees Indian strategic capacity for the Indo-Pacific contest — making India a more credible balancer against China. A destabilized South Asia actually reduces Chinese coercive pressure on Taiwan (by distracting US/Indian attention).

## Section 3: The Unified Portfolio Map — June 2026 Edition

This is the synthesis. Based on the theater inventory and correlation matrix, here is how a geopolitically-informed portfolio is structured in June 2026:

### Core Long Positions (Structural, 18–36 Month Horizon)

**1. European Defense Equities — HIGH CONVICTION**
- Rationale: Benefits across all Ukraine scenarios except nuclear escalation. Additionally benefits from rearmament impulse regardless of any single theater.
- Key names: Rheinmetall, BAE Systems, Saab, Leonardo, Thales, KNDS
- ETF expression: NATO (iShares), EUAD (VanEck), DFNS (HANetf)
- Position sizing: 8–12% of equity allocation
- **Kill switch:** Ukraine ceasefire + sustained US-Russia détente + NATO spending reversal (none of these are near-term)

**2. Energy Producers + Infrastructure — MEDIUM CONVICTION**
- Rationale: Structural energy price floor from Hormuz risk premium + Russia supply rerouting + energy transition underinvestment
- Key names: Saudi Aramco (indirect via ETFs), Qatar Petroleum bonds, US LNG exporters (Cheniere), European energy infrastructure (Enagas, Fluxys)
- ETF expression: XLE (US energy), MLPA (pipelines)
- Position sizing: 6–8% of portfolio
- **Kill switch:** Iran nuclear deal that fully restores supply + Ukraine ceasefire removing Russia premium simultaneously (low probability)

**3. Critical Minerals + Transition Metals — MEDIUM CONVICTION**
- Rationale: DRC cobalt disruption + Sahel uranium disruption + EV supply chain restructuring
- Key names: Glencore (cobalt/copper), Cameco (uranium), MP Materials (US rare earths)
- ETF expression: REMX (rare earths), URNM (uranium), CPER (copper)
- Position sizing: 4–6%
- **Kill switch:** DRC stabilization + OPEC+ cobalt supply diversification (2–3 year lag minimum)

**4. Gold — STRUCTURAL HEDGE**
- Rationale: Performs across all high-risk scenarios. Central bank reserve diversification (away from USD) continues regardless of any single theater. Real rates declining in multi-theater instability environments.
- Position sizing: 5–8%
- **Kill switch:** No near-term kill switch. This is permanent geopolitical insurance.

### Tactical / High-Conviction Opportunistic

**5. Ukrainian Reconstruction Exposure — ASYMMETRIC TRADE**
- Rationale: Ceasefire (45% probability) triggers 30–50% rally in Ukrainian Eurobonds. Currently priced for war, not reconstruction.
- Instrument: Ukrainian Eurobonds (USD-denominated, due 2028/2030/2033). Available via EM bond funds or direct market.
- Position sizing: 2–3% — sized for binary outcome, not for comfort
- **Kill switch:** Any escalation to Scenario C (Russian advance) → immediate exit. The bond market will signal before news does.

**6. Japan Defense + Strategic Infrastructure — MEDIUM CONVICTION**
- Rationale: Japan's defense build-up (Takaichi Doctrine: 2% GDP → 3%) is the largest Japanese military expansion since 1945. Driven by North Korea + China pressure. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Kawasaki Heavy Industries, NEC (defense electronics) are structural beneficiaries.
- Position sizing: 2–4%
- **Kill switch:** Japan political reversal on defense spending (opposition wins on pacifist platform — low probability)

### Short / Underweight Positions

**7. Emerging Market Sovereign Debt (Fragile States) — UNDERWEIGHT**
- Egypt, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Ghana — all carry IMF revolving door dynamics + geopolitical exposure
- Tactical exceptions: only at deep distress valuations during IMF stabilization windows (not now for most)

**8. Chinese Equities (Mainland) — UNDERWEIGHT**
- Taiwan risk premium + structural decoupling from Western capital + domestic economic challenges
- Not zero (for diversification reasons), but significantly underweight vs. EM index weight

- Energy: Slight uplift (geopolitical risk premium)

- Energy: BULLISH
- Ukrainian bonds: SELL
- Gold: STRONGLY BULLISH
- EM exposure: NEGATIVE
- **Portfolio performance: POSITIVE overall** (hard assets and defense dominate)

**Key finding from stress test:** The portfolio is positively skewed across all four major concurrent scenarios. The structural long positions in defense, energy, and hard assets function as natural hedges against the tail risks that would detonate typical long-only equity portfolios.

## Section 5: What We Are NOT Doing — The Discipline

Every framework requires negative constraints. Here is what this portfolio explicitly avoids:

**1. Single-country geopolitical bets** (except small tactical positions in Ukrainian bonds)
Ukraine, Pakistan, Egypt — these are single-factor bets that require specific scenario outcomes. Position sizing reflects this: 2–3% maximum, sized for binary outcomes.

**2. Short Russian assets through Western channels**
Russian assets are effectively uninvestable for Western capital. Any position is hypothetical until capital markets normalize. We don't speculate on instruments we cannot actually access.

**3. Tactical energy trading around geopolitical events**
We do not trade around a Hormuz incident announcement or Houthi shipping disruption. Our energy exposure is structural (12–18 month minimum), not responsive to individual news events. We lack the latency advantage to trade around news.

**4. Leveraged positions without explicit risk reasoning**
Every position above is unleveraged equity or bond exposure. No 2x or 3x products. Geopolitical uncertainty is already the leverage.
