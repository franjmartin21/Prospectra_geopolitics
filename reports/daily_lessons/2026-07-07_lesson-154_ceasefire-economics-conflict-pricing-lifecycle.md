## The Core Framework: The Conflict Premium Lifecycle

Every geopolitical conflict that affects markets goes through a predictable pricing lifecycle. Understanding which stage you are in is more valuable than predicting the outcome.

### Stage 1 — Outbreak Premium
When conflict begins or escalates unexpectedly, markets price in a **conflict premium** — the expected value of the worst-case scenario multiplied by its probability. The premium typically overshoots actual disruption (markets amplify fear).

- **Example:** February 24, 2022: Russia invades Ukraine. Brent crude +8% in 24 hours. European natural gas +50% in one week. By March 7, EU gas was 10x pre-war levels. Actual supply disruption came over 6-12 months — far slower than the price spike implied.
- **Investment signal:** Outbreak premiums are buying opportunities in affected commodities for those who can distinguish headline risk from actual supply disruption — and hold through the volatility.

### Stage 2 — Normalization
As conflict becomes embedded in the news cycle, markets price out the initial panic. Alternative supply chains emerge, hedging strategies deploy, the "new baseline" gets established.

- **Example:** By May 2022 (three months into Ukraine war), European gas had partially retraced as spot demand destruction kicked in and LNG cargoes rerouted from Asia. Markets were *learning* the new supply architecture.
- **Investment signal:** The normalization phase is where informed investors accumulate structural plays — defense, energy diversification capex, friend-shoring beneficiaries — that the panic phase put on sale.

### Stage 3 — Resolution/Ceasefire Rally
When ceasefire talks begin or an agreement is announced, markets price resolution before it is durable. The resolution premium is the mirror image of the outbreak premium: prices move too fast, too far, assuming the conflict ends cleanly and permanently.

- **Example:** Every credible Ukraine peace rumor in 2022-2023 produced a 10-20% correction in European defense stocks and a European gas sell-off — before talks collapsed and prices re-widened.
- **Investment signal:** Resolution rallies are the most dangerous trades. The critical question: **is this a durable peace, a frozen conflict, or a failed ceasefire?**

### Stage 4 — Resolution Classification: The Four Types

Not all ceasefires are equal. Markets treat "ceasefire" as binary (on/off). Reality is a spectrum:

| Type | Description | Duration | Market Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1: Full Resolution** | Underlying dispute settled; withdrawal; normalization | Permanent | Conflict premium collapses fully; normal pricing restored |
| **Type 2: Durable Armistice** | Fighting stops; dispute unresolved but managed (Korean model) | Decades | Premium compresses but persists; volatility premium baked into affected assets |
| **Type 3: Frozen Conflict** | Ceasefire with no political settlement; resumption risk latent | Months to years | Markets under-price resumption risk over time — asymmetric opportunity builds |
| **Type 4: Failed Ceasefire** | Ceasefire collapses within months | Weeks to months | Violent re-pricing; those who treated Type 4 as Type 1 face maximum drawdown |

**The classification question is the investment question.** The premium paid or earned from a conflict position is entirely a function of correctly classifying the ceasefire type before markets do.

## Historical Grounding: Three Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Korea, 1953 — The Armistice That Never Became Peace (Type 2)

The July 1953 Korean armistice stopped the fighting. A peace treaty was never signed. The Korean War is technically still active — 73 years of ceasefire.

**Market outcome:** KOSPI (Korean equities) trades at a permanent "military premium" discount relative to peers with equivalent economic fundamentals. Every North Korean missile test or nuclear provocation reprices Korean equities and the Korean won downward — 3-8% corrections on credible escalation signals. The discount compresses during dialogue phases (Moon-Kim summits 2018-2019 produced an 18% KOSPI rally over 6 months) and widens during provocations.

**Investment lesson:** Type 2 conflicts create a *structural discount* in adjacent equity markets. The premium on peace talks is real and tradeable. But the baseline is never "peace" — it is "managed nuclear standoff with 30,000 US troops as a tripwire." Size positions in the dialogue phase compression, not the permanent resolution that never comes.

### Case Study 2: Gulf War I, 1991 — The Clean Exit (Type 1)

August 1990: Iraq invades Kuwait. Oil spikes from $17 to $42 (+147%). February 1991: Coalition forces eject Iraq in 100 hours. Oil collapses back to $20 within three weeks.

**Market outcome:** This is the Type 1 model executed in unusually clean form. The reason: the underlying dispute (Iraqi control of Kuwaiti oil fields) was definitively reversed. Kuwait's production resumed. Saudi excess capacity absorbed uncertainty. The conflict had a definitive winner who solved the supply problem.

**Investment lesson:** Type 1 resolutions produce violent mean-reversion. Traders long energy through the war premium who did NOT exit before the armistice announcement suffered maximum drawdown. Speed of exit relative to announcement matters more than the original long position itself. **Never be long the conflict premium when the structural conflict has been resolved.**

### Case Study 3: Minsk Agreements, 2014–2022 — The Failed Ceasefire Template (Type 4)

Minsk I (September 2014) and Minsk II (February 2015) were signed ceasefire agreements for eastern Ukraine. Both broke down immediately in practice but were maintained as the diplomatic "framework" for 8 years.

**Market outcome:** Ukrainian sovereign bonds traded with a moderate, persistent discount (250-350 bps above EM peers). Eastern European defense equities began a slow, multi-year appreciation. Russian assets traded with a sanctions discount. But the FULL conflict premium did not price in until February 2022 — when the ceasefire definitively collapsed.

**Investment lesson:** Failed ceasefires (Type 4) create *periods of market complacency* where the underlying risk is known but structurally under-priced because the ceasefire is nominally "in force." This is exactly when the asymmetric opportunity is largest: the cost of the hedge is low, the premium of the next outbreak is enormous. The Minsk framework from 2015-2022 was 7 years of building Eastern European defense positions at steep discounts to their eventual value.

## Applying the Framework: Where Are We Now?

### Hormuz MOU — Type Classification

The current Hormuz ceasefire sits at the **Type 3/4 boundary**. Evidence for each classification:

**Evidence for Type 2 (Durable Armistice tendency):**
- Both sides are financially exhausted from the conflict period; ceasefire extends solvency runways
- The MOU has survived 60 days of political pressure — more durable than markets expected
- US domestic political interest in avoiding re-escalation before the 2027 midterms
- Iran's population is anti-war following the economic devastation of the strait closure

**Evidence for Type 4 (Failed Ceasefire risk):**
- The nuclear dispute is entirely unresolved — the MOU is a pause, not a framework
- No physical verification mechanism; Iran retains full Hormuz closure capability
- President Trump has not formally endorsed the MOU — it exists through State Department and IRGC-adjacent back-channels
- Iran's hardliner faction views the ceasefire as strategic retreat and is actively working to undermine it

**CEO Verdict:** Type 3 (Frozen Conflict) trending toward Type 2 if Trump formally endorses the MOU and nuclear track talks begin. Snaps to Type 4 if the 60-day clock expires without extension. The portfolio positioning in the July 6 briefing — energy optionality, tanker equity, defined-risk calls on USO — is calibrated specifically for Type 3/4 uncertainty. It is NOT positioned for Type 1 (full resolution) or Type 4 (immediate re-closure).

**Classification triggers to watch:**
- Trump formal MOU endorsement → Type 2 signal; oil corrects toward $65; reduce energy overweight to 3-5%
- MOU expires without extension, IRGC signals → Type 4; energy to 12% immediately, add tankers
- Nuclear framework talks announced → Type 1 trajectory begins; structural de-risking of Gulf energy premium

### Ukraine-Russia — Type Classification

This is a clear **Type 3 (Frozen Conflict)** trending toward permanent Frozen:
- Frontlines static for 18+ months
- Neither side can achieve decisive military breakthrough
- Russia is under fiscal strain from military expenditure but not fatal strain
- No political settlement framework exists
- Trump administration's bandwidth is constrained by the Hormuz situation

**Market implication:** Ukraine is fully normalized in market pricing — it is no longer a volatility driver on its own. The asymmetric risk is an unexpected de-escalation (positive for European equities, deeply negative for defense stocks) or a re-escalation beyond current frontlines (inverse). Hold defense as structural long; no position in Ukrainian reconstruction until political settlement is credible.
