## I. The Fundamental Problem We Are Solving

There is a well-documented cognitive failure in geopolitical investing: **the narrative-action gap**.

Investors who study geopolitics can construct compelling narratives about how events will unfold. But they cannot translate those narratives into investment decisions with an appropriate level of conviction, position sizing, and falsifiability. The result is one of three failure modes:

1. **Over-conviction:** They are so certain of their geopolitical read that they size the position too large, ignore contradicting evidence, and ride the position through falsifying events because "the narrative is still intact."

2. **Under-conviction:** The geopolitical thesis is intellectually compelling but the investor cannot bridge it to a specific asset and mechanism, so they remain perpetually in "watch and wait" mode. The event plays out, the trade would have worked, and they made nothing.

3. **Timing error:** They are right about the direction but wrong about the horizon. They enter too early, experience pain, and exit just before the thesis plays out. Being right about direction but wrong about patience is indistinguishable from being wrong.

The framework developed over 150 lessons addresses all three failures systematically. It is a **five-layer decision stack** — applied in order, it produces a position with explicit conviction, sizing, falsification criteria, and time horizon.

### LAYER 1: Event Classification — Signal or Noise?

The first decision is the most underrated. Most geopolitical events are **noise** — events that produce narrative content but no durable investment signal. The framework's first job is separating signal from noise.

**The four classification questions:**

**Q1: Is this structural or tactical?**
- *Structural:* The event changes the underlying power relationships, resource flows, or institutional architecture in a way that persists for years. Examples: The Russia-Ukraine war forcing European energy independence; China's BRI creating new trade corridors; the US One Big Beautiful Bill locking in $2T annual deficits.
- *Tactical:* The event is a move within an existing structure — a negotiation outcome, a temporary supply disruption, a political statement. It creates volatility but does not change the underlying regime.

**Rule:** Only structural events justify new long-horizon positions. Tactical events may justify short-term hedges, but entering a 12–18 month position on a tactical event is how investors get trapped.

**Q2: Is this new information or recategorized information?**
- *New information:* The event reveals something the market did not know. Price adjustment is likely.
- *Recategorized information:* The event confirms something the market knew but had not yet priced. The adjustment may be partially complete.
- *Noise:* The event is widely known and fully priced. No adjustment forthcoming.

**Rule:** The best entries come from *recategorized* events — where the market has the information but is mis-filing it. The Hormuz crisis became investable not when it started (new information) but when the market's initial reaction proved incomplete (recategorization of "temporary disruption" to "structural toll architecture").

**Q3: Does this have a clear asset class footprint?**
- Some geopolitical events have obvious transmission paths (conflict near oil infrastructure → crude price). Some have ambiguous paths (political leadership change → uncertain policy direction). Some have no near-term market path at all (decades-long demographic trends).
- Without a clear transmission path, there is no trade — only a research note.

**Q4: What is the time horizon of the effect?**
- Under 90 days: Tactical volatility event. Can be traded, but not our framework's focus.
- 6–18 months: Our primary window. Structural events with medium-term resolution.
- 3–10 years: Long-cycle structural bets. Require the largest conviction and tolerance for interim volatility.

**Classification Output:** After Layer 1, the event is categorized as:
- `SIGNAL-STRUCTURAL` (proceed to Layer 2)
- `SIGNAL-TACTICAL` (consider short-term hedge only)
- `NOISE` (file and monitor; no position)

### LAYER 3: Transmission Mechanism — What Is the Exact Path from Event to Asset?

This is the layer most investors skip, and skipping it produces the narrative-action gap. It is not enough to say "conflict in the Middle East is bullish for oil." You must specify:

1. **What is the specific supply/demand/flow disruption?**
2. **Which market measures it most directly?**
3. **What is the lag between the geopolitical event and the market effect?**
4. **What other factors affect the same asset that could confound the signal?**
5. **What is the early observable that confirms the mechanism is operating?**

**The Transmission Mechanism Template:**

```
GEOPOLITICAL EVENT
       ↓
[Specific disruption to: supply / demand / trade flow / capital flow / 
 policy / sentiment]
       ↓
[Transmission path: physical market / currency / credit / equity / 
 derivatives / sentiment]
       ↓
[Lag: immediate (0–30 days) / short (1–3 months) / medium (3–12 months)]
       ↓
[Primary observable: tanker rates / yield spread / FX move / volume data]
       ↓
[Confirmation signal: the data point that tells you the mechanism is active]
       ↓
ASSET PRICE ADJUSTMENT
```

**Hormuz Case — Full Mechanism:**
- Event: Iranian interdiction of tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz
- Disruption: Physical supply reduction (~18M bbl/day transit chokepoint partially blocked)
- Transmission: Physical crude market → spot price → energy equity earnings expectations → shipping rates (Suezmax, VLCC)
- Lag: Immediate on spot, 30–60 days on equity earnings revision, 60–90 days on physical supply substitution
- Primary observable: AIS tanker traffic through Hormuz channel; Lloyd's insurance premiums on Hormuz-transiting vessels
- Confirmation signal: Tanker rates and AIS traffic diverged from spot price normalization → mechanism still operating even as political narrative shifted to "resolution"
- Asset price adjustment: Brent crude, XLE (energy equities), VLCC shipping rates, Middle East risk premium in gold

**Rule:** If you cannot specify the transmission mechanism clearly enough to identify a "confirmation observable," you do not have an investable thesis — you have an opinion. Opinions are held. Theses are tested.

### LAYER 4: Scenario Matrix — Three Scenarios, Explicit Probabilities

Layer 3 gives you the mechanism. Layer 4 forces you to build *three scenarios* — not one — and assign explicit probabilities. This is the layer that prevents over-conviction.

**The Three-Scenario Rule:**

Every geopolitical investment decision must include:
1. **The Base Case** — The most likely outcome, with probability assigned
2. **The Upside Case** — The scenario where the thesis plays out faster/stronger than expected
3. **The Tail Risk Case** — The scenario where the thesis is wrong in a way that creates loss

No base case probability should exceed 60% for a geopolitical event. If you are assigning 75%+ probability to one outcome in a live geopolitical event, you are almost certainly overconfident.

**The Scenario Matrix Template:**

| Scenario | Description | Probability | Asset Impact | Your Position Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base Case | [Most likely outcome] | [40–60%] | [Direction, magnitude] | [P&L direction] |
| Upside Case | [Thesis plays out strongly] | [20–35%] | [Stronger move in thesis direction] | [Larger gain] |
| Tail Risk | [Thesis is wrong] | [10–25%] | [Adverse move] | [Loss quantified] |

**Rule:** The position is only worth taking if:
`(P_base × P&L_base) + (P_upside × P&L_upside) + (P_tail × P&L_tail) > 0`

This is the expected value calculation. It must be positive on an expected value basis AND the tail risk loss must be survivable (i.e., not a position size that impairs the portfolio if the tail occurs).

**Hormuz Scenario Matrix (Applied, June 2026):**

| Scenario | Description | Probability | Brent Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base: Structural toll | Iran sustains partial interdiction; insurance/routing costs create persistent $8–12/bbl premium | 50% | +$8–12/bbl, sustained |
| Upside: Full blockade | Full interdiction event; emergency release of SPRs insufficient | 25% | +$20–35/bbl spike |
| Tail: Full resolution | US-Iran deal removes risk entirely; supply normalizes | 25% | -$5–8/bbl from current |

Expected value at $75/bbl entry: `(0.50 × +$10) + (0.25 × +$25) + (0.25 × -$6) = $5 + $6.25 - $1.50 = **+$9.75/bbl expected gain**`. Position was justified.

### LAYER 5: Position Architecture — Size, Instrument, Exit

Layer 4 gives you the expected value. Layer 5 answers: *given positive expected value, how do I build the position?*

**Five position architecture decisions:**

**Decision 1: Which instrument?**

Not all instruments transmit the thesis equally:

| Thesis | Direct Instrument | Leveraged Instrument | Indirect Exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crude oil supply shock | USO / front-month futures | Calendar spread (backwardation play) | XLE, XOP |
| USD weakness | DXY puts / EUR long | EM FX basket | Gold, commodities |
| Defense spending surge | RTX, LMT, BAES.L | ITA (defense ETF) | European defense ETF |
| US fiscal dominance | Short TLT | 30-year yield futures | TIPS over nominals |
| Gold as reserve hedge | GLD, IAU | GDX (gold miners, leveraged) | Physical gold |

**Decision 2: What is the position size?**

The Kelly Criterion (simplified): Size = (Expected Value) / (Maximum Loss if wrong)

For geopolitical positions: Never risk more than 2–3% of portfolio on a single geopolitical thesis in its initial expression. This is not timidity — it is the acknowledgment that tail events in geopolitics can be severe and fast-moving.

**Decision 3: What is the time horizon?**

Set explicit entries with explicit expiry logic:
- *Structural thesis:* Minimum 12-month hold commitment before reviewing
- *Tactical hedge:* 30–90 day window with explicit exit trigger
- *Tail risk hedge:* 6-month rolling, sized as insurance (not return-seeking)

**Decision 4: What is the falsification trigger?**

A position without a falsification trigger is a belief, not a trade. The falsification trigger is the specific observable that tells you the thesis is wrong — not that the price moved against you, but that the *mechanism* failed.

Examples:
- *Hormuz bullish on crude:* Falsified by AIS traffic normalizing AND tanker insurance premiums returning to pre-crisis levels AND Iran making credible exit concessions
- *Short TLT on fiscal dominance:* Falsified by CBO revising the 10-year deficit trajectory materially downward AND term premium declining below 50bps on a sustained basis
- *Long gold on dollar debasement:* Falsified by Fed raising rates above inflation (positive real rates) AND foreign central bank gold purchases reversing

**Decision 5: How does this position interact with existing portfolio?**

Geopolitical positions are rarely isolated — they interact:
- Long energy (Hormuz) + Long gold (dollar weakness) = Correlated, double-weighted on risk-off
- Short TLT (fiscal dominance) + Long gold (dollar debasement) = Aligned thesis, complementary assets
- Long defense equities + Short EM FX = Conflict premium simultaneously long and short risk

Map every new position against existing positions for correlation before sizing.


**Why the falsification trigger matters most:**

Most investors define "being wrong" as "price moved against me." This is the wrong definition. Price volatility is normal — especially in geopolitical theses, where the market frequently moves against the thesis before confirming it. The falsification trigger forces you to define wrong as "the underlying mechanism failed" — not "the price hurt me for 30 days."

The 1994 Clinton bond vigilante episode: if you had shorted bonds in early 1994 because the fiscal signal was clear, the price would have moved against you in Q1 before violently confirming in Q2–Q4. A price-based stop would have taken you out before the trade worked. A mechanism-based falsification trigger (deficit reduction bill passes with significant fiscal consolidation) would have kept you in.
