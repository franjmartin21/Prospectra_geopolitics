## I. The Foundational Mistake — Cost-Plus Pricing for Value Delivery

The most common pricing error in early-stage research firms is structural: they price based on their costs.

The reasoning feels sound. Server costs, time, infrastructure, analytical labor — add them up, apply a margin, and that's the price. Clean. Defensible. Safe.

It is also completely divorced from how institutional buyers evaluate price.

### Why Cost-Plus Fails Institutional Buyers

A $20B sovereign wealth fund's alternatives team does not care what your servers cost. They care about one thing: **what is the value of the information advantage this product provides, relative to what we would spend to produce a comparable capability internally?**

If the GRI framework allows a portfolio manager to make one well-timed allocation decision per quarter — one avoided drawdown, one sector rotation that captures a geopolitical regime shift before it appears in consensus — the value of that decision, at institutional scale, is measured in basis points on billions. A 10-basis-point improvement on a $2B portfolio is $2M in annual value.

Against that benchmark, a $60,000 annual subscription is not expensive. It is trivially cheap. And a firm that prices at $60,000 because that's what their costs-plus-margin math produces has left enormous value on the table — and simultaneously signaled to the buyer that they don't understand the value they're providing.

Institutional buyers interpret low prices as a signal of low confidence. This is not intuitive for founders who fear losing deals on price. But it is how institutional procurement actually works.

### The Three Pricing Inputs That Matter

A defensible institutional research pricing architecture rests on three inputs:

1. **Value delivered to the buyer** — what decisions does this enable, at what scale, with what expected improvement over the counterfactual?
2. **Market reference prices** — what do comparable research products (GPRI, Oxford Analytica, Eurasia Group, Rhodium Group) actually charge, and where is Prospectra positioned relative to those benchmarks?
3. **Buyer segmentation** — different institutions have radically different ability and willingness to pay, and a single price destroys value by simultaneously overcharging smaller buyers and undercharging larger ones.

Cost is not in this list. Cost is a floor, not a pricing input.

## II. The Research Pricing Landscape — What the Market Actually Charges

Before designing the Prospectra pricing architecture, you need to know the market. Here is what the institutional geopolitical research market actually looks like as of mid-2026:

### Tier 1 — Global Incumbents (Eurasia Group, Oxford Analytica, Control Risks)

**Annual subscription price:** $50,000–$250,000 per institution, depending on access level and organization size.

**What you get:** Daily research portal access, analyst briefings, ad-hoc inquiry access, scenario modeling, multi-country coverage, established brand with decades of track record.

**Positioning:** Trusted incumbents. Priced on organizational budgets, not value delivered. The institutional default choice — which means they win by inertia, not by analytical edge.

**What Prospectra is NOT:** A full-service multi-country political risk consultancy. We are a quantitative, data-driven GRI framework — a different analytical category, not a like-for-like competitor.

### Tier 2 — Specialist Research Boutiques (Rhodium Group, CSIS, Stratfor)

**Annual subscription price:** $15,000–$60,000 per institution.

**What you get:** Specialized thematic coverage, sector-specific analysis, data exports, research reports. Narrower scope than Tier 1. Deeper on specific themes.

**Positioning:** Supplement to incumbent providers, not a replacement. Often purchased alongside Eurasia Group/Oxford Analytica. The buyer adds them for a specific analytical gap.

**What Prospectra is:** Closer to this tier in product scope, but differentiated by the quantitative GRI scoring framework, Databricks-powered near-real-time signals, and investment-grade translation of geopolitical risk into actionable asset class views.

### Tier 3 — Data and Signal Products (GDELT-based, Bloomberg BRIEF feeds, Geopolitical Risk Indices)

**Annual subscription price:** $5,000–$25,000.

**What you get:** Data feeds, indices, quantitative scores. Little to no interpretive analysis. Raw material for in-house analysts.

**Positioning:** Infrastructure product, not a research product. Buyers are quant teams who build their own models.

**Where Prospectra should NOT be priced:** This tier. Pricing here signals you are a data vendor, not a research firm. It commoditizes the analytical layer and destroys the pricing power of the interpretive work.

### The Prospectra Positioning Gap

Prospectra sits between Tier 2 and the Tier 3 space — but should price anchored to Tier 2 or above. The GRI framework is not raw data. It is not the Rhodium Group. It is a differentiated, quantitative analytical product with a defensible methodology, a growing track record, and an investment-grade output orientation that most Tier 2 competitors lack.

**Target anchor price:** $25,000–$75,000 per institution per year, depending on tier, with strategic flexibility to reach $100,000+ for large institutions with full access and quarterly analyst briefings.

## III. The Three-Tier Architecture — Designing the Prospectra Product Stack

A single price point is a pricing architecture failure. Institutional buyers have radically different budgets, use cases, and organizational structures. A single price either undercharges the large buyers who could afford more or prices out the smaller buyers who become your first reference clients.

The right architecture is a three-tier stack with clear differentiation between tiers.

### Tier 1 — Prospectra Signal: $18,000/year

**What's included:**
- Weekly GRI Report — full country scoring, regime shift analysis, signal flags
- Monthly Investment Implications Summary — directional views on equities, commodities, currencies, and sovereign bonds
- Portal access to historical GRI archive (3-year lookback)
- Email delivery, no analyst access

**Who it's for:** Boutique asset managers, family offices with limited research budgets, financial advisors building a geopolitical research capability, corporate strategy teams at companies with $500M–$2B in revenue.

**Why this price:** Low enough to remove budget approval friction at smaller institutions. High enough to signal serious institutional research, not a newsletter. The $18,000 price point often sits below a single departmental discretionary spending authority, meaning a portfolio manager can subscribe without a formal procurement process.

**The strategic purpose of this tier:** Reference client generation. The Signal tier is how Prospectra builds the client roster that makes Tier 2 and Tier 3 sales possible. Every Signal client is a reference call for a larger institution. The price is set to maximize adoption, not margin.

### Tier 2 — Prospectra Intelligence: $48,000/year

**What's included:**
- Everything in Signal, plus:
- Biweekly Deep Dive Reports — extended analysis on specific geopolitical themes or country/region situations
- Quarterly Analyst Briefing — 60-minute live session with the CEO / lead analyst, dedicated to the client's portfolio and how the GRI signals apply to their specific positions
- Priority Inquiry Response — the client can submit up to 4 specific analytical questions per year for dedicated research responses (48-hour turnaround)
- Databricks-generated signal alerts — push notifications when GRI scores cross defined thresholds for countries/sectors relevant to the client's portfolio

**Who it's for:** Family offices with $500M–$5B AUM, regional banks with active investment arms, medium-sized endowments and foundations, corporate treasury teams with significant emerging market exposure.

**Why this price:** The $48,000 price point typically requires a light budget approval process but not a formal RFP. It sits in the range most institutions treat as a "research budget" rather than a "strategic technology investment." The quarterly briefings add personalization that justifies the premium over Signal.

**The strategic purpose of this tier:** The revenue engine. If Prospectra reaches 20 Intelligence clients, that is $960,000 in ARR — a business. The tier is designed to be the largest revenue contributor over years 2–4.

### Tier 3 — Prospectra Partnership: $90,000/year+

**What's included:**
- Everything in Intelligence, plus:
- Monthly Analyst Briefing — 12 sessions per year, fully customized to the client's portfolio and risk framework
- Portfolio-Specific GRI Integration — the CEO works with the client's risk team to integrate the GRI framework into their internal risk scoring process, with a custom country/sector watchlist
- Exclusive Early Release — Partnership clients receive research outputs 48 hours before Signal and Intelligence distribution
- Annual Strategy Session — full-day off-site (or virtual) session reviewing the prior year's GRI performance and the investment thesis for the coming year
- Co-research option — Partnership clients can commission one bespoke research project per year (a deep dive on a specific country, sector, or geopolitical scenario) delivered as a proprietary document (not distributed to other clients)

**Who it's for:** Sovereign wealth funds, large pension funds, global macro hedge funds, large family offices ($5B+ AUM), multinational corporations with strategic geopolitical exposure in their capital allocation.

**Why this price:** The $90,000+ price point enters formal procurement territory but is well below the threshold that triggers extended institutional approval cycles at most targets. The co-research option and portfolio integration justify the premium — the client is not just buying research; they are buying analytical capacity that effectively functions as an outsourced geopolitical research function.

**The strategic purpose of this tier:** Anchor client generation, premium brand positioning, and the source of case studies. One Partnership client generating $90,000/year provides more than research revenue — they provide the proof-of-concept that the product works at institutional scale, which opens every subsequent sale to comparable institutions.

## IV. The Anchoring Dynamic — How the Three-Tier Structure Changes Each Conversation

The most important function of the three-tier architecture is not the tiers themselves. It is what the tiers do to buyer psychology.

When a prospect asks "what does this cost?" and you have only one price, you are asking them to evaluate a binary decision: this price, or no purchase. Every price feels expensive in a binary evaluation.

When a prospect asks "what does this cost?" and you present three tiers, you have created an anchoring dynamic. The Partnership tier ($90,000) anchors the conversation. The Intelligence tier ($48,000) looks moderate by comparison. The Signal tier ($18,000) looks inexpensive.

You have also created a self-qualification mechanism. The prospect reveals their budget, their use case, and their organizational structure by which tier they gravitate toward. A prospect who immediately asks "what's included in the Partnership tier?" is telling you they have budget and they're serious. A prospect who says "we'd probably start with Signal and see how it goes" is telling you they are a reference client prospect, not a revenue client prospect — at least initially.

The three-tier structure also enables the most powerful sales technique in institutional research: the strategic downgrade.

If a prospect balks at Intelligence ($48,000), you do not negotiate. You offer Signal ($18,000) with an explicit expectation: "Most clients who start with Signal upgrade to Intelligence within 12–18 months after the first quarterly review. We can structure a Signal subscription with a pre-negotiated Intelligence upgrade path so the price discovery is already done when you're ready."

This approach converts a lost deal at $48,000 into a won deal at $18,000 with a pipeline upgrade. It maintains price integrity on the Intelligence tier (you never discounted it) while capturing the client relationship.

## V. The Annual Price Review — Building Pricing Power Over Time

Pricing architecture is not static. Early-stage research firms make a critical mistake: they set prices and then hold them constant, fearing client attrition. This is an error on two dimensions.

First, holding prices constant as your track record grows is implicit discounting. If the GRI framework was worth $48,000/year in 2026 when you had 12 months of track record, it is worth substantially more in 2027 when you have 24 months of track record, three published case studies, and a methodology document that has been cited by two institutional research teams. Flat pricing ignores this value accrual.

Second, institutional clients expect price increases. An annual CPI-linked increase (2–4%) is expected and does not generate friction. A value-linked increase (5–10%, justified by expanded coverage, new analytical capabilities, or the track record document) generates a conversation but rarely generates attrition from clients who are genuinely using the product.

**The Prospectra Annual Price Review Protocol:**

1. Every November, audit the pricing architecture against the prior year's track record, expanded capabilities, and competitive landscape.
2. Announce price increases for the following year's new subscriptions by December 1.
3. Offer existing clients a "lock rate" — they can renew at the current year's price if they sign the renewal by December 31. This creates urgency for renewals, rewards loyalty, and maintains price integrity for new clients.
4. Never negotiate on price within a tier. If a client cannot afford a tier, offer the tier below, not a discount on the tier they want. Price discounting destroys the integrity of the entire architecture.
