### 4. VLCC Rate Update — Any Compression Below $400K/day?

**Answer: YES — significant rate compression is underway. Rates have moved well below $400K/day.**

This is the most important new data point in today's session.

Per available market reporting as of July 3, 2026:
- **VLCC Middle East–China route (TD3C): approximately $287,000/day**, compared with peaks above $500,000 earlier in the crisis and the $420,000–$470,000 range cited in L143 (early July fixtures).
- **The compression is approximately 35–40% from the $470K July 1 peak in a matter of days.**

The Marinelink headline from this week — "Is Hormuz Half-Open or Half-Closed? Tanker Rates on the Mend" — captures the market's current framing.

**What is driving the rate compression?**

Three concurrent developments have combined to reduce VLCC spot rates faster than expected:

**A. The Southern Corridor Is Generating Commercial Trust Faster Than Expected**

The JMIC corridor (announced June 27) was described in L143 as "less than a week old" with behavioral lag slowing adoption. In the six days since, it appears commercial operators have begun using it more actively. The combination of US military presence, AIS transponders on, and no reported incidents in the corridor has reduced the effective risk premium operators are pricing. Fewer operators requiring the Iran-permit-system northern route means more total vessel capacity available — and rate competition increases.

**B. The Doha Talks "Positive Progress" Signal**

The Doha talks' "positive progress" language directly affects forward rate expectations. If a durable Hormuz agreement is possible after July 9 (post-funeral), operators who need to fix vessels for August loadings are now pricing in some probability of normalized rates by August. The July/August fixture spread is beginning to invert — operators are paying crisis premiums for July but not for August, reflecting this probability.

**C. The Iran Sanctions Waiver — More Oil, Less Rate Leverage**

With the sanctions waiver allowing Iranian crude back into the market, Iranian-licensed vessels (which have always operated through the northern route) are now carrying more crude. This adds supply to the tanker market from vessels that were not competing for the premium market. Iranian VLCCs now exporting to China via the northern corridor displace some demand for the war-premium Western vessels using the southern corridor.

### The Kill Condition Reassessment

The updated tanker kill condition from L141:
- **UKMTO threat-level downgrade to "moderate" or below** — currently "substantial," JMIC: CRITICAL. **NOT MET.**
- **Hormuz traffic recovery to ≥50% pre-war baseline (~65+ vessels/day)** — still approximately 24 all-type vessels/day per last available data. **NOT MET.**

**However, rates have already compressed 35–40% from their peak without either kill condition formally firing.** This creates an important distinction:

> **The kill condition is designed to protect against exiting the position too early on a false rate spike. It was not designed to hold a position through rate normalization.** If rates compress from $470K to $287K to $150K over the next three weeks — without the UKMTO ever formally downgrading or the vessel count ever cleanly reaching 65/day — the thesis has been operationally falsified even if the formal kill trigger never fires.

**CEO Judgment on Tanker Position**: The rate compression signals the tanker thesis is entering its **terminal phase**. Rates at $287K are still extraordinary by historical standards (pre-crisis rates ~$50–80K/day), but the compression trajectory is now the leading risk indicator. The relevant question is no longer "when does the kill condition fire?" but "at what rate does the position's risk/reward invert?"

**Provisional rate-based kill condition overlay**: If rates compress below **$200,000/day** for Hormuz fixtures, the remaining premium is small enough that the position's tail-risk exposure (a sudden resumed attack driving rates back to $400K+) no longer compensates for the expected downside of continued rate normalization. The $200K level functions as a discretionary overlay to the formal kill condition.

**Updated tanker position: HOLD — but with active monitoring and a new soft trigger at $200K/day. The clock on the tanker thesis is running faster than the UKMTO's formal threat-level clock.**

### 6. Gold and TIPS — Direction Post-Warsh Statement

**Answer: Gold held above $4,100 on weaker NFP; TIPS story is more complex than L143 framed it.**

**The NFP surprise**: June non-farm payrolls came in below consensus expectations. The weaker jobs number:
- Reduced the perceived urgency of additional rate hikes in the near-term
- Supported gold (lower employment → lower rate expectations → lower real yields)
- Drove Treasuries higher in price (yields lower) during Thursday's session

**However, the picture is more complex than it appeared in L143:**

The market-implied probability of a December 2026 Fed rate hike is now approximately **79–88%** — significantly higher than L143's framing suggested. The contradiction between "Warsh signals no urgency" (gold-bullish) and "market prices 79–88% December rate hike" (gold-bearish) needs resolution.

Here is the resolution:

| Signal | Interpretation | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Warsh "no urgency" comment (July 1) | Fed not hiking immediately; September/October hike off the table | Short-term (2–3 months) |
| 79–88% December hike probability | Market believes the Fed resumes hiking by year-end if inflation reaccelerates | Medium-term (5–6 months) |
| Weaker June NFP | Slows rate path, supports the "no urgency" framing | Immediate |

**The critical insight**: These signals are not contradictory — they are sequential. The market is pricing:
- No hike now (July/September) → gold-supportive in near term
- Likely hike by December (if inflation rebounds) → ceiling on gold's further upside in H2

This is a **conditional rate path**, not a flat rate forecast. Gold at $4,100+ is the market's equilibrium for the near-term no-hike regime. If December rate hike expectations firm further (e.g., August CPI comes in hot), gold faces a ceiling.

**TIPS Yield**: The 10-year TIPS real yield remains well below the 3% kill trigger. With nominal 10-year yields around 4.46% and inflation breakevens at approximately 2.1–2.2%, TIPS real yield is approximately **2.25–2.35%** — still ~65–75 basis points below the 3% kill threshold. Even the December rate hike scenario (if it materializes) would push TIPS yields to perhaps 2.6–2.8% — still below 3%.

**Gold kill switch assessment: STILL FAR FROM FIRING.** The December rate hike risk creates a soft ceiling, not a kill trigger. The thesis remains intact. But the gold narrative has added a new term: it is now a function of both geopolitical uncertainty AND a conditional inflation/rate path. Monitor August CPI and September Fed meeting signals.

## Reflection Questions

1. The tanker kill condition was built around UKMTO threat-level and vessel count thresholds. Rates have now compressed 35–40% from their peak without either threshold firing. This is a case of the kill condition lagging market reality. In general, how should a rules-based investor handle situations where market-implied evidence suggests thesis falsification faster than formal kill conditions can register it? What is the cost of waiting for the formal trigger vs. acting on the market signal? And how do you design future kill conditions to be less laggy?

2. The Khamenei funeral creates a structural 6-day diplomatic freeze. This is analogous to the "earnings quiet period" in equity investing — a period where the underlying dynamics don't change but external reporting is suppressed. From an investment framework perspective, should you treat the funeral pause as dead time (pause the clock, pick up after July 9), or as active time (new information is being generated about how both sides behave under the pause — does it hold? what does holding tell you about the IRGC leg's durability?)? What does your framework do with structural pauses?

3. The P01 kill switch requires all three legs simultaneously. As of today, the price leg is 2-of-3 with near-certainty of completing July 10 — but Lebanon and IRGC have a conjunctive probability of satisfying by July 31 of roughly 20–35%. This means the kill switch may never fire even though the price basis for the position (Hormuz supply disruption) is 75% healed. The multi-leg design protects against premature exits, but does it create a risk of premature *holds*? In other words: is there a version of the kill switch being too conservative? How would you know if you were stuck holding a dead thesis because the kill conditions were too demanding?
