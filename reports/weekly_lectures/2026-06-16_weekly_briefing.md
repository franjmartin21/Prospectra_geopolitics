## EVENT 1 — IRAN DEAL APPROACHING: HORMUZ REPRICING IN PROGRESS

### What Happened
On June 9, oil prices fell ~6% on the week after the U.S. Energy Secretary stated that Hormuz ship traffic is increasing and a senior administration official put the probability of a formal deal at 80%. Trump opened the G7 Évian summit on June 15 declaring the Iran deal "largely done." As of June 16, Brent crude is trading near $87–91/barrel — down from $108 in early May but still 20%+ above pre-war levels (the U.S.-Israeli strike on Iran began February 28).

### Geopolitical Significance
The Strait of Hormuz handles ~20% of global oil trade. Its disruption since late February triggered the largest oil supply shock in recorded history. The deal reportedly involves reopening of the strait, dismantling of Iran's nuclear program, removal of the naval blockade, and economic incentives for Tehran. If signed, it ends the acute phase of the crisis — but not the structural reconfiguration of Gulf energy geopolitics. The Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait) spent three months scrambling to route supply around Iran; those logistics changes and security anxieties do not disappear with a signed agreement.

### Investment Implications
- **Crude oil (SHORT thesis under construction):** Brent at $87–91 already reflects significant deal probability. A signed agreement could push Brent back toward $70–75 (pre-war range). But two risks remain: Iran deal collapses in negotiations, and/or Saudi Arabia uses the supply normalization window to cut output and defend price. **Directional view: cautious near-term long on crude as deal risk lingers; medium-term bearish if deal closes and Hormuz reopens fully.**
- **Energy equities:** Integrated majors (XOM, CVX, Shell) benefited from high oil; a deal reprices them lower. Refiners get mixed signals — lower crude input cost but reduced crack spread premium. **Hold energy positions; do not add at current spread vs. pre-war.**
- **Defense:** No direct change; U.S.-Israel operations continue even if Hormuz deal closes. Defense spending across NATO and the Gulf remains elevated.
- **Shipping/Tankers:** A Hormuz reopening is structurally negative for tanker rates, which spiked on rerouting. Watch VLCC day-rates as a leading indicator of deal probability.
- **Gold:** Partial safe-haven unwind likely if deal is confirmed. Gold may pull back $50–80 from current levels.

## EVENT 2 — G7 ÉVIAN SUMMIT: OPEN FRACTURES ON RARE EARTHS AND TARIFFS

### What Happened
The 52nd G7 summit opened June 15 at Évian-les-Bains, France (runs through June 17). The opening day produced an immediate fracture: the Trump administration **rejected France's proposal for a permanent Critical Minerals Secretariat**, pushing instead for bilateral deals. China's rare earth export curbs — still in force — have cost each G7 economy an estimated $1.5 trillion in supply chain disruption costs. A **Section 122 tariff cliff hits July 24**, six weeks from now, when the 150-day window on 15% surcharges expires, creating urgent pressure on U.S.-European trade diplomacy. No final communiqué has been issued as of June 16.

### Geopolitical Significance
The Évian summit reveals that the G7 coalition — already under strain from Trump's 2025 tariff regime — is now splitting on the precise question of *how* to respond to China's economic warfare. France and the EU want multilateral coordination; the U.S. prefers leverage through bilateral deals. This is not a tactical disagreement — it reflects fundamentally different theories of the China challenge. A failure to issue a unified rare-earths communiqué would signal to Beijing that its export-restriction playbook is working: disrupting G7 coordination is itself a strategic objective.

### Investment Implications
- **Critical minerals (LONG, high conviction):** Neodymium, dysprosium, terbium, and related REE prices remain elevated. Western domestic production assets — MP Materials (USA), Lynas (Australia) — benefit regardless of whether the G7 agrees on a secretariat. **This is a structural multi-year long.**
- **European industrial equities (cautious):** The July 24 tariff cliff is a binary event. If a U.S.-EU trade arrangement is not reached before that date, U.S. tariffs on European goods snap back from framework agreement levels. German auto, French luxury, Italian manufacturing would all face meaningful earnings headwinds.
- **USD vs EUR:** A tariff cliff hit is USD-positive (risk-off), EUR-negative. Watch the July 24 date as a hard catalyst.
- **Defense / European rearmament:** G7 fractures accelerate European defense spending independence. Long BAE Systems, Rheinmetall, Thales — the thesis is intact regardless of summit outcome.
- **Semiconductors:** Rare earth export curbs feed directly into chip equipment supply chain. Long ASML (despite US-EU tension), long domestic U.S. fab capacity plays.

## EVENT 3 — SOUTH CHINA SEA: CHINA ESCALATES AGAINST TAIWAN'S ISLANDS

### What Happened
On June 11, a PRC law enforcement ship and a supply vessel briefly entered the **restricted and prohibited waters of Itu Aba (Taiping Island)** — Taiwan's largest South China Sea holding — for the first time in recorded history. Taiwan's Coast Guard Administration condemned it as a violation of sovereignty. This followed: (1) a CCG vessel circling Pratas/Dongsha twice while patrolling in Taiwan's restricted waters for 24+ hours (June 5–6), and (2) a coordinated CCG + PRC research vessel patrol around Pratas — also a first. AEI's June 12 China-Taiwan update flagged all three incidents as a meaningful escalation pattern.

### Geopolitical Significance
China is running a low-level, persistent escalation campaign against Taiwan's South China Sea positions. This is not random harassment — it is a deliberate, graduated pressure strategy that serves two purposes: (1) probe Taiwan's response thresholds without triggering U.S. military involvement, and (2) establish "facts on the water" around Taiwan's island holdings, creating administrative ambiguity that could be exploited in a future negotiation or confrontation. The Pratas/Dongsha atoll is particularly strategic: it sits at the northern gateway to the South China Sea and is lightly defended. This escalation comes **after** the Trump-Xi summit that was supposed to have stabilized cross-strait tension — signaling that Beijing interpreted the summit as buying space for continued pressure below the military threshold.

### Investment Implications
- **Taiwan semiconductor risk (not yet pricing):** TSMC and the broader semiconductor supply chain are priced as if Taiwan risk is contained post-summit. It is not. This is a slow-building tail risk that markets consistently underprice until a sudden repricing. **The right move is to hold TSMC (productive asset) but hedge with put spreads.**
- **ASEAN equities:** South China Sea incidents increase ASEAN hedging behavior — more U.S. defense partnerships, more trade diversification away from China. Long Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines manufacturing exposure.
- **Defense (Pacific):** Japanese, Australian, and South Korean defense spending accelerations are validated by this pattern. Mitsubishi Heavy, Kawasaki, Hanwha — long-term structural longs.
- **China equities:** Short-term, markets may not react. Medium-term, each escalation increases probability of Western capital restrictions on Chinese equities. **Reduce China A-share exposure on any significant rally.**

## EVENT 4 — HOUSE DEFIES TRUMP: UKRAINE AID + RUSSIA SANCTIONS BILL PASSES

### What Happened
On June 4, the U.S. House of Representatives passed a major Ukraine aid bill, with 18 Republicans breaking ranks to defy President Trump and GOP leadership. The bill includes billions in military support and new sanctions targeting Russian oil exports and banking sectors. This followed earlier months where formal U.S.-mediated Ukraine-Russia talks stalled after the Iran war began in late February. Both sides observed partial ceasefires (Orthodox Easter in April, Victory Day in May) but continued accusing each other of violations.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a significant constitutional moment in U.S. foreign policy. A bipartisan coalition in the House has now overridden Trump's Ukraine posture twice (this is the latest in a pattern of GOP fractures on the issue). The practical effect: U.S. military and economic support for Ukraine continues even without executive branch enthusiasm, and the new Russia sanctions tighten the economic vise. For markets, the more important signal is what this says about **Trump's ability to deliver a Ukraine deal**: it is now clear that any negotiated settlement would face legislative resistance in the U.S., giving Ukraine diplomatic leverage it didn't have before.

### Investment Implications
- **Russian assets (avoid):** New banking and oil sanctions increase the isolation of Russian financial assets. Ruble remains under structural pressure. Russian sovereign debt is uninvestable for Western institutional investors.
- **European defense (LONG, high conviction):** The unpredictability of U.S. Ukraine commitment is now locked in as a permanent variable, regardless of this particular vote. European defense independence spending is a multi-year, multi-trillion structural trend. **This is the highest-conviction long in the portfolio.**
- **Reconstruction plays (early positioning):** Ukraine reconstruction will eventually be a massive economic event. Polish, Romanian, and German construction/industrial firms are the likely beneficiaries. Too early to size this position; monitor.
- **Energy (Ukraine-relevant):** LNG demand from Europe remains elevated as Russia-European energy reconnection is now politically toxic. Long U.S. LNG exporters (Cheniere, Venture Global) on any dip.
- **Ukrainian hryvnia / sovereign bonds:** Still high-risk, but with U.S. legislative support locked in, sovereign default probability decreases. Not a position to take at scale; worth monitoring for the macro signal.

## EVENT 5 — PERU ELECTION: LEFT LEADS IN RAZOR-THIN COPPER COUNTRY VOTE

### What Happened
In Peru's June 7 presidential runoff, leftist candidate Roberto Sánchez leads conservative Keiko Fujimori by fewer than 20,000 votes (50.055% vs. 49.945%) with 96% of ballots counted. The result remains uncertified. Sánchez is an ally of imprisoned ex-President Pedro Castillo; Fujimori is the daughter of disgraced former president Alberto Fujimori. Neither candidate cleared 20% in the April first round.

### Geopolitical Significance
Peru is the world's second-largest copper producer and one of the top producers of zinc, gold, and lithium. A Sánchez presidency follows a pattern of Latin American left-wing governments using mining royalties and nationalization threats as economic populism tools (see: Petro in Colombia, Boric in Chile). The Castillo precedent is particularly relevant: Castillo's 2021 election triggered massive copper equity selloffs before his proposals were neutralized by Congress and his own political implosion. The question is whether Sánchez has the political strength to actually move against the mining sector, or whether Peru's fragmented congress constrains him as it constrained Castillo.

### Investment Implications
- **Copper (complex, medium-term):** Copper prices have already pulled back from March highs on demand concerns and Hormuz-related freight disruptions. A Sánchez win adds a political risk premium. **Watch for Sánchez administration's first statements on mining royalties — that is the key signal.** Southern Copper (SCCO) and Freeport-McMoRan (FCX) would be most exposed.
- **Peru sovereign bonds:** A Sánchez win with a narrow mandate and fragmented congress is manageable for bondholders if Congress neutralizes radical mining proposals. Watch the congressional seat count.
- **PEN (Peruvian sol):** Expect near-term weakness; the sol will act as the immediate shock absorber on electoral uncertainty. Could be a medium-term entry on depreciation if congress neutralizes mining risk.
- **EM FX basket:** This vote adds to the "Latin American political risk" premium that has been building through Colombia, Chile, Bolivia, and Mexico over the past 24 months. **EM equity exposure should tilt toward Asia over Latin America in the current cycle.**

## CEO PORTFOLIO NOTE

The single most important portfolio decision this week is **not** to celebrate the approaching Hormuz deal as a full-risk-off unwind. Here is the logic:

The Hormuz shock compressed four structural trends into one acute moment: (1) oil supply disruption pricing, (2) defense spending acceleration, (3) dollar safe-haven demand, and (4) critical minerals urgency. A deal resolves trend #1 partially. Trends #2, #3, and #4 are structural and accelerating.
