## EVENT 1 — THE LAKE LUCERNE ROADMAP: THE WAR ENTERS ITS ENDGAME, NOT ITS END

### What Happened
After talks that ran past midnight and were briefly disrupted by a Trump threat mid-session, U.S. and Iranian negotiators (Vance, Kushner, and Witkoff for Washington; Foreign Minister Araghchi leading Tehran's delegation; Qatar and Pakistan mediating) concluded marathon talks at Bürgenstock, Switzerland on June 21–22 with a **"roadmap" to a final deal within 60 days** — not a final deal itself. Concrete elements: a new deconfliction communication mechanism for the Strait of Hormuz "to avoid incidents," a framework aimed at ending Israeli military operations in Lebanon (mediators called this the "first real test" of the agreement), a committee on nuclear monitoring and sanctions, and Iranian claims of oil/petrochemical export waivers, lifted port blockades, partial frozen-asset releases, and a reconstruction-plan discussion. Qatar and Pakistan described the atmosphere as "positive and constructive"; U.S. officials called it "constructive but tense." Brent fell 2.1% Monday to ~$77–78/barrel — the sharpest single-day move since May's deal optimism — while equities rallied broadly (S&P +0.5%, Dow +0.9%). Tanker equities (FRO, DHT, STNG) simultaneously **rose** 3%+, a genuine divergence from the oil move, driven by Frontline's record $1.55 ex-dividend and by reopening dynamics expanding fleet-addressable volume rather than shrinking it.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is real progress — the most structured framework the talks have produced since the war began February 28. But it is explicitly a roadmap, not the ceasefire/IRGC-standdown/strait-normalization trifecta the project's own kill-switch criteria require, and Lesson 131 (June 21) documented in granular detail how the *previous* declared step forward — the June 17 Islamabad MOU — collapsed within 72 hours specifically over the unresolved Lebanon track. The new deconfliction mechanism is, by the mediators' own description, an unproven "first test." Until Lebanon holds through one full uninterrupted news cycle, the base rate this month says "roadmap" announcements are necessary but not sufficient.

### Investment Implications
- **Crude oil (HOLD, no new action):** Brent's intraday move to $77–78 is the closest single print yet to the Energy position's kill-switch line (three *consecutive weekly closes* below $80, plus formal Lebanon ceasefire and IRGC stand-down — none of the three conditions is fully met). **Directional view: do not chase the spot decline; the relevant checkpoint is Friday's weekly close, not Monday's print.** A genuine break and hold below $80 for three weeks would be the first real trigger this thesis has seen since April.
- **Tankers (HOLD, collect dividend):** The oil-down/tanker-up divergence is exactly what Lesson 131's "multi-quarter structural position" reframe anticipated — rerouting normalization and dividend mechanics decouple tanker equity performance from spot crude direction in the near term. No exit case until a confirmed multi-week transit trend holds alongside Lebanon/IRGC conditions.
- **Gold:** Modest softening on de-escalation optimism is likely, but a pattern of declared deals reversing within 48–72 hours twice this month argues against chasing the unwind in size. **HOLD; do not reduce on this print alone.**
- **USD:** Roadmap progress is mildly dollar-negative near-term (risk-on), but the 60-day uncertain runway and the recent reversal history cap the size of the move. Not a tradable signal yet.

## EVENT 2 — G7 ÉVIAN CONCLUDES: AN ALLIANCE, NOT A TREATY

### What Happened
The G7 summit at Évian (June 15–17) closed without a unified joint communiqué for the **second consecutive year** under the Trump administration. Leaders did issue a declaration creating a non-binding **Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance**, targeting reliance on any single non-G7/partner supplier for rare earths and permanent magnets below 60% by 2030 (ambition: 50% "as soon as possible"), with coordination on financing, traceability, stockpiling, and recycling. The Trump administration confirmed it will not back France's proposed permanent Critical Minerals Secretariat, preferring bilateral supply deals — resolving last week's open fracture in the U.S.'s favor.

### Geopolitical Significance
The G7 chose coordination-without-commitment over a binding multilateral body — a structurally weaker response to Chinese rare-earth leverage than France wanted, but a real one. The 2030 target is the first quantified, dated G7-wide benchmark on critical-minerals dependence, even if non-binding. Two consecutive years without a unified communiqué is itself a data point: G7 cohesion is now the exception that needs explaining, not the default.

### Investment Implications
- **Critical minerals (LONG, high conviction, unchanged):** A quantified 2030 de-risking target — even non-binding — is a multi-year tailwind for Western domestic producers (MP Materials, Lynas) regardless of enforcement mechanism. No change to conviction or sizing.
- **European defense / industrial policy:** A weaker multilateral outcome modestly reinforces the case that Europe must build redundant capacity bilaterally and nationally rather than wait for G7 coordination — consistent with, not contradictory to, the existing European defense long.
- **No new action this week.** This is confirmation of an existing thesis, not a new signal.

## EVENT 3 — PERU FLIPS: FUJIMORI RETAKES THE LEAD AS FINAL BALLOTS COUNTED

### What Happened
With over 99% of ballots now tallied, Keiko Fujimori leads Roberto Sánchez by roughly 36,000 votes — a reversal of the ~20,000-vote Sánchez lead reported last week at 96% counted. The remaining contested ballots are concentrated in Fujimori's Lima stronghold. Sánchez has not conceded, alleges fraud, and is drawing an explicit comparison to Fujimori's own 2021 dispute of her loss. Formal JNE certification is expected by mid-to-late July.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a genuine reversal, not noise — it changes the base case from "narrow left-wing win with mining-royalty risk" (last week's framing) to "narrow market-friendly win contested by the left." The Castillo-2021 playbook this project has flagged twice now (left-wing mining populism neutralized by a fragmented congress) may not even be tested if Fujimori's lead holds through certification. The dispute itself — not the outcome — is now the source of near-term volatility.

### Investment Implications
- **Copper (LONG, reinforced):** A Fujimori win removes, for now, the single live political-risk catalyst this project had flagged against the broader structural copper long (COPX/SCCO/FCX). **Directional view: incrementally bullish for the existing position — no new entry warranted on this alone, but it strengthens the case for not hedging the position around Peru-specific risk.**
- **PEN (Peruvian sol):** Expect continued volatility through certification given Sánchez's fraud allegations; a clean Fujimori certification would likely support the sol. Not a position to take at scale.
- **EM LatAm vs. Asia tilt:** Modestly reduces (does not eliminate) the LatAm political-risk premium argument from prior briefings. Maintain the existing Asia-over-LatAm tilt pending certification; revisit if Sánchez's fraud claims gain institutional traction.

## EVENT 4 — SOUTH CHINA SEA: THE GRAY-ZONE CAMPAIGN AGAINST TAIWAN CONTINUES

### What Happened
The pattern flagged in last week's briefing has continued and compounded. China Coast Guard vessels have now entered Dongsha's (Pratas) restricted waters on six occasions in 2026; a June 13 incursion by two mainland law-enforcement vessels into Taiping Island's prohibited waters was, per Taipei, the first time mainland government vessels had entered that specific restricted zone. Near-daily PLA sorties continue across the Taiwan Strait median line. Analysts at the Global Taiwan Institute and AEI characterize this as a sustained escalation pattern dating from after the May 14–15 Trump-Xi summit in Beijing — not a series of isolated incidents.

### Geopolitical Significance
This remains the project's clearest "market underpricing a slow-building tail risk" case. The post-summit timing is the key analytical point: Beijing appears to be reading the Trump-Xi summit as having purchased room for continued sub-military pressure, not as a stabilization. Six incursions into the same restricted zone in under six months is no longer a probe — it is a sustained campaign to normalize PRC presence in Taiwan-administered waters.

### Investment Implications
- **Taiwan semiconductor risk (unchanged stance):** Markets continue to price Taiwan risk as contained. **Directional view: hold TSMC as a productive long-term asset, maintain put-spread hedges; do not let the absence of a market reaction this week be read as the absence of risk.**
- **Pacific defense (LONG, high conviction, unchanged):** Mitsubishi Heavy, Kawasaki, Hanwha — the structural case strengthens with each incident, independent of any single week's market reaction.
- **ASEAN diversification:** Continues to validate long Vietnam/Indonesia/Philippines manufacturing exposure as supply chains hedge against escalation risk.

## EVENT 5 — USMCA SIX-YEAR REVIEW: THE JULY 1 TRADE CLOCK STARTS

### What Happened
The USMCA's legally mandated joint six-year review window opens July 1, 2026. The Trump administration has signaled it intends to use the review to force renegotiation — not treat it as procedural — targeting automotive rules-of-origin content thresholds, stronger forced-labor import enforcement, and new restrictions on Chinese investment and content in North American supply chains. If all three parties agree to renew, USMCA continues for another 16 years; if not, the agreement does not expire immediately but falls into an annual-review limbo with elevated uncertainty, and any party could ultimately move toward withdrawal or bilateral arrangements. Canada and Mexico had expected a largely procedural review as recently as a few months ago; both have shifted posture as Washington's intentions became clear.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a new structural risk this project has not previously tracked, and it arrives on a similar binary-deadline footing to the Section 122 tariff cliff (July 24) already on the board. Unlike Section 122, USMCA's review does not expire the agreement outright on a missed deadline — but capital-intensive sectors (autos above all) plan years ahead, and "renegotiation overhang" is itself a tax on investment decisions regardless of the eventual outcome.

### Investment Implications
- **North American autos / nearshoring (NEW WATCH-ITEM, no position yet):** Mexican auto-parts and assembly exposure faces a renewed rules-of-origin overhang heading into July 1. This sits alongside — and is structurally distinct from — the existing European autos underweight; recommend tracking as a separate line rather than folding into the European thesis.
- **MXN / CAD:** Expect rising volatility into July 1 as renegotiation rhetoric intensifies. Not yet a position; a clean signal would be either side moving to invoke formal exit/annual-review provisions.
- **Action for Bolo:** Add a USMCA review tracker alongside the existing Section 122 calendar — two hard, dated trade catalysts now sit five weeks apart (July 1 and July 24).
