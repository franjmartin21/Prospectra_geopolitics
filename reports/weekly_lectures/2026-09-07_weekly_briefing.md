## EVENT 1: IRAN WAR — CEASEFIRE COLLAPSES, BRENT BACK ABOVE $92

### What Happened
The 60-day US-Iran ceasefire expired in mid-August. On September 1, the first exchanges of direct US-Iranian fire in over a month occurred, sending Brent crude up 2% to $92/barrel on Tuesday. Strait of Hormuz traffic remains partially suppressed — nowhere near pre-war levels. Treasury Secretary Bessent gave a notable interview (September 4) predicting oil falls to $40 once the war ends, pulling down bond yields. The market is increasingly pricing a "no war, no peace" equilibrium extending into 2027.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the defining structural disruption of 2026. Hormuz passage — 20%+ of global seaborne oil — is still constrained. Iran has no incentive to agree to a ceasefire that doesn't address regime survival guarantees. The US has no incentive to offer those guarantees before the September 24 Xi-Trump summit. The ceasefire collapse means the window for a diplomatic settlement has narrowed significantly. Besent's $40 call tells you the US fiscal scenario *requires* peace — fiscal pressure and high oil are incompatible with a soft landing.

## EVENT 2: XI-TRUMP SUMMIT COUNTDOWN — TARIFFS, OVERCAPACITY, SEPTEMBER 24

### What Happened
Xi Jinping attended the SCO Summit in Bishkek (August 31–September 1), then conducted state visits to Egypt and India — diplomatic positioning before his White House visit, currently scheduled for September 24, 2026. The US this week moved to impose an additional 7.5% tariff on Chinese goods for industrial "overcapacity" ahead of the summit, layering on top of the July 24 12.5% forced-labor tariff. Combined with Section 301 tariffs, effective rates on many Chinese product categories now reach 62.5–112.5%. China has maintained 35% retaliatory tariffs on US imports.

The September 24 summit is the most consequential US-China diplomatic event of 2026. Both sides want stability — China needs to arrest its economic deceleration; Trump wants a visible win and lower oil via Chinese pressure on Iran. Expected agenda: trade, AI, Taiwan, Iran. China was the only G20 dissenter on the external surplus language — a signal that it views overcapacity tariffs as illegitimate, but it won't torpedo the summit over them. Xi's pre-summit tour of Egypt, India, and SCO countries is soft power posturing: "I come to your summit with a full dance card."

Separately, a localized ceasefire took effect September 5 to allow IAEA-supervised repairs to the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant's off-site power connection. ## EVENT 4: SCO BISHKEK SUMMIT — MULTIPOLARITY CODIFIED, IRAN WAR CONDEMNED

### What Happened
The 26th SCO Summit met August 31–September 1 in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, marking the organization's 25th anniversary. Leaders signed 28 outcome documents covering security, AI, energy, climate, and nuclear security. The summit explicitly condemned US-Israeli military strikes on Iran as violations of international law and expressed condolences over Supreme Leader Khamenei's death. Putin declared the SCO an "influential centre of the emerging multipolar world." Modi, notably, addressed Putin directly on live television calling for an end to the Ukraine war — a rare public rebuke.

### Geopolitical Significance
The SCO is now functioning as the institutional backbone of the non-Western counter-alignment. The Iran condemnation was unanimous — China, Russia, India, Pakistan, and Central Asian states all signed on. This tells you three things: (1) the Global South framing of the Iran war as Western aggression is hardening; (2) China's pre-summit diplomatic tour successfully locked in the SCO position before September 24; (3) Modi's Ukraine rebuke of Putin was domestically directed — India needs to position for a post-war world while not alienating Russia as a defense supplier. The 28 documents included AI and energy cooperation — these are not symbolic; they represent infrastructure for a parallel digital and energy order.

## EVENT 5: WARSH FED — 66% SEPTEMBER HIKE PROBABILITY, WALLER DISSENTS

### What Happened
Following Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech (August 28), markets shifted significantly. As of this week, CME FedWatch shows a 66% probability of a 25bp rate hike at the September FOMC meeting (likely September 16–17). Warsh warned inflation "has yet to meaningfully slow" and the Fed will "deliver price stability." However, Governor Christopher Waller said September 3 that he sees "signs of disinflation" and leans toward holding — the most prominent internal dissent from the Warsh line since he became chair in May 2026. Current fed funds target: 3.50–3.75%.

### Geopolitical Significance
The Fed's September decision is as geopolitically loaded as it is monetary. A hike sends the dollar higher, tightens EM financial conditions, adds to the US fiscal cost (every 25bp costs ~$50B annually on rolling debt), and creates friction with Treasury Secretary Bessent who needs low yields to manage the debt load. The Waller dissent is significant — if the September data (CPI September 10, PPI September 11) shows deceleration, the hike could be pulled back. Warsh is playing a credibility game: he can't be seen backing down from his Jackson Hole framing without damaging Fed independence perception. The Bessent-Warsh tension is a live structural risk for markets.
