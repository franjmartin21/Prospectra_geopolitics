## EVENT 1 — HORMUZ WAR WIDENS TO A SECOND SOVEREIGN TARGET, THEN STANDS DOWN; TALKS MOVE TO DOHA

### What Happened
The full week's chronology: the Bürgenstock "roadmap" talks (covered in last week's briefing) formally concluded June 22–23, with Iran calling the technical session a success. Two days later (June 25), a container ship — not a tanker, not on the IMO's evacuation list — was struck off Oman's coast in an attack the US attributed to Iran; the IMO paused its own evacuation program in response even as commercial Hormuz transit hit a wartime high the same week (Lesson 137). Friday, June 26 produced the cycle's first reasonably well-confirmed sub-$80 weekly close: Brent settled at **~$71.99–72/bbl** (down 4.3% on the day, its lowest level since before the war), now converged across TradingEconomics and Investing.com rather than resting on a single source. Overnight Friday into Saturday, a second tanker — the *Kiku*, carrying 2 million+ barrels of Qatari crude — was struck by a drone in the strait; the US launched a first round of retaliatory strikes on Iranian military infrastructure. Iran responded Sunday, June 28 by striking **two** sovereign targets in a single day: a drone/missile attack near Bahrain's international airport (host of the US Fifth Fleet; one residential building damaged, no deaths) and a strike on **Kuwait's Ali Al Salem airbase**, which also hosts US forces (intercepted by Kuwaiti air defenses, no damage). The US answered with a second, larger round of strikes, this time including Iranian minelaying capability. Iran threatened a "complete halt" to talks — but late Sunday, both sides instead agreed to **"stand down for now,"** and negotiations are set to resume **Tuesday, June 30, in Doha, Qatar**, not Switzerland. Brent opened Monday at $75.09 and traded $71.94–$75.43 intraday on stand-down relief — a bounce, not a reversal through $80.

### Geopolitical Significance
This resolves the question Lesson 138 left open. It is not a clean Al Asad-style single-shot ceiling (Iran struck twice, adding Kuwait as an entirely new target country this conflict had not previously touched) — but it is also not an open-ended spiral. The fact that both sides chose to stand down *and* explicitly preserve the negotiating channel, relocating it to Doha rather than letting it collapse the way the June 17 Islamabad MOU did, is a meaningfully different evidentiary pattern than either prior cycle this project has graded. Qatar moving from mediator to host is itself a small but real signal of Gulf-state stake-holding in an outcome. Call this pattern **"escalate-twice-then-cap-and-keep-talking"** — a third distinct mode, alongside the Al Asad ceiling and the Islamabad collapse, that the framework should now hold as a live possibility rather than retrofitting after the fact.

### Investment Implications
- **Crude oil (HOLD, no change):** Friday's close (~$71.99–72) stands as the **first of three** required consecutive sub-$80 weekly closes — the same count as L138, now on firmer evidentiary footing. Monday's intraday bounce to $75 is relief-rally noise on the stand-down news, not a confirmed close. **The checkpoint remains Friday, July 3 — the second required close.** Do not chase either direction on Monday's print.
- **Gold (HOLD, HIGH conviction, unchanged):** Kill switch (Brent <$75 AND 10Y TIPS real yield >3% for 60 days) is nowhere close on either leg. Unaffected by this week's escalation.
- **Tankers (HOLD — first genuinely mixed read on the decoupling thesis):** FRO closed $35.52 June 26 — a ~13% pullback from its June 18 all-time high ($40.93), though still +89% YTD. STNG fell to $75.46 (June 25), well off its May all-time high ($86.10). DHT was roughly flat (~$17.36, June 26). This is the first week the decoupling-from-Brent thesis (L131) shows tanker softness *alongside* continued Brent decline, rather than clean divergence — graded honestly as a partial complication, not a falsification, on one week of data. Separately: the FRO/STNG/DHT dated-price gap flagged for four consecutive sessions (L136–L138) is now partially closed — see Databricks Angle.

## EVENT 2 — KEIR STARMER RESIGNS: A G7 PRIME MINISTER STEPS DOWN MID-TERM

### What Happened
The UK Labour leadership question this project has carried for two sessions is resolved into a much larger story than previously framed. Poor May 2026 local election results triggered a cascading crisis: Health Secretary Wes Streeting resigned May 14; Defence Secretary John Healey and a junior minister resigned June 11 over defence-spending disputes; Greater Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham then won the June 18 Makerfield by-election — a seat vacated specifically to let him enter Parliament and qualify for a leadership bid. Facing open calls for a challenge, **Keir Starmer announced his resignation as PM and Labour leader on June 22.** Labour's NEC has set nominations for July 9–16; if a contest is triggered, voting runs August 6–27, with a result announced August 29. Burnham has secured backing from 200+ MPs (the threshold is 81) and Streeting has endorsed him rather than running himself — most coverage frames this as heading toward an uncontested "coronation." Angela Rayner was cleared in a separate tax inquiry around the same time but has not announced a candidacy.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the first mid-term G7 head-of-government resignation this project has had to grade. The market-relevant fact is not the resignation itself — sterling had already drifted down roughly 3% since February as Starmer's position weakened, so the announcement confirmed rather than surprised — it is that Burnham is widely viewed as more fiscally left-leaning than Starmer/Reeves, meaning markets are now pre-pricing a policy-regime question (looser fiscal rules, higher gilt issuance) two months ahead of any actual vote. Note an open item: no source confirms the interim governance arrangement explicitly, though standard UK convention (as in 2022) would have Starmer remain as caretaker PM until Labour's new leader is confirmed, since no general election is triggered by an internal party leadership change.

### Investment Implications
- **GBP / Gilts (NEW WATCH ITEM, no position yet):** Initial reaction was muted (GBP ~$1.319, 10Y gilt ~4.85%) precisely because the move was already priced. The real test is the succession outcome, not the resignation.
- **UK equities:** No discernible standalone reaction; no position recommended.
- **Action for Bolo:** Build a UK political-risk tracker (GBP, 10Y gilt yield, FTSE) spanning the July 9–16 nomination window through the August 29 result. Revisit explicitly once (if) Burnham is confirmed, with specific attention to his stated fiscal positioning.

## EVENT 3 — WASHINGTON BUILDS THE SECTION 122 SUCCESSOR WHILE THE USMCA CLOCK STARTS

### What Happened
Two North American trade tracks moved in parallel. First: USTR has proposed Section 301 forced-labor duties (10% for countries with a forced-labor import ban, 12.5% for others — covering roughly 60 countries and 99% of imports) explicitly as the mechanism to take over **when Section 122 lapses by statute July 24** (the Federal Circuit's June 11 stay keeping the current 10% tariffs in effect through appeal remains unchanged this week). The Section 301 comment period closes July 6, with a USTR hearing July 7 — both timed to land just ahead of the cliff. Second: USMCA's six-year review opens July 1 as previously flagged; Trump's posture is unchanged ("I would rather not have the agreement, but I may sign it"); Mexico's Sheinbaum government continues a negotiated de-escalation strategy, with a third round of bilateral talks scheduled for next month, while Canada's Carney maintains the harder "the old relationship is over" line. The currency divergence flagged June 22 has sharpened: MXN is up roughly 4% in Q2 2026, while USD/CAD sits near 1.38 — a 2026 low for the Canadian dollar.

### Geopolitical Significance
The Section 301 plan reframes the entire "Section 122 cliff" narrative this project has tracked since L128: this is not tariffs lapsing into a gap, it is a **tariff-mechanism swap** — the practical burden on most affected imports may not fall at all, just migrate to a different legal basis. Combined with USMCA's own review opening the same window, North American and global trade policy is being rebuilt on two parallel, legally distinct tracks simultaneously — the exact kind of category-conflation risk L128 specifically warned against when it separated Section 122 from the EU-US framework.

### Investment Implications
- **Do not treat July 24 as a tariff-removal date.** Treat it as a transition date. This reinforces, rather than introduces, the structural-inflation/TIPS thesis: a persistent tariff-pass-through channel survives the "cliff" regardless of the Section 122 outcome.
- **MXN (incrementally constructive, no new position):** Mexico's negotiated approach continues to be rewarded with currency strength.
- **CAD (incrementally cautious, no new position):** Canada's more confrontational posture correlates with continued weakness; track against USMCA review progress rather than treating it as a standalone short.
- **European autos underweight:** Unaffected — this position runs on the already-resolved EU-US 15% framework (confirmed separate in L128), not Section 122 or USMCA.
- **Action for Bolo:** Build the `trade_deadline_ledger` flagged June 22 — now urgent, with three dated catalysts (July 1 USMCA review opens, July 6/7 Section 301 comment/hearing, July 24 Section 122 statutory expiry) landing within four weeks. Track as three distinct rows, not one composite score.

### What Happened

Fujimori's count holds at roughly 50.11%–49.89% with 99.72%+ counted; OAS and EU election observers continue to characterize the vote as clean. ## EVENT 5 — THE DOLLAR BREAKS 100, GOLD ROUND-TRIPS BELOW $4,000, AND THE FED'S HIKE ODDS WHIPSAW

### What Happened
DXY broke above 100 (~100.7, highest since May 2025) on the back of the Fed's hawkish June 17 hold. Gold fell to roughly $4,000–4,005/oz on June 25 — its fourth straight weekly decline — before bouncing ~0.7% to ~$4,063 on June 26. September rate-hike-probability readings diverged sharply within the same week: some sources still showed 63–70%, but a later read — following a PCE print showing the Fed's preferred inflation gauge at a three-year high alongside early signs it may have peaked — cut the September-hike probability to **~47.5%**, the first sub-50% reading since this repricing began. The 10-year TIPS real yield held steady at 2.16–2.17%.

### Geopolitical Significance
Less a single event than a stress test of the "Warsh = hawkish, structural dollar strength" narrative this project has carried since L126/L127. A single data point cutting hike odds by more than 20 points within days shows how fragile the post-FOMC repricing actually is — the same "narrative running ahead of confirmation" pattern L131 flagged for geopolitical announcements, now visibly present in monetary-policy reads too.

### Investment Implications
- **Gold (HOLD, HIGH conviction, kill switch nowhere close):** TIPS leg unmoved at 2.16–2.17%; Brent leg, if anything, reinforced (confirmed <$75 on Friday's close). A genuine hike-odds reversal toward 50/50 would remove some of the dollar-strength headwind behind this week's dip below $4,000 — modestly supportive into next week.
