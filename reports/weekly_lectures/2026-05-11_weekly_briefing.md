## EVENT 1 — US-IRAN: ONE-PAGE DEAL OR ONE MORE FALSE DAWN?

### What Happened
The White House and Tehran moved closer to a one-page Memorandum of Understanding to formally end the US-Iran war and open a 30-day framework for permanent negotiations. The MOU's 14 points include: a moratorium on Iranian uranium enrichment (duration disputed — Iran wants 5 years, the US wants 20, current range is 12-15 years), snap IAEA inspections, lifting US sanctions, release of frozen Iranian funds, and mutual withdrawal of Hormuz restrictions. As of May 10, Iran had not formally responded, citing internal deliberations. Oil prices fell nearly 3% Friday on the prospect of a settlement.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the most consequential active negotiation in the world right now. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since the war began (February 28), choking roughly 20% of global oil flow. A deal would: (1) re-open the strait, (2) reintegrate Iranian oil supply into global markets (~2-3 mbpd), (3) reduce Israel-Lebanon escalation risk, and (4) give Trump a historic foreign-policy trophy before midterms. The structural obstacle is verification — Iran's nuclear posture is non-negotiable domestically, and any deal that reads as capitulation is politically toxic in Tehran.

**Key risk:** Iran's demand to settle Hormuz before nuclear terms — the sequencing dispute is the real sticking point.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Brent Crude | **Bearish (structural)** | Deal = 2-3 mbpd reintegration + strait reopening. $108 Brent reflects a war premium of ~$25-30. Downside to $75-80 on full settlement. |
| Energy equities (E&P) | **Bearish on resolution** | War-premium baked into US shale producers. Fade on confirmed deal. |
| Shipping / tankers | **Bearish on resolution** | Hormuz rerouting has inflated tanker rates. Normalization compresses them sharply. |
| Gold | **Neutral to slightly bearish** | Safe-haven demand partly driven by Hormuz risk. Deal reduces geopolitical premium. |
| Israeli equities / shekel | **Bullish on ceasefire** | Shekel already hit 33-year high vs. dollar on deal optimism. Peace dividend for Israeli tech if Lebanon talks also advance. |
| EM bonds (Gulf states) | **Bullish** | Saudi, UAE, Kuwaiti spreads should tighten as regional risk premium deflates. |

**CEO Directional View:** The MOU is likely to be signed within 30 days, but the permanent deal will take 12+ months and may fail. Trade the immediate relief rally in risk assets (short energy, long Israeli equities), but position for the permanent deal falling apart. Structural bearishness on oil should be hedged — do not go net short energy before the MOU is formally signed.

## EVENT 2 — TRUMP-XI BEIJING SUMMIT (MAY 14-15): MANAGED COMPETITION OR MANAGED THEATER?

### What Happened
President Trump is scheduled to visit Beijing May 14-15 — the first US presidential visit to China in nearly a decade (last: Trump himself in 2017). Pre-summit talks in Paris focused on rare-earth supply access and the outline of a joint US-China "Board of Trade" plus a "Board of Investment." Both sides are expected to announce soybean purchase agreements, possible jet engine sales, and framework language on a more regularized dispute-resolution process. China's foreign ministry cautioned that ties have been "stable despite many twists," and warned of potential Taiwan maneuvering.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a managed-competition summit, not a détente. Beijing is strategically patient — China's calculus includes US midterm elections and the Supreme Court's February ruling that struck down many of Trump's tariffs as unlawful (reducing US leverage). The rare-earth angle is signal: China controls ~90% of rare earth processing and has been using export controls as a coercive tool. A Board of Trade framework is a face-saving mechanism to reduce tariff chaos without either side publicly conceding.

**Taiwan risk:** Beijing will push on Taiwan to test Trump's commitment to the existing "One China" framework. Any ambiguity from Trump on Taiwan is immediately investable.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Rare earth / critical mineral miners | **Bullish (cautious)** | Any supply-access deal for US rare earths lifts non-China producers (MP Materials, Lynas). But headline risk if deal falls through. |
| US agriculture (soybeans) | **Bullish short-term** | Soybean purchase agreement likely. Spot uplift for US ag producers. |
| Chinese tech equities | **Bullish on summit outcome** | Reduced tariff rhetoric = multiple expansion for Alibaba, Tencent. Fade quickly if Taiwan dispute surfaces. |
| Defense sector | **Neutral to bearish short-term** | Summit "peace dividend" temporarily suppresses defense spending urgency narrative. Long-term secular bull case intact. |
| USD/CNY | **Slightly bullish CNY** | Trade framework reduces depreciation pressure on yuan. |

**CEO Directional View:** Summit will produce optics, not architecture. The bilateral relationship is structurally adversarial — a trade framework doesn't change semiconductor restrictions, military buildups, or Taiwan posture. Take any rally in China equities as a trade, not a thesis. Maintain structural positioning in ex-China rare earth producers and defense.

## EVENT 3 — JAPAN'S $34.5 BILLION YEN DEFENSE: A POLICY BIND AT THE HEART OF GLOBAL CARRY

### What Happened
Japan's Ministry of Finance intervened in currency markets on May 1 with an estimated $34.5 billion — the largest intervention since July 2024 — after the yen hit multi-decade lows against the dollar. The BOJ's policy rate sits at 0.75% vs. the Fed's 3.50-3.75%, a ~300bps gap that has fueled the yen carry trade at scale. The intervention briefly spiked the yen by 2%+ before markets resumed testing it. By May 7, yen was again under pressure and speculation of a second intervention emerged. The US was notified in advance per G7 agreement.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is not just a currency story — it's a stress indicator for the entire global carry trade architecture. The yen carry trade (borrow cheap yen, buy higher-yielding assets globally) is estimated to underpin hundreds of billions in positions across EM bonds, US equities, and commodities. An aggressive BOJ rate hike to defend the yen would trigger a forced unwind. The July 2024 precedent (flash crash in global equities after a modest BOJ hike) is the template. Japan is trapped: the yen is a national competitiveness and import-cost issue, but the cure (rate hikes) risks a global liquidity shock.

**Geopolitical layer:** Iran war-driven oil prices are a direct tax on Japan — it imports nearly 100% of its oil, predominantly through the Strait of Hormuz. The yen's weakness is partly an Iran-war transmission mechanism.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| USD/JPY | **Volatile, structural yen weakness** | 300bps rate gap too large for intervention alone to close. Yen structurally weak until BOJ hikes meaningfully. |
| Japanese equities (Nikkei) | **Bullish on yen weakness, hedged** | Weak yen inflates earnings of exporters. But BOJ rate hike risk creates tail event. |
| EM bonds | **Tail risk** | Yen carry unwind is the largest systemic tail risk in global markets right now. A BOJ rate shock could trigger forced selling across EM. |
| Gold | **Bullish** | Yen weakness + Iran risk + dollar uncertainty = gold demand. |
| BOJ rate expectations | **Watch closely** | Any BOJ meeting surprise is the single most underpriced systemic risk in global markets. |

**CEO Directional View:** Yen carry unwind is the black swan nobody is adequately pricing. Our Databricks platform should flag BOJ meeting dates as high-volatility events and model the contagion path to EM bonds. Do not be long EM fixed income without hedging this tail.

## EVENT 4 — MALI SIEGE: SAHEL STATE COLLAPSE AND THE GREAT POWER VACUUM

### What Happened
On April 25, thousands of JNIM (al-Qaeda affiliate) and Azawad Liberation Front fighters launched simultaneous coordinated attacks on Bamako, Gao, Kidal, Mopti, and Sevare — Mali's capital and major military installations. Mali's Defense Minister Sadio Camara was killed at his residence in Kati (11 miles from Bamako). Russian Africa Corps (Wagner successor) mercenaries withdrew from Kidal following reported Algerian diplomatic intervention. Armed groups now control several northern cities. The Sahel accounts for 51% of global terrorism deaths per the 2026 Global Terrorism Index.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the most significant African security event of the year. Mali's junta expelled French forces in 2022 and pivoted to Russia — but Russian mercenaries are now retreating. The vacuum is being filled by jihadists with a governance project (JNIM administers territory). This has five direct geopolitical consequences:
1. **Resource access** — Mali has bauxite, gold, lithium, manganese, phosphate, uranium. Jihadist territorial control threatens extraction.
2. **Migration pressure** — Sahel collapse is a primary driver of Mediterranean migration toward Europe. Intensification will pressure EU politics.
3. **Regional contagion** — Burkina Faso and Niger are in similar junta-jihadist dynamics. A Mali collapse accelerates the domino.
4. **Great power repositioning** — Russia is retreating; China is watching; the US has minimal presence. This is a real power vacuum.
5. **Uranium supply chain** — Mali and neighboring Niger are significant uranium producers. Security deterioration is bearish for reliable supply.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Uranium | **Bullish** | Sahel instability threatens uranium supply from Niger/Mali. Structural supply deficit story strengthens. |
| Gold miners (West Africa) | **Bearish / exit** | Mali is a major gold producer. Operating risk for Barrick, B2Gold, and others with Mali exposure is sharply elevated. |
| French defense sector | **Bullish** | France will face political pressure to re-engage or build a multilateral Sahel force. Defense procurement argument strengthens. |
| European equities | **Migration risk discount** | Sahel collapse intensifies migration pressure on southern EU states, feeding populist political risk. Structural bearish on EU periphery. |
| Lithium explorers (West Africa) | **Risk-off** | Several pre-production lithium projects in the region face force majeure risk. |

**CEO Directional View:** Sahel collapse is a slow-moving structural signal, not a one-week trade. The uranium thesis is the cleanest investable angle. Gold miner exposure to West Africa should be reduced — this is not a recoverable situation in 6-18 months.

## EVENT 5 — OPEC+ FRACTURES: UAE EXITS, OUTPUT HIKES, AND OIL MARKET ARCHITECTURE UNDER STRESS

### What Happened
OPEC+ announced a production increase of 188,000 barrels per day at its May 3 meeting — the first meeting held without the UAE after nearly 60 years of membership. The UAE formally quit the cartel to pursue its energy strategy without production quotas, citing its need to monetize reserves rapidly as energy transition accelerates. Brent crude is currently at ~$108/bbl, up ~78% since January 2026 — almost entirely attributable to the Iran war and Hormuz closure. Eight producers simultaneously reaffirmed an "output pause" — creating internal contradictions.

### Geopolitical Significance
OPEC+'s institutional credibility is fracturing under multiple pressures simultaneously: (1) the Iran war has made production quotas irrelevant given Hormuz disruption; (2) the UAE's exit signals that Gulf states are increasingly optimizing for national revenue over cartel discipline; (3) Saudi Arabia's ability to enforce compliance weakens as UAE exits and Iraq chronically overproduces; (4) a US-Iran deal could flood 2-3 mbpd back into the market, potentially triggering a price crash that reveals just how broken OPEC+ coordination is. Post-Iran-deal, the cartel will face its most severe cohesion test in decades.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Brent / WTI | **Bearish on deal, volatile on no-deal** | War premium of $25-30/bbl. On Iran MOU: target $75-80 Brent. On deal collapse: retest $115+. |
| OPEC+ equities (Saudi Aramco) | **Bearish medium-term** | UAE exit weakens cartel discipline → downward price pressure even without Iran deal. Saudi needs ~$85 oil to balance budget. |
| US shale producers | **Bullish on Iran deal at lower price** | US shale breakeven is ~$55-60/bbl. Iranian supply at $75-80 oil is still profitable for US producers; collapses Gulf fiscal space. |
| Energy ETFs (XLE) | **Neutral to slightly bearish** | War premium removal > production hike offset. Fade on confirmed deal, reload on cartel breakdown signal. |
| LNG / nat gas | **Bullish structurally** | OPEC fracture + Hormuz vulnerability is accelerating LNG as an alternative. European demand remains robust. |

**CEO Directional View:** OPEC is entering a structural decline as an effective cartel. The UAE exit is the most underappreciated long-term signal from this week. Position for higher oil price volatility, not sustained high prices. Energy transition + cartel fragmentation + US shale resilience = structurally lower oil price ceiling over 5-10 years, with violent swings in between.
