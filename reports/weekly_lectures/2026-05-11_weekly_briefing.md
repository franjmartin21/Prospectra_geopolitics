## EVENT 1 — US-IRAN: ONE-PAGE DEAL OR ONE MORE FALSE DAWN?

### What Happened
The White House and Tehran moved closer to a one-page Memorandum of Understanding to formally end the US-Iran war and open a 30-day framework for permanent negotiations. The MOU's 14 points include: a moratorium on Iranian uranium enrichment, snap IAEA inspections, lifting US sanctions, release of frozen Iranian funds, and mutual withdrawal of Hormuz restrictions. As of May 10, Iran had not formally responded. Oil prices fell nearly 3% Friday on the prospect of a settlement.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the most consequential active negotiation in the world right now. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since the war began (February 28), choking roughly 20% of global oil flow. A deal would: (1) re-open the strait, (2) reintegrate Iranian oil supply (~2-3 mbpd), (3) reduce Israel-Lebanon escalation risk, and (4) give Trump a historic foreign-policy trophy before midterms. **Key risk:** Iran's demand to settle Hormuz before nuclear terms — the sequencing dispute is the real sticking point.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Brent Crude | **Bearish (structural)** | Deal = 2-3 mbpd reintegration + strait reopening. $108 Brent reflects war premium of ~$25-30. Downside to $75-80 on full settlement. |
| Energy equities (E&P) | **Bearish on resolution** | War-premium baked into US shale producers. Fade on confirmed deal. |
| Shipping / tankers | **Bearish on resolution** | Hormuz rerouting inflated tanker rates. Normalization compresses them sharply. |
| Gold | **Neutral to slightly bearish** | Safe-haven demand partly driven by Hormuz risk. Deal reduces geopolitical premium. |
| Israeli equities / shekel | **Bullish on ceasefire** | Shekel hit 33-year high vs. dollar on deal optimism. |
| EM bonds (Gulf states) | **Bullish** | Saudi, UAE, Kuwaiti spreads should tighten as regional risk premium deflates. |

**CEO Directional View:** The MOU is likely to be signed within 30 days, but the permanent deal will take 12+ months and may fail. Trade the immediate relief rally in risk assets (short energy, long Israeli equities), but position for the permanent deal falling apart.

## EVENT 2 — TRUMP-XI BEIJING SUMMIT (MAY 14-15): MANAGED COMPETITION OR MANAGED THEATER?

### What Happened
President Trump is scheduled to visit Beijing May 14-15 — the first US presidential visit to China in nearly a decade. Pre-summit talks focused on rare-earth supply access and the outline of a joint US-China "Board of Trade" plus a "Board of Investment." Both sides are expected to announce soybean purchase agreements, possible jet engine sales, and framework language on a regularized dispute-resolution process.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a managed-competition summit, not a détente. Beijing is strategically patient. The rare-earth angle is signal: China controls ~90% of rare earth processing and has been using export controls as a coercive tool. **Taiwan risk:** Beijing will push on Taiwan to test Trump's commitment to the existing "One China" framework.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Rare earth / critical mineral miners | **Bullish (cautious)** | Any supply-access deal lifts non-China producers (MP Materials, Lynas). |
| US agriculture (soybeans) | **Bullish short-term** | Soybean purchase agreement likely. |
| Chinese tech equities | **Bullish on summit outcome** | Reduced tariff rhetoric = multiple expansion for Alibaba, Tencent. Fade quickly if Taiwan dispute surfaces. |
| USD/CNY | **Slightly bullish CNY** | Trade framework reduces depreciation pressure on yuan. |

**CEO Directional View:** Summit will produce optics, not architecture. Take any rally in China equities as a trade, not a thesis.

## EVENT 3 — JAPAN'S $34.5 BILLION YEN DEFENSE: A POLICY BIND AT THE HEART OF GLOBAL CARRY

### What Happened
Japan's Ministry of Finance intervened in currency markets on May 1 with an estimated $34.5 billion — the largest intervention since July 2024 — after the yen hit multi-decade lows against the dollar. The BOJ's policy rate sits at 0.75% vs. the Fed's 3.50-3.75%, a ~300bps gap fueling the yen carry trade at scale.

### Geopolitical Significance
The yen carry trade (borrow cheap yen, buy higher-yielding assets globally) is estimated to underpin hundreds of billions in positions across EM bonds, US equities, and commodities. An aggressive BOJ rate hike to defend the yen would trigger a forced unwind. The July 2024 precedent (flash crash in global equities) is the template.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| USD/JPY | **Volatile, structural yen weakness** | 300bps rate gap too large for intervention alone to close. |
| EM bonds | **Tail risk** | Yen carry unwind is the largest systemic tail risk in global markets right now. |
| Gold | **Bullish** | Yen weakness + Iran risk + dollar uncertainty. |

**CEO Directional View:** Yen carry unwind is the black swan nobody is adequately pricing. Our Databricks platform should flag BOJ meeting dates as high-volatility events.

## EVENT 4 — MALI SIEGE: SAHEL STATE COLLAPSE AND THE GREAT POWER VACUUM

### What Happened
On April 25, thousands of JNIM (al-Qaeda affiliate) and Azawad Liberation Front fighters launched coordinated attacks on Bamako, Gao, Kidal, Mopti, and Sevare. Mali's Defense Minister was killed. Russian Africa Corps mercenaries withdrew from Kidal. Armed groups now control several northern cities.

### Geopolitical Significance
Five direct geopolitical consequences:
1. **Resource access** — Mali has bauxite, gold, lithium, manganese, uranium. Jihadist territorial control threatens extraction.
2. **Migration pressure** — Sahel collapse is a primary driver of Mediterranean migration.
3. **Regional contagion** — Burkina Faso and Niger are in similar junta-jihadist dynamics.
4. **Great power repositioning** — Russia is retreating; the US has minimal presence.
5. **Uranium supply chain** — Mali and Niger are significant uranium producers.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Uranium | **Bullish** | Sahel instability threatens uranium supply. |
| Gold miners (West Africa) | **Bearish / exit** | Operating risk for Barrick, B2Gold, and others with Mali exposure. |
| French defense sector | **Bullish** | Political pressure to re-engage. |

**CEO Directional View:** Sahel collapse is a slow-moving structural signal. The uranium thesis is the cleanest investable angle.

## EVENT 5 — OPEC+ FRACTURES: UAE EXITS, OUTPUT HIKES, AND OIL MARKET ARCHITECTURE UNDER STRESS

### What Happened
OPEC+ announced a production increase of 188,000 barrels per day at its May 3 meeting — the first held without the UAE after nearly 60 years of membership. The UAE formally quit the cartel to pursue its energy strategy without production quotas.

### Geopolitical Significance
OPEC+'s institutional credibility is fracturing: (1) the Iran war has made production quotas irrelevant; (2) the UAE's exit signals Gulf states are increasingly optimizing for national revenue over cartel discipline; (3) Saudi Arabia's ability to enforce compliance weakens; (4) a US-Iran deal could flood 2-3 mbpd back, triggering a price crash.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Brent / WTI | **Bearish on deal, volatile on no-deal** | War premium of $25-30/bbl. On Iran MOU: target $75-80 Brent. |
| US shale producers | **Bullish on Iran deal at lower price** | US shale breakeven ~$55-60/bbl. |
| LNG / nat gas | **Bullish structurally** | OPEC fracture + Hormuz vulnerability accelerates LNG adoption. |

**CEO Directional View:** OPEC is entering a structural decline as an effective cartel. The UAE exit is the most underappreciated long-term signal.
