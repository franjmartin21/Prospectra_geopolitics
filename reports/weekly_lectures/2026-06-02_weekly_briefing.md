## EVENT 1 — IRAN/LEBANON NEXUS: THE DEAL HELD HOSTAGE

### What Happened

The week produced the most complex 72-hour sequence of the Iran crisis to date:

**May 28:** US and Iranian negotiators formally agreed the text of a 60-day MOU — extending the ceasefire, opening the Strait of Hormuz (no mines, no tolls, Iran to de-mine within 30 days), and beginning nuclear talks. The deal required only Trump's signature.

**May 29–31:** Israel expanded its military offensive in southern Lebanon, launching heavy strikes on Hezbollah infrastructure. Iran's IRGC simultaneously fired ballistic missiles toward Kuwait (intercepted). Iran's Foreign Ministry announced it was **halting all communications with Washington** until Israeli operations in Lebanon and Gaza ceased.

**June 1:** Trump stated he had secured pledges from both sides — Hezbollah agreed "all shooting will stop"; Israeli leadership would "stand down." Netanyahu issued a simultaneous statement: Israeli operations in southern Lebanon "will continue as planned." Iran responded by calling Washington's brokerage capacity into question.

**June 2 (intraday):** Brent surged 8% on Iranian state media reports of full Strait closure. Then retreated 4% when Trump claimed the Lebanon talks were proceeding "at rapid pace." WTI closed above $92/bbl. Brent near $98.

### Geopolitical Significance

The Lebanon complication reveals the structural architecture of the Iran deal problem with absolute clarity. There are **three principals in the room** — and only two are at the table:

1. **The Iranian political leadership** (willing to sign an MOU for sanctions relief)
2. **The IRGC** (operationally autonomous; firing missiles while negotiators signal flexibility)
3. **Israel / Netanyahu** (conducting a parallel war in Lebanon that Iran has made a precondition for any US deal)

Trump cannot control Netanyahu's operational tempo. Netanyahu cannot or will not stop for a ceasefire he didn't negotiate. Iran will not sign while Israel operates freely in Lebanon. The MOU is a **three-party problem structured as a two-party negotiation** — and it will remain stuck until either Israel pauses (politically costly for Netanyahu) or the US conditions further support on Israeli restraint (politically costly for Trump).

**The IRGC split-principal problem** (raised in the June 1 briefing) is now confirmed in both directions: political negotiators signal agreement while operational commanders fire ballistic missiles. A signed MOU does not end the physical threat to the Strait as long as the IRGC retains operational independence.

## EVENT 2 — THE BOND VIGILANTE SPEAKS: 30-YEAR TREASURY AT 5.2%

### What Happened

The 30-year US Treasury yield reached **5.2% on May 19** — its highest level since 2007. The week ending June 2 has seen yields hover between 5.0–5.2% with no structural buyer emerging to provide relief. Key data points:

- Annual US budget deficit: ~$2.0 trillion (FY2026 projected)
- Net interest cost on US debt: **$1.0 trillion/year** — now exceeding the entire defense budget
- US debt-to-GDP: >100% for the second time since WWII
- CBO estimate: "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" adds $3.4 trillion to 10-year deficit trajectory
- Recent 30-year auction: $25 billion at 5.0% — first 5-handle 30-year auction since 2007
- Demand shift: Central bank buyers (China, Japan) pulling back; hedge funds stepping in at demanding yields

The 10-year remains in the 4.25–4.60% range — not yet at the 5% threshold that would trigger broad asset repricing. But the curve is steepening and the bond market is clearly demanding a term premium that has not been formally acknowledged by equities.

### Geopolitical Significance

The bond market's message is structurally geopolitical in two ways:

**1. The buyer strike is not just financial — it's geopolitical.** Japan holds ~$1.1 trillion in Treasuries but is in an active debate about diversifying as the yen carry unwinds. China holds ~$750 billion and has been a net seller since 2022. Gulf sovereign wealth funds — traditional marginal buyers — are watching the US manage the Iran crisis and asking whether the dollar system's protector role is being upheld. The marginal buyers at 30-year Treasury auctions are now **hedge funds requiring 5%+** — not the long-duration, patient capital base that historically suppressed term premia.

**2. Fiscal dominance is now live.** The Fed cannot tighten aggressively without triggering a debt service crisis. This is the exact Lesson 73 framework (Central Bank Independence vs. Fiscal Dominance) playing out in real time. The Fed's credibility is being tested not by inflation alone but by the combination of structural fiscal excess and geopolitically-motivated buyer retreat. The political constraint is binding: Congress cannot structurally reduce deficits during a war cycle. The monetary consequence: 2% inflation is a political aspiration, not a monetary achievable.

## EVENT 3 — G7 ÉVIAN: THE FIRST CRISIS-ERA SUMMIT

### What Happened

The 52nd G7 Leaders' Summit convenes **June 15–17 in Évian-les-Bains, France** — 13 days from today. Security preparations: 8,000 French police officers; 5,000 Swiss troops across Lake Geneva; 8-day Swiss airspace closure. This is the first G7 summit convened in the middle of an active crisis — the Iran war, Ukraine war, and global trade restructuring are all live as leaders sit down.

**Key participants:** US (Trump), France (Macron — his last summit), Germany (TBD chancellor), UK, Japan (PM Takaichi — her first summit), Canada, Italy. Invited: India (Modi), Brazil (Lula), South Korea, Kenya. Notably absent from invitation list: Saudi Arabia, UAE — conspicuous given the Iran crisis.

**Agenda priorities declared by French G7 presidency:**
- Reducing global economic imbalances (direct reference to US-China trade structure and European overcapacity exposure)
- AI and quantum technology governance
- Non-bank financial intermediary regulation
- Climate and extreme weather financial impact
- Cross-border payments reform

**Unannounced but inevitable agenda items:** Iran/Hormuz, Ukraine peace process, US fiscal trajectory, China industrial policy.

### Geopolitical Significance

The Évian summit will be structurally different from recent G7s in one critical way: **every leader arrives with binding domestic constraints** that prevent agreement on the issues that most need addressing.

- **Trump** cannot make commitments on Iran that constrain Israel; cannot commit to fiscal discipline; cannot accept EU trade demands under IEEPA constraint after the Supreme Court ruling
- **Macron** is a lame duck — constitutionally cannot stand for re-election; limited mandate for concessions
- **Takaichi** (Japan) is a first-time attendee navigating an earthquake in Japan's internal politics, a weak yen, and Hormuz-driven LNG import costs
- **Modi** arrives as the indispensable partner everyone wants but won't commit to — non-alignment as leverage

**The real G7 output to watch** is not the communiqué — it is bilateral meetings. Specifically: Trump-Modi (India friendshoring), Trump-Macron (Iran/Lebanon sequencing), Trump-Takaichi (yen, chip supply chains, Taiwan security guarantee language).

The Évian summit's historical significance: it may be the last G7 where the US is unambiguously the dominant voice setting the agenda. If Trump cannot deliver an Iran deal or Ukraine framework, the summit will accelerate the perception of US strategic overextension.

## EVENT 4 — TAIWAN'S $25 BILLION REARMAMENT: DETERRENCE IN PRACTICE

### What Happened

The **Taiwanese Legislative Yuan passed a special defense budget of 780 billion New Taiwan Dollars (~$25 billion USD) on May 8** — the largest single defense appropriation in Taiwan's history. The budget allocates:

- **300 billion NTD (Round 1):** HIMARS rocket artillery, M109A7 self-propelled howitzers, anti-armor drone missile systems, anti-tank guided missiles
- **480 billion NTD (Round 2):** Anti-ballistic missile systems, low/medium altitude air defense, counter-drone systems, anti-tank missile replenishment

Simultaneously, the PLA has continued its sustained military presence around Taiwan — the "new normal" of constant encirclement — and PRC intelligence agencies have been actively targeting indebted Taiwanese military personnel for espionage recruitment.

The US Defense Secretary's Shangri-La message ("no change in status") was notably softer than 2025's warning that Chinese military threat "could be imminent." US strategic bandwidth is demonstrably constrained by the Iran crisis.

### Geopolitical Significance

Taiwan's defense budget is the most important geopolitical data point of the month that markets are underweighting.

Three structural realities:

**1. Taiwan is self-relying because it has to.** The softer US deterrence signal from Shangri-La, combined with the Iran bandwidth drain, tells Taipei that the Article 5-equivalent commitment is less certain than 18 months ago. Taiwan is responding rationally: building its own deterrent capacity at record speed.

**2. The $25B budget is anti-invasion infrastructure, not anti-air show.** HIMARS on Taiwan = the kind of mobile, survivable precision fires that made Russian armored formations ineffective in Ukraine. Anti-ballistic missile systems directly counter China's second-strike capability. This is not symbolic spending — it is a serious military investment that will materially raise the cost of any PLA operation across the strait.

**3. PRC espionage targeting financially stressed military personnel is a tell.** It signals that China assesses its kinetic advantage in straight military confrontation and is instead investing in degrading Taiwan's operational reliability through intelligence penetration. This is asymmetric warfare at its most sophisticated — and it works in time pressure scenarios (a fast amphibious operation depends on Taiwan's early warning and response capability).

**The investor takeaway:** Taiwan is not complacent. The equity market (TSM, AMAT, ASML) is pricing Taiwan risk below the CEO's estimate. The $25B defense budget is a market signal that Taiwan's leadership is making contingency plans — not for "if" but for "when."

## EVENT 5 — RUSSIA-UKRAINE: THE FROZEN CONFLICT CLOCK STARTS

### What Happened

The past week confirmed what the CEO assessed as the most likely 18-month scenario: **a frozen conflict without formal resolution, with ongoing attrition and periodic localized ceasefires that do not hold**.

Key data points from May 26–June 2:
- Putin reiterated willingness to meet Zelensky "in Moscow or a neutral location" — rejected by Kyiv
- Zelensky publicly stated the US wants a peace deal "by June" — US diplomats neither confirmed nor denied
- European Council President Costa and former ECB chief Draghi both named as potential European mediators
- Ukraine completed its Gripen fighter jet deal with Sweden (16 donated + 20 purchased)
- Romania NATO drone injury remains uninvestigated through formal Article 5 processes — no NATO response mechanism triggered
- Russia launched major Kyiv strike (hundreds of drones/missiles) — largest since 2022 — on the same week Putin offered talks

**The ceasefire arbitrage:** Polymarket prices an 87–100% probability of ceasefire by June 30. The CEO prices it at 15–25%, with the delta explained by the market's tendency to extrapolate from "talks" language toward "resolution."

### Geopolitical Significance

The frozen conflict thesis is now operationally confirmed. What this means in practice:

1. **No major territorial change.** Russia holds ~20% of Ukraine; Ukraine is defending the line. NATO will not allow Ukrainian collapse; Russia will not accept a return to pre-2022 lines. Stalemate is the equilibrium.

2. **European security architecture has permanently shifted.** German defense spending at 3.5% GDP is not a political moment — it is a structural reallocation lasting a generation. The same is true for Poland (4%), the Nordic states (approaching 3%), and Romania (accelerating after the drone incident).

3. **Russia's war economy is durable because China and North Korea sustain it.** The Putin-Xi summit (covered May 25 briefing) institutionalized this supply chain. Russia can endure the attrition for 2–3+ more years without an economic collapse.

4. **The mediation vacuum is Europe's opportunity.** With the US consumed by Iran, Europe is being asked to lead peace diplomacy. If Macron can establish an EU-mediated framework before the Évian summit, it becomes the defining moment of French G7 leadership — and of EU strategic autonomy.
