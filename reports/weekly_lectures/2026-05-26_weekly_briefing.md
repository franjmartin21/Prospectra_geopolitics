## EVENT 1 — Strait of Hormuz: Trump Pauses Escort Operations, Deal Pressure Mounts

### What Happened
On May 5, President Trump announced he was pausing the US military effort to guide stranded vessels through the Strait of Hormuz — through which nearly 20% of global petroleum and 20% of global LNG normally transits — to create space for a ceasefire deal with Iran. The US-Iran-Israel war, which began February 28 with US-Israeli airstrikes killing Supreme Leader Khamenei, has effectively closed the Strait since March 4. Only ~5% of pre-war vessel traffic is currently transiting. Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain collectively shut in 10.5 million barrels per day of production in April. Total global supply losses since February: **12.8 mb/d**.

The UK has deployed drones, fighter aircraft, and a Royal Navy warship to join an international mission to secure commercial shipping. Brent averaged **$117/barrel** in April — the highest since June 2022. Current forecasts: ~$106/barrel in May-June, falling to ~$89/barrel in Q4 if the Strait gradually reopens. A US-Iran peace deal has 87.5% probability priced in prediction markets.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the most significant energy supply shock since Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The closure tests the resilience of the Pax Americana order in the Persian Gulf — the same order that has kept oil flowing since 1980. It is also a test of whether US military power can be converted into diplomatic outcomes. Trump's pause-and-negotiate tactic creates short-term price volatility but signals a desire to exit, which aligns with domestic political economy (high gasoline prices hurt the Republican base). The UAE's departure from OPEC effective May 1 is an underappreciated signal: Gulf states are hedging against OPEC's ability to manage the crisis.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Brent crude | **Elevated, volatile** | $89–117 range depending on deal timeline; every week of stalemate = upside pressure |
| LNG / nat gas | **Strongly bullish** | Qatar LNG rerouted; European TTF and Asian JKM spot remain elevated |
| Energy equities (XLE, integrated majors) | **Cautiously long** | EPS upgrades if prices stay above $90; downside risk if surprise deal |
| Shipping / tankers | **Bifurcated** | VLCC spot rates collapsing on Hormuz closure; rerouting firms benefit |
| S&P 500 | **Headwind** | $100+ oil is a 60–90bp drag on GDP; consumer confidence eroding |
| USD | **Mixed** | Safe haven flows support DXY; but fiscal deterioration (see Event 2) counters |
| EM oil importers (India, Turkey, Japan) | **Negative** | Current account deterioration, imported inflation, currency pressure |

**CEO View:** The Strait will reopen — the deal probability is high and the economics of continued closure are catastrophic for everyone. But the timing is uncertain. Maintain exposure to energy through the resolution; the risk/reward asymmetry favors being long until a formal reopening is confirmed. Do not chase the downside move when the deal is announced — the structural supply shortage doesn't resolve overnight.

## EVENT 2 — Moody's Downgrades US Sovereign Debt; Debt Exceeds GDP for First Time Since WWII

### What Happened
The US has now been downgraded by all three major rating agencies. Moody's cut the US from **Aaa to Aa1**, joining Fitch (AA+) and S&P (AA+). US debt held by the public crossed **$31.27 trillion**, exceeding GDP ($31.22 trillion) for the first time since the brief COVID period — and before that, World War II. Fitch projects a **7.9% deficit-to-GDP** for 2026 and 2027, driven by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), which adds an estimated **$3.4 trillion** to the federal debt through 2034. A Supreme Court ruling earlier this year striking down the bulk of Trump's tariffs removes approximately $1.7 trillion of projected 10-year revenue, worsening the trajectory further. Total projected debt over the next decade: **$58 trillion**.

The 10-year Treasury yield is holding in the 4.00–4.50% range. The OBBBA raised the statutory debt limit by $5 trillion, removing near-term default risk — but replacing it with long-term structural fiscal deterioration.

### Geopolitical Significance
The US credit rating is no longer the world's gold standard. This is a slow-moving but historic shift. The fiscal trajectory undermines the dollar's credibility as a reserve currency — not today, not this quarter, but over a 5–10 year horizon. It creates a perverse incentive structure: the US needs foreign buyers of Treasuries (Japan, China, oil exporters) at precisely the moment its relationships with those buyers are most adversarial. This is the Triffin Dilemma meeting fiscal dominance in real time.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| 10-year Treasuries | **Bearish long-term** | 4.00–4.50% range but upward pressure over 12–18 months; term premium repricing |
| USD (DXY) | **Structurally bearish** | Reserve currency status deterioration is slow but directional |
| Gold | **Strongly bullish** | The classic fiscal hedge; central banks already buying; $3000+ is a floor, not a ceiling |
| TIPS | **Modestly bullish** | Fiscal dominance creates latent inflation risk; real rate protection warranted |
| EM sovereign debt | **Selective** | Dollar weakness supports EM; but risk-off from US fiscal shock is a countervailing force |
| Bitcoin | **Speculative long** | Hard-asset narrative gains credibility; not core to thesis but worth monitoring |

**CEO View:** The Moody's downgrade is the most structurally important event this week. It isn't a near-term market shock — yields barely moved. That's precisely why it matters more, not less: the market is not pricing this correctly on a 3–5 year horizon. This is the strongest confirmation yet of the Bretton Woods III thesis we covered in Lesson 70. Build gold exposure systematically. The structural dollar short (expressed through gold, commodity currencies, and EM FX) remains our highest-conviction long-duration view.

## EVENT 3 — US-China Trade Truce: Fragile Agreement on Tariff Restoration

### What Happened
As of May 20, the US and China appear to have reached a tentative agreement on terms for restoring the voided Section 301 tariffs, but have not yet agreed to extend the one-year trade truce negotiated in October 2025. Treasury Secretary Bessent indicated China would accept restoration of prior tariff rates "as long as they don't go higher." The current effective tariff rate on most Chinese imports is **145%** when IEEPA border security and fentanyl tariffs are included. Exclusions on 178 product categories were extended until November 10, 2026. The two Section 301 investigations are set to conclude this summer — a key deadline. China has effectively stopped purchasing US agricultural exports since the trade war escalation.

### Geopolitical Significance
The trade relationship is no longer merely economic — it is the primary theater of the great power competition. China's halt of US agricultural purchases is an explicit signal: Beijing will use economic coercion as a negotiating lever. The 145% tariff wall has effectively bifurcated global supply chains in real time. The summer Section 301 conclusions will define the trade architecture for the next 12–24 months. The extension of product exclusions signals neither side wants a complete rupture — but neither is willing to make structural concessions.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| US agricultural commodities (soybeans, corn, wheat) | **Bearish** | China demand removal is structural; Brazil/Argentina gain share |
| US tech hardware, semiconductors | **Bearish near-term** | 145% tariffs on Chinese inputs = margin compression |
| Vietnam, Mexico, India equities/FX | **Bullish** | Friendshoring beneficiaries; supply chain diversion accelerating |
| Chinese domestic equities (A-shares) | **Neutral-to-bullish** | Fiscal stimulus + import substitution thesis; decoupled from US sentiment |
| Global shipping | **Elevated volatility** | Trans-Pacific volumes depressed; rerouting adds freight costs |

**CEO View:** The 145% tariff wall is the single most important structural change to the global trading system since the WTO's creation in 1995. The tentative agreement is not a resolution — it is a pause. The summer Section 301 conclusions could reignite tensions. Our friendshoring thesis (Vietnam, Mexico, India) remains intact. Do not read the détente as a signal to rotate back into US-China trade exposure.

## EVENT 4 — Russia-Ukraine: Trump's 3-Day Ceasefire, Putin Signals Openness to Talks

### What Happened
Following competing unilateral ceasefire declarations in early May (Russia declared May 8–9 for Victory Day; Ukraine declared May 5–6 to pre-empt the Russian narrative), President Trump announced and secured a **3-day ceasefire** running May 9–11, with 1,000 prisoners exchanged by each side. Putin subsequently hinted the war is "coming to an end" and expressed willingness to hold direct talks with Zelenskyy in Moscow or a neutral country. However, broader peace negotiations remain stalled, and both sides continued attacks between formal ceasefires. No structural agreement on territory, sovereignty, or security guarantees exists.

### Geopolitical Significance
The 3-day ceasefire is theater, not strategy. What matters is the direction: Putin's language about the war's end — unprecedented since 2022 — suggests Russian military objectives have either been achieved or are being reframed for a negotiated off-ramp. The critical unknown is the territorial settlement: any frozen-conflict outcome leaves Ukraine's NATO membership question unresolved and European security permanently unstable. European defense spending trajectories are now structurally elevated regardless of how this war ends.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| European defense (Rheinmetall, BAE, Leonardo, KNDS) | **Strongly bullish** | 2%+ NATO targets are floors, not ceilings; multi-year procurement cycle locked in |
| Ukrainian reconstruction bonds/funds | **Speculative, watch** | Peace deal = massive reconstruction opportunity; too early to size |
| European nat gas (TTF) | **Moderately bearish on ceasefire** | Russian supply resumption possibility if deal holds; but Hormuz offsets |
| Russian ruble / Russian equities | **Uninvestable** | Sanctions architecture intact; no Western capital re-entry path visible |
| CEE equities (Poland, Czech Republic, Romania) | **Constructive** | EU cohesion funds + defense spending + reconstruction proximity |

**CEO View:** Do not trade the ceasefire; trade the structural consequence. European defense is the clearest multi-year theme regardless of ceasefire outcome. If a genuine peace deal emerges in H2 2026, the Ukrainian reconstruction trade becomes the single largest infrastructure opportunity since post-WWII Europe — size it then, not now.

## EVENT 5 — India-Pakistan: One Year Since Operation Sindoor, Ceasefire Holds Under Pressure

### What Happened
May 10, 2026 marks the one-year anniversary of the India-Pakistan ceasefire that ended the 4-day "Operation Sindoor" conflict — triggered by the Pahalgam terrorist attack of April 22, 2025. The ceasefire, brokered by the Trump administration, holds. However, analysts note that both sides have signaled **resolve for further escalation** in any future conflict. Backchannel diplomatic talks exist but remain secret and fragile. The space for meaningful dialogue has narrowed as both governments face domestic constituencies demanding hardline postures.

### Geopolitical Significance
A conflict between two nuclear-armed states with a combined population of 1.8 billion is never noise. The structural risk here is a low-probability, high-impact event: a future terrorist attack could trigger an escalation cycle that moves faster than diplomacy. What has changed post-Sindoor: India's strategic posture is more assertive; Pakistan's military establishment sees nuclear signaling as a viable deterrence tool. The risk window for renewed conflict is higher in 2026–2027 than at any point since 2019 (Balakot). That said, the economic cost to both countries — especially Pakistan, which is on IMF life support — provides a powerful deterrent.

### Investment Implications

| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| Indian equities (Nifty, BSE Sensex) | **Bullish with risk overlay** | India growth story intact; geopolitical risk is a discount, not a veto |
| Pakistani assets | **Uninvestable near-term** | IMF program dependency + political instability + military influence = poor risk/reward |
| Indian defense sector | **Bullish** | Post-Sindoor procurement acceleration; import substitution (Make in India) policy |
| Indian rupee (INR) | **Neutral** | Oil import cost headwind (Hormuz) partially offsets India growth premium |
| Regional EM funds with South Asia exposure | **Underweight Pakistan, overweight India** | Rebalance within EM allocation |

**CEO View:** India remains our top single-country long in emerging markets — not because geopolitical risk is absent, but because the risk is priced in and the structural growth story (demographics, manufacturing pivot, digital infrastructure) is independent of Pakistan. The Hormuz crisis is the bigger near-term headwind for India than a ceasefire holding.
