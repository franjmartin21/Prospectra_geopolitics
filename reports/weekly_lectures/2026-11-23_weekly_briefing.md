### EVENT 1: APEC SHENZHEN — TRUMP-XI-PUTIN HISTORIC TRILATERAL

**What Happened**  
Trump arrived at APEC Shenzhen on November 18. The Trump-Xi bilateral took place on November 19, with Xi describing relations as "stable and improved" and Trump touting their "outstanding personal relationship." The same day, an unprecedented trilateral summit between Trump, Xi, and Putin convened at Kylin Villa. The declared agenda: nuclear arms, Iran conflict management, Ukraine endgame, and global trade. Xi pressed Trump to "do the right thing on Taiwan" to avoid a "major setback" in US-China relations. The US-China trade truce — extended to January 10, 2027 in early November — was confirmed but no structural deal was reached. The Commerce Department's new chip access restrictions targeting third-country routing of AI chips were on the agenda; Beijing voiced serious concerns. SCMP observed that "major deliverables" were expected to wait until the G20.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The trilateral format is the most significant structural signal: the US is openly co-managing global order with China and Russia simultaneously, implicitly sidelining the G7 and European institutions. Xi hosting in Shenzhen frames China as the architect of multipolar stability — a deliberate counter-narrative to US-led alliances. The Taiwan signal from Xi is unusually direct; it means the truce framework has a hidden condition: US restraint on Taiwan arms sales and diplomatic support. The January 10 trade truce cliff is now subordinate to this larger summit dynamic — a breakdown on Taiwan could collapse the truce instantly, while a "constructive" tone could buy another 6-month extension.

**Investment Implications**
- **Semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, ASML, TSMC, Samsung):** The bilateral produced no relaxation of AI chip controls — the new third-country routing rule stands. Bearish for AI semiconductor export revenue exposed to China. TSMC remains in the crossfire; the Taiwan sovereignty signal from Xi adds geopolitical premium to Taiwan-based manufacturing.
- **CNY/USD:** Constructive summit tone should allow modest CNY appreciation near-term. Watch CNH (offshore yuan) — it is the real-time gauge of market confidence in the truce. Break above 7.00 CNH/USD would signal meaningful confidence.
- **Rare earths / critical minerals (MP Materials, Lynas):** No resolution on rare-earth coercion dynamics. Western dependency on Chinese processing confirmed. Structural long thesis intact.
- **US Treasury yields:** A US-China "managed stability" narrative reduces tail-risk premium. Modest bull-flattening bias on 10Y UST if truce extends. Watch for PBOC reserve management signals post-APEC.
- **EM Asia (EWY, EWT, VNM):** Summit reduces near-term tail risk for Korean, Taiwanese, and Vietnamese exporters — but the new chip routing restrictions create a secondary risk for Korean semiconductor supply chains. Cautious.

Compare readout tone to CNH/USD and semiconductor ETF moves in the 48 hours post-summit. This is a teachable pattern for high-impact diplomatic events.

### EVENT 2: STRAIT OF HORMUZ ESCALATION — US-IRAN TRUCE FRACTURES AGAIN

**What Happened**  
Multiple tankers were struck in the Strait of Hormuz in the November 16–21 window. UKMTO reported a Marshall Islands-flagged bulk carrier (Manta Ursula) hit by an unidentified projectile, with engine-room damage. Iran's IRGC claimed a VLCC sank — not independently verified. Reports describe four tanker attacks within 24 hours by mid-week, and eight total since the start of November. One analysis described the week as a "US-Iran escalation shatters fragile Hormuz recovery" moment. The IEA's forecast of a 1.7M bpd global oil surplus — the first since the Iran war began — now has a significant asterisk: Iran is signaling it will not allow Western oil prices to stabilize while it remains under maximum pressure.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
Iran's strategic logic is transparent: the oil surplus narrative weakens its leverage, and it cannot allow the narrative to consolidate. By maintaining Hormuz disruption, Iran keeps the global oil risk premium elevated — which is simultaneously an economic weapon against the West (higher energy costs) and a negotiating card for any future nuclear or sanctions deal. The APEC trilateral's agenda included Iran energy management, which suggests the US-China-Russia conversation is partly about how to contain Iran. Any deal made in Shenzhen that sidelines Iran's interests would escalate attacks further. The 86 million barrels of "apparent surplus" sitting in tankers in the war zone is not usable supply — it is hostage supply.

**Investment Implications**
- **Brent crude:** The surplus narrative ($95 → $75–80 directional call from last week) is on hold. Renewed Hormuz attacks keep a $5–10 risk premium in the price. Near-term range: $88–102. Directional bias remains bearish on 12M horizon, but the path is volatile.
- **Shipping / maritime insurance (Lloyd's, Everest Re, tanker operators):** War risk premiums spiking on routes through Hormuz. Tanker spot rates elevated. Positive for tanker operators (Frontline, Euronav) on supply constraint logic, but counterparty risk increasing.
- **Defense / missile defense (RTX, LMT, Israeli defense suppliers):** Any escalation that draws direct US military response in the Gulf is a strong catalyst for defense equities. Tail risk, but not negligible.
- **Energy infrastructure (pipelines, LNG terminals):** Any Hormuz escalation that damages LNG infrastructure would spike European TTF gas prices. Watch Qatar LNG export terminals as the most exposed single point.
- **USD:** Oil disruption strengthens petrodollar recycling demand — modest USD bullish signal in acute escalation scenario.

### EVENT 3: RUSSIA-UKRAINE — MASS ATTACK PUNCTUATES STALLED ABU DHABI TALKS

**What Happened**  
The Abu Dhabi talks (November 10–11) produced only a large-scale prisoner exchange — no ceasefire, no territorial framework. Russia's demands remain maximalist: NATO withdrawing to 1997 boundaries, no European troops in Ukraine post-ceasefire. Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge to Crimea on November 17 as a negotiating signal. Russia responded with a mass attack on November 20 — 84 cruise missiles and 404 drones targeting Ukrainian cities, one of the largest single salvos of the war. The Kremlin has stated it wants "peace for centuries," not a temporary ceasefire. US envoys traveled to Moscow after Abu Dhabi; next round is expected in Berlin, but no date is set. Putin attended the APEC trilateral — the Ukraine endgame was on the agenda.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The strategic geometry of Ukraine peace is now three-dimensional: Russia-Ukraine-US talks in Abu Dhabi, US-Russia-China trilateral in Shenzhen, and a European "Coalition of the Willing" track running in parallel. These tracks are not synchronized. Russia's simultaneous mass attack while its delegation was in Abu Dhabi is a classic "escalate to negotiate" pattern — it signals that Moscow sees military pressure and diplomacy as complementary, not contradictory. The key new variable from Shenzhen: Xi's presence in a Ukraine framework conversation, possibly as a de facto co-guarantor. If China commits to supporting a settlement, the dynamics shift materially.

**Investment Implications**
- **European defense (Rheinmetall, SAAB, Leonardo, Thales):** Structural long thesis unchanged. The rearmament decision is a decade-long European sovereign choice — no single negotiation reverses it. Any ceasefire creates short-term event risk (defense stocks may sell off on "peace premium") but the thesis holds on 3–5Y horizon.
- **Ukrainian reconstruction (Heidelberg Materials, Saint-Gobain, Holcim):** Optionality value building, not yet priced. The Berlin talks being scheduled is a signal that the conversation is progressing. These names are the right vehicles if a ceasefire is announced within 6–12 months.
- **European natural gas (TTF):** A ceasefire that includes energy infrastructure relief could ease European gas prices by 15–25%. Long TTF as a hedge in the interim.
- **Russian assets:** Technically uninvestable due to sanctions. Watch OFAC signals — any post-summit movement on sanctions relief would be a major re-rating event for Russian energy equities. Not actionable yet.
- **Commodities — wheat, corn:** Ukraine is a major grain exporter. Resumed infrastructure attacks disrupt Black Sea grain corridor. Modest bullish signal for grain prices.

The ratio of cooperative/conflictual events by Russian-Ukrainian actor pairs is the leading indicator of ceasefire likelihood. Build this ratio and backtest against 2022–2026 ceasefire proposals.

### EVENT 4: TAIWAN NOVEMBER 28 LOCAL ELECTIONS — PRC INFORMATION WARFARE AT PEAK INTENSITY

**What Happened**  
Taiwan holds nationwide local elections on November 28, filling 11,051 positions including city mayors, county magistrates, and councilors. The Taipei mayoral race is the highest-stakes contest: DPP challenger Puma Shen vs. incumbent KMT mayor Chiang Wan-an in a close race. Beijing has opened a police investigation against Shen — an extraordinary interference in Taiwan's domestic politics. Taiwan's Criminal Investigation Bureau has investigated 219 cases of election-related misinformation and AI-generated deepfake videos. Radio Free Asia reports China is deploying AI-driven cognitive warfare to influence the outcome. The Xi-Trump APEC exchange included Xi's direct warning that the US must "do the right thing on Taiwan" — a signal Beijing views this election as part of its pressure campaign.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
Taiwan's local elections function as the most direct barometer of Taiwanese public opinion on cross-strait relations before the 2028 presidential election. A DPP surge would signal public resistance to Beijing's intimidation — which historically accelerates PLA pressure in the following months. A KMT showing would give Beijing space to claim its soft-power strategy is working. The PRC information warfare campaign, if it succeeds, sets a template for 2028. If it backfires (as Beijing's interference has in the past), it hardens Taiwanese identity and complicates any US-China deal on Taiwan. The USCC's November 2026 chapter on Taiwan as "A Crucial Window for a Critical Partner" is required reading for the investment thesis — it argues even Beijing-favorable electoral outcomes may not produce the political movement toward unification it expects.

**Investment Implications**
- **TSMC (TSM):** The Taiwan geopolitical risk premium is structurally elevated regardless of election outcome. A DPP win that provokes PLA drills would cause a short-term TSM correction — this is a buying opportunity, not a thesis change. The 2nm fab in Arizona is the medium-term hedge the market is already pricing.
- **EWT (Taiwan ETF):** Elevated event risk around November 28 and in the 2–4 weeks following. Cautious near-term; structural long on Taiwan's position in the semiconductor supply chain.
- **USD/TWD:** Any PLA military response to a DPP win would pressure TWD. Watch 32.0 as the near-term support level.
- **US defense contractors (LMT, RTX, NOC):** Taiwan arms sales are a direct function of cross-strait tension. Xi's APEC warning about Taiwan signals US arms sales are on the negotiating table. Any arms sale delay would modestly reduce near-term revenue visibility for these names.
- **Korean won (KRW) and Korean chipmakers (Samsung, SK Hynix):** A Taiwan military incident would pressure Korean semiconductor assets as collateral geopolitical risk.

### EVENT 5: COP31 CLOSES IN ANTALYA — CLIMATE FINANCE DEADLOCK DEEPENS NORTH-SOUTH FAULT LINE

**What Happened**  
COP31 closed in Antalya, Turkey on November 20 without a breakthrough on climate finance. The core demand — $500B+/year from developed nations (a multiple of the undelivered $100B/year pledge from COP15) — was not met. The oil surplus, the US outside the Paris framework under Trump 2.0, and Chinese BRI-linked infrastructure as a competing offer to Western climate finance created the conditions for deadlock. Turkey's hosting of COP31 is itself a geopolitical signal: Ankara is using NATO relevance, COP credibility, and refugee-route leverage simultaneously to advance EU accession talks. The Trump-attending-APEC-not-COP dynamic was a visible illustration of the US strategic reprioritization.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The climate finance failure is not primarily about climate — it is about North-South economic statecraft. Developing nations (Bangladesh, Kenya, Indonesia, Nigeria) are being offered a choice between Western climate-conditioned lending and Chinese infrastructure investment with no climate strings. The choice shapes supply chains for critical minerals, port access, and digital infrastructure for a generation. China's consistent offer: build the port, the rail, and the data center, with no democracy or climate conditions. The EU's offer: climate-conditioned loans that require institutional reform. For emerging markets caught between two models, the BRI is often the faster path. This is not a 2026 story — it is a 20-year structural competition.

**Investment Implications**
- **Green energy / clean tech (ICLN, clean hydrogen, offshore wind):** Near-term headwind from cheaper oil, COP deadlock, and reduced policy urgency under Trump 2.0. The long-horizon structural thesis (energy transition as supply chain security) remains intact. This is a buying opportunity in quality clean-tech names on weakness, not a thesis change.
- **Carbon markets (EUAs, California ARB):** European carbon prices most affected by COP failure. Watch for EU ETS reform signaling post-Antalya. EUA prices likely range-bound without policy catalyst.
- **EM sovereign bonds (Bangladesh, Kenya, Indonesia, Nigeria):** Climate finance deadlock means these economies must increase BRI borrowing — which elevates their China dependency and sovereign risk in a US-China decoupling scenario. Cautious on EM sovereigns most exposed to China's debt diplomacy.
- **Critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, copper, rare earths):** The energy transition continues regardless of COP outcomes — it is now driven by supply chain security mandates in the US, EU, and Japan. DRC cobalt, Chilean lithium, South African platinum remain structurally long. The COP deadlock slightly reduces near-term demand signals, but the 5–10Y structural case is unchanged.
- **Turkish assets (TRY, ISE-30):** Turkey's hosting of COP31 and NATO relevance give Erdogan leverage in EU negotiations. TRY has benefited from geopolitical positioning; watch EU accession headline risk as a potential TRY positive catalyst.
- **Databricks Signal:** Track IEA, IRENA, and UNFCCC text pipelines for energy transition language and climate finance commitment signals. Sentiment on "energy transition urgency" in official documents leads European carbon price moves by 3–6 months. Build a text pipeline from UNFCCC press releases.
