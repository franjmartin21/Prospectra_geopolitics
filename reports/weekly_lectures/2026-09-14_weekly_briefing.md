## EVENT 1: IRAN WAR ESCALATES — BRENT BREAKS $101, HORMUZ THROUGHPUT AT 23% OF PRE-WAR LEVELS

### What Happened
On September 8, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched a second wave of missile and drone attacks on US Navy ships in the Persian Gulf — the most significant naval engagement since the war began in February. Oil markets responded immediately: Brent rose to $99 on September 8, then $101.21 on September 9 — its highest close since May 22. By Friday (September 12), Brent closed below $105 but the weekly gain was approximately 9%. The critical structural data: Hormuz throughput has collapsed from 21.6 million barrels per day pre-war to 4.9 million bpd — a 77% reduction. Goldman Sachs this week warned that intensifying shipping attacks raise the probability of Brent exceeding $120/barrel.

### Geopolitical Significance
The $101 print changes the strategic calculus in three directions simultaneously. First, Iran's second naval attack signals that the ceasefire collapse from last week was not a pause but a deliberate escalation — Iran is probing US naval tolerance, likely calculating that election-year pressure and oil-shock inflation will force the US toward a settlement on Iran's terms. Second, at 4.9M bpd through Hormuz, the global oil supply disruption is now catastrophic by any peacetime standard. The IEA has forecast the largest annual demand contraction since COVID in 2026 — but supply disruption is the dominant driver, not demand destruction. Third, Bessent's September 4 interview calling for $40 oil looks increasingly disconnected from operational reality — this week's data shows the war is not winding down.

## EVENT 2: AUGUST CPI PRINTS 3.4% — WARSH SEPTEMBER 16 HIKE NOW NEAR-CERTAIN

### What Happened
The Bureau of Labor Statistics released August CPI data on September 11, 2026. The headline: CPI rose 3.4% year-over-year, with a 0.4% month-over-month increase — significantly higher than July's 0.1% monthly gain. The driver: gasoline prices rose 3.9% in August, accounting for more than one-third of the total monthly increase. Core CPI remains above 3%. The September 16 FOMC decision is now widely expected to deliver a 25bp hike, taking the fed funds target to 3.75–4.00%. Prior to this CPI print, markets had priced a 66% hike probability; that figure has likely moved to 85%+ since the September 11 release.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the Iran war entering the US domestic political economy. The mechanism is direct: Hormuz throughput collapse → oil above $100 → gasoline +3.9% MoM → CPI misses on the upside → Warsh has no choice but to hike. The Warsh-Bessent tension crystallizes here: Treasury needs low rates to manage rolling debt refinancing (a 25bp hike costs ~$50B annually on the national debt stock); the Fed's credibility requires hiking given its Jackson Hole commitment. Waller's dissent camp loses this argument when CPI prints 3.4%. The political implication: Trump will face rising mortgage rates, a stronger dollar, and oil-driven inflation simultaneously going into the November 2026 midterms — a historically difficult trifecta.

## EVENT 3: XI-TRUMP SUMMIT CONFIRMED FOR SEPTEMBER 24 — TRADE ARCHITECTURE UPDATE

### What Happened
The Trump-Xi summit remains on schedule for September 24, 2026 in Washington, DC — the second bilateral summit of the year (the first was in Beijing in May). The May summit produced: (1) the "constructive strategic stability" framework, (2) US-China Board of Trade and Board of Investment (both now stalled), and (3) Chinese commitments to buy US agricultural and airline products. For September 24, the working agenda is: trade, AI, Taiwan, and Iran. On trade, an informal understanding has emerged that China's aggregate tariff burden will remain near 20%, likely through a combination of new announcements and suspended timelines. Xi is reportedly bringing Chinese commercial executives to the White House to signal economic goodwill.

### Geopolitical Significance
The May summit set a floor under US-China relations. The September summit must either show progress or the floor cracks. Three structural tensions make progress hard: (1) the tech and AI decoupling is accelerating regardless of what leaders agree — US export controls and CHIPS Act execution don't pause for diplomatic windows; (2) China's overcapacity in EVs, solar, and steel is creating sustained European and US trade friction that cannot be resolved bilaterally; (3) Iran is the wildcard — Trump wants Xi to pressure Iran toward a settlement, but China cannot be seen publicly pressuring a fellow SCO member it condemned US strikes against. What to watch on September 24: whether the Board of Trade gets substantive content (a real tariff reduction schedule) or stays a framework shell. The difference tells you whether this summit is a diplomatic reset or a photo op.

**CEO Directional View:** Don't trade the summit event itself — trade the structural decoupling. Even a successful September 24 summit doesn't reverse the semiconductor controls, the friendshoring buildout, or the rare earth supply chain diversification imperative. Our structural long on semiconductor supply chain (TSMC, ASML) and Mexico/Vietnam/India is intact and unaffected by whatever Xi and Trump announce on September 24.

## EVENT 4: BRAZIL OCTOBER 4 FIRST ROUND — FLÁVIO BOLSONARO AHEAD IN RUNOFF MODELS

### What Happened
Brazil's October 4 first-round presidential election is now 20 days away, and it has moved decisively into "geopolitical risk" territory. The key facts: Jair Bolsonaro is ineligible, having been indicted and imprisoned in September 2026 for plotting a coup following his 2022 defeat. His son, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, is the Liberal Party (PL) candidate. Incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (age 80) is running for an unprecedented fourth term. First-round polling shows Lula at 37–41% and Flávio at 30–37%, with a near-certain runoff on October 25. The runoff models are a statistical tie — Real Time Big Data shows 44–44, and Vox Brasil puts Flávio fractionally ahead at 45.1% to 44.5%. Prediction markets favor Flávio at 41% to Lula's 36%.

### Geopolitical Significance
Brazil matters geopolitically on three dimensions: (1) BRL and EM FX contagion — a Flávio Bolsonaro victory would likely trigger BRL appreciation and EM risk-on, while a Lula win extends current fiscal trajectory uncertainty; (2) Brazil is a top-5 oil producer and China's largest soybean supplier — the election outcome affects both commodity trade architecture and China's agricultural import dependency; (3) Brazil is a key swing state in the BRICS+ multipolar dynamic — Lula has been closer to the SCO/BRICS line while a Bolsonaro government would likely realign toward Washington. The uncertainty is the risk — with prediction markets in a dead heat, Brazilian assets will be volatile between now and October 25.

### Investment Implications
| Asset Class | Direction | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| BRL | **Cautious/Volatile** | Election uncertainty = BRL at risk. Near-term underweight; reassess after October 4 first round |
| Brazilian equities (Ibovespa) | **Neutral/Underweight** | Political uncertainty + USD strength + oil import cost pressure (Brazil is an oil exporter but refined product importer). Wait for clarity |
| Brazilian sovereign bonds | **Cautious** | Fiscal trajectory uncertainty under both candidates. Spreads may widen into election |
| Brazilian oil producers (Petrobras) | **Watch** | Flávio win = less state intervention risk for Petrobras. Lula win = price intervention risk. Binary political bet |
| Soybean/agricultural commodities | **Monitor** | Brazil is China's top soy supplier. Election outcome affects government negotiating position with Beijing |
| Mexico peso (MXN) | **Underweight regardless** | USD strength + oil import cost is a separate headwind from Brazil; EM FX basket underweight holds |

**CEO Directional View:** Brazil is a new risk that wasn't on last week's radar. The election is too close to call and the BRL is not currently pricing the uncertainty correctly. Reduce or avoid BRL and Brazilian equity exposure until after October 4. If Flávio wins the first round convincingly (45%+), reassess: the market will read that as pro-business and BRL-positive. This is a watch and wait position, not a blind short.
