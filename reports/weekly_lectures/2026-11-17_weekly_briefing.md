### EVENT 1: OIL MARKET TIPS BACK INTO SURPLUS — FIRST TIME SINCE IRAN WAR

**What Happened**  
The IEA's November monthly report projects global oil supply will exceed demand by approximately 1.7 million bpd in Q4 2026 — the first surplus since the Iran war disrupted Gulf exports. Gulf oil exports have recovered to roughly 74% of pre-war levels (18.4 million bpd in October), though Strait of Hormuz tanker attacks continue. Brent crude was trading around $95/barrel at time of writing, having fallen ~16% over the past week. For 2027, the IEA projects the surplus widening to ~3.8 million bpd.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
This is a structural inflection point, not a transient data point. The Iran war created an artificial deficit that caused energy inflation globally, elevated defense spending in Gulf states, and strengthened the dollar (oil priced in USD, demand for USD up). A durable surplus regime would reverse all three. The fragility caveat is real: 86 million barrels of the apparent "surplus" sits on tankers in the war zone, not in onshore storage. One renewed Hormuz incident reprices the entire surplus narrative within 48 hours.

**Investment Implications**
- **Oil (Brent/WTI):** Bearish directional tilt on 6–12M horizon if surplus is confirmed. The $95 level is not yet pricing in a durable 1.7M bpd surplus — implies further downside toward $75–80 range if Gulf export recovery holds. Watch $90 as near-term support.
- **Energy equities (XLE, integrated oils):** Margin compression at lower realized prices. Underweight relative to materials. Refiners may outperform on wider crack spreads.
- **USD:** Bearish on oil-driven USD demand (petrodollar recycling slows). Slight tailwind for EM currencies with oil import exposure (India, Turkey, South Korea).
- **Defense sector:** Tail risk. Gulf security contracts remain active; any re-escalation reprices defense very fast.
- **Databricks Signal:** GDELT event data on Iran-related conflict events is the leading indicator here. Build a "Hormuz Disruption Index" — count of GDELT event codes involving Iran + maritime + conflict — and correlate with Brent price lag. This is a live, testable signal.

### EVENT 2: US-CHINA APEC SUMMIT — TRUMP-XI BILATERAL WITH TRUCE ON THE LINE

**What Happened**  
Washington and Beijing extended the Busan trade truce to January 10, 2027, buying two months of time rather than sealing a deal. Commerce published a formal suspension of the Affiliates Rule on November 5. A new Commerce Department rule published November 16 targets Chinese access to advanced AI chips through third-country routing — China's commerce ministry threatened countermeasures on November 17. Trump and Xi are scheduled to meet bilaterally at APEC in Shenzhen on November 18–19, with rare-earth export controls and chip access as the core agenda.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The pattern here is "managed escalation with periodic truces" — both sides are applying pressure in targeted domains (chips, rare earths) while avoiding full tariff war. The January 10 cliff is the next hard deadline. Xi hosting APEC in Shenzhen is a deliberate signal: China frames itself as the anchor of regional economic integration while the US restricts. The rare-earth lever is China's most potent non-military card — 90%+ of global rare earth processing is Chinese.

**Investment Implications**
- **Semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, ASML, TSMC):** High-volatility event risk around APEC. A constructive Trump-Xi meeting is good for chip sector short-term. A breakdown triggers another correction. Net bias: neutral/cautious into APEC, then reassess.
- **Rare earths / critical minerals (MP Materials, Lynas, uranium adjacent):** Structural long thesis strengthens regardless of APEC outcome — the truce dependency on Chinese rare-earth cooperation underscores Western supply chain vulnerability.
- **CNY/USD:** A productive APEC meeting should allow modest CNY appreciation. Watch offshore yuan (CNH) as the real-time sentiment gauge during the summit.
- **EM Asia (EWY, EWT, VNM):** South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam are the countries most exposed to the chip restriction expansion. Any signal that the chip war is widening would pressure these markets.
- **Databricks Signal:** Build a US-China Trade Tension Index from GDELT — track event tone between US and China actors + BIS enforcement news via FRED-linked text sentiment. Use as a regime indicator that flags truce → tension transitions.

### EVENT 3: RUSSIA-UKRAINE PEACE TALKS RESUME IN ABU DHABI

**What Happened**  
US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine resumed in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 — the first formal trilateral session in nine months. Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky faced Ukrainian delegation head Rustem Umerov, with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff leading US mediation. The two days produced: (1) agreement in principle on a large-scale prisoner exchange, (2) no ceasefire agreement, (3) Russia signaling openness to a potential Trump-Putin-Xi trilateral summit to end the conflict. Meanwhile, Ukraine struck the Kerch Strait Bridge to Crimea on November 17, suggesting Kyiv continues applying military pressure alongside diplomacy.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The resumption itself is signal — both sides accepted a framework conversation, which was not a given. The Kerch strike is Kyiv's negotiating leverage, not contradiction: Ukraine will not negotiate from a position of strategic retreat. The proposed Trump-Putin-Xi trilateral is the most significant new variable — it would formalize the US-China alignment on forcing a settlement and potentially restructure European security architecture outside NATO's direct role.

**Investment Implications**
- **European defense (SAAB, Rheinmetall, Leonardo, defense ETFs):** A ceasefire scenario is bearish near-term for defense stocks — but the structural case for European rearmament is a decade-long trend that a tactical ceasefire does not reverse. Hold long-term thesis; manage short-term event risk.
- **Ukrainian reconstruction plays (construction materials, European banks with Ukraine exposure):** Optionality on ceasefire. Heidelberg Materials, Saint-Gobain, Holcim have reconstruction upside that is not yet priced.
- **Natural gas (European TTF):** A ceasefire that includes energy infrastructure relief could ease European gas prices. Bearish for TTF on ceasefire scenario.
- **Russian assets (RSX equivalent, RUB):** Still largely uninvestable due to sanctions, but a formal ceasefire framework would begin changing that calculus. Watch for OFAC signals post-APEC.
- **Databricks Signal:** Ukraine conflict event counts in GDELT (event codes 190s — use of force) are the leading indicator for ceasefire likelihood. Track the ratio of cooperative to conflictual events between Russian and Ukrainian actors. A sustained shift toward cooperative tone precedes formal ceasefires historically.

### EVENT 4: ESTONIA CLOSES RUSSIA BORDER AFTER ALLEGED SABOTAGE PLOT

**What Happened**  
Estonia closed its border crossings with Russia following an alleged Russian sabotage plot targeting an Estonian drone manufacturer. The incident is part of an ongoing pattern of Russian gray-zone activity in the Baltic region — including infrastructure sabotage, disinformation campaigns, and hybrid pressure operations — that has intensified since the Ukraine war's escalation phase.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
The Baltics are the front line of NATO's eastern flank and the most exposed members to Russian gray-zone coercion. Estonia closing its border is a de-escalatory containment move, but it signals that hybrid warfare is not pausing during the Abu Dhabi talks — Russia runs diplomatic and covert tracks in parallel. This is classic Russian "escalate to negotiate" doctrine. The EU's response will determine whether this triggers Article 4 consultations.

**Investment Implications**
- **Baltic state sovereign bonds:** Very modest risk premium, already priced for NATO membership backstop. No structural change unless NATO Article 5 is implicated.
- **European cyber / defense tech:** Drone manufacturers, cyber security firms (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Thales) benefit from continued hybrid warfare spending regardless of Ukraine ceasefire scenario.
- **Nordic equities / SEK, NOK:** Norway and Sweden are least exposed; Finland and Estonia are most exposed. Limited direct market impact, but watch for EU emergency defense fund discussions that could affect European defense budgets.
- **Databricks Signal:** GDELT has strong coverage of Baltic-Russia events. A "Baltic Tension Index" — Russian actors + Estonia/Latvia/Lithuania + conflict events — could be an early warning indicator for NATO Article 4 triggers.

### EVENT 5: COP31 OPENS IN ANTALYA — CLIMATE FINANCE AND ENERGY TRANSITION UNDER PRESSURE

**What Happened**  
COP31 opened in Antalya, Turkey this week, running through November 20. The summit is under pressure on three fronts: (1) the Iran war-driven oil surplus reduces near-term urgency for energy transition investment, (2) the US remains outside the Paris framework under Trump 2.0, (3) developing nations are demanding climate finance commitments that developed nations are not prepared to deliver. Turkey's hosting signals its pivot toward EU alignment — Ankara has been using NATO relevance and COP credibility as leverage in its ongoing EU accession negotiations.

**Geopolitical Significance**  
COP31 matters less for near-term policy than for the structural signal it sends about the pace of energy transition. The oil surplus narrative makes it harder to accelerate the transition politically — cheap(er) oil reduces the urgency argument. The climate finance gap ($100B/year promise vs. ~$30B delivered) is now a North-South geopolitical fault line, with China offering BRI-linked infrastructure deals as an alternative to Western climate finance. This is economic statecraft, not just climate policy.

**Investment Implications**
- **Green energy / clean tech (ICLN, clean hydrogen, offshore wind):** Near-term headwind from cheaper oil and reduced policy urgency. Long-horizon structural thesis intact — the energy transition is about supply chain security as much as climate, which keeps the long-horizon case intact.
- **Carbon markets (EUAs):** European carbon prices are most affected by COP outcomes. Watch for EU ETS reform signals coming out of Antalya.
- **Critical minerals again:** Climate finance debates are partly about who controls the supply chains for the transition. DRC cobalt, Chilean lithium, South African platinum remain structurally long.
- **Emerging market bonds:** Countries eligible for climate finance deals (Bangladesh, Kenya, Indonesia) could see spread compression if breakthrough climate finance package is announced. Low probability this week.
- **Databricks Signal:** Track IEA, IRENA, and UN climate press releases via text pipeline. Sentiment on energy transition language in official documents is a leading indicator of policy direction, which has a 6–12M lag to asset prices.
