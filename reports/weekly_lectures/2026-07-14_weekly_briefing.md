### EVENT 2 — Trump Declares US "Guardian of the Hormuz": Naval Blockade + 20% Cargo Toll, Effective TODAY
**Date:** July 13–14, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** CRITICAL — New Architecture

#### What Happened
In the most consequential unilateral US maritime action since the Gulf War, President Trump announced Sunday evening that: (1) the US Navy will reimpose a **naval blockade of all Iranian ships and Iranian coastal areas**, effective 4pm ET July 14 (TODAY); (2) all non-Iranian ships seeking safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz will be charged a **20% reimbursement on all cargo** — described as compensation for US security provision. Trump stated the US would henceforth be known as "THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT." CENTCOM will enforce the blockade, which covers "the entirety of the Iranian coastline to include but not limited to Iranian ports and oil terminals." The International Maritime Organization (IMO), a United Nations body, immediately stated it "stands firmly against charging fees for passage through straits used for international navigation." In an ironic echo, Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi cited Trump's statement to justify Iran's own long-standing demand to collect service fees from ships transiting Iranian waters — declaring Iran "has always been the GUARDIAN of the Strait and will remain so FOREVER."

Brent crude surged 9.4% on Monday to near $83/bbl. WTI holds near $78, its biggest rally since April.

#### Geopolitical Significance
The 20% cargo toll is legally unprecedented in modern international maritime law. The UNCLOS framework — to which the US is not a formal party but has historically upheld — guarantees innocent passage through international straits. Trump's toll demand effectively nationalizes Hormuz security and monetizes it, creating a coercive revenue mechanism. The structural implications extend far beyond Iran: if the US can charge transit fees in one strait under security justification, the framework is available elsewhere (Taiwan Strait, South China Sea). The IMO's opposition signals this will be challenged at international law forums. Iran's mirror claim creates a dual-sovereignty standoff over the world's most critical energy chokepoint — both parties claiming "Guardian" status, each demanding payment. Global shipping firms now face a compliance dilemma: pay the US toll (legitimizing it), route around the Cape (cost +$1–2M per voyage), or halt Persian Gulf operations. This is a restructuring of global maritime trade economics.

### EVENT 3 — July 17 OFAC Deadline: 72-Hour Clock to Maximum Pressure Reimposition
**Date:** Deadline July 17, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** HIGH — Hard Catalyst

#### What Happened
General License X1, issued July 7, provides a 10-day wind-down period for all Iranian oil transactions. At 12:01 a.m. EDT on July 17 — **72 hours from the time of this briefing** — all Iranian crude oil, petroleum products, and petrochemical transactions face full maximum-pressure sanctions enforcement. No new transactions could be entered into after July 7. Any payments made during the wind-down to blocked persons must be directed into blocked, interest-bearing US accounts. The Foley & Lardner and Thompson Hine analyses confirm: companies and vessel operators that have not completed their Iran exposure by July 17 face penalties across multiple OFAC sanctions programs simultaneously.

#### Geopolitical Significance
The July 17 deadline was always a hard stop — but the simultaneous naval blockade announced for today (Event 2) makes it doubly structural. Iranian crude, which had been recovering toward pre-conflict averages and selling at a 20% discount since the June 18 MOU, is now: (a) blocked by naval enforcement from July 14, AND (b) sanctioned under full OFAC maximum pressure from July 17. The dual mechanism is designed to make circumvention via third parties (China's teapot refiners, Indian buyers, Turkish intermediaries) maximally costly. However, China invoked its blocking statute on exactly this scenario (see July 13 briefing), meaning Beijing-linked buyers will continue to absorb Iranian crude in defiance of both the blockade and the sanctions. The key market variable is how much Iranian supply the blockade physically removes vs. how much continues to flow through non-US-enforcement channels.

### EVENT 4 — Coalition of the Willing Paris Summit: Anti-Ballistic Missile Coalition, Rafales, €140B Commitment
**Date:** July 13, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** HIGH — Structural Multi-Year

#### What Happened
French President Macron hosted 25+ heads of state at the Hôtel des Invalides on July 13, with President Zelensky, NATO Secretary General Rutte, German Chancellor Merz, and outgoing UK Prime Minister Starmer in attendance. Key outcomes: (1) **Anti-Ballistic Missile Coalition established** — a dedicated multinational structure to develop and deploy additional ABM capabilities specifically for Ukraine; (2) **€70 billion pledged for 2026 AND at least €70 billion for 2027** — a €140 billion two-year floor for military assistance; (3) **16 Rafale fighter jets** to be delivered to Ukraine "as early as 2028 or 2029," alongside radar systems and missiles; (4) **SAMP/T air defense batteries** (new-generation, France/Italy-built) to be delivered; (5) First **joint exercises of the Multinational Force for Ukraine** announced — a step toward potential deployment of multinational troops once fighting ends; (6) Coalition committed to ramping up economic pressure on Russia and strengthening sanctions coordination. The Co-Chairs' Statement (UK + France) was released simultaneously, making the commitment formally bileteral rather than ad hoc.

#### Geopolitical Significance
This summit took place one day after the US announced the Iran blockade — creating a striking split-screen: Washington consumed by Iran/Hormuz while European allies deepened their structural commitment to Ukraine. The Anti-Ballistic Missile Coalition is the most consequential outcome: it creates a dedicated institutional structure (not ad hoc support) for deploying ABM systems in Ukraine — directly counter to Russian hypersonic and ballistic missile strikes. The Rafale commitment is also significant: France is for the first time committing advanced 4th/5th-gen fighters to Ukraine, with a delivery window in 2028-2029 that signals a long-term war assumption. The €140 billion two-year floor is the largest explicit multi-year commitment made outside NATO's formal budget.

China is observing US overstretching across two theaters (Iran, Ukraine) and using the moment to demonstrate its own escalation capacity — signaling to Washington that any coercive action on Taiwan would face a nuclear underscore.

The completion of China's nuclear triad (land, air, sea-based second-strike capability) is the most structurally significant deterrence development in the Asia-Pacific in a generation. The JL-2/JL-3 test demonstrated Pacific reach from PRC internal waters — meaning China's second-strike capacity cannot be preemptively destroyed without itself triggering nuclear escalation. Combined with the Taiwan Coast Guard rotation (gray-zone pressure) and US focus on Iran, this week confirms Beijing is in active strategic messaging mode.
