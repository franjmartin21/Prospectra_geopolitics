### EVENT 1 — US-Iran War: Past the Red Lines
**Dates:** July 13–19, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** CRITICAL | **Continuity:** Week 2 of active war

#### What Happened
The US-Iran conflict moved into its second week of sustained exchanges with no diplomatic off-ramp in sight. On July 15, the US launched a fourth night of strikes explicitly framed as "protecting vessels in the Strait of Hormuz." On July 17, CNN's live coverage characterized the situation as "Iran and US widen attacks as renewed conflict shows no sign of de-escalating." Iran struck two vessels it labeled "rogue supertankers" in the Strait, killing one crew member — the first confirmed civilian maritime casualty of the conflict. NPR described both sides as having "blown past red lines." The ceasefire MOU signed June 18 is now a dead letter. Shipping through the Strait remains at approximately 5% of pre-conflict levels; the IEA reports oil trading at roughly $82/bbl as of July 20 — elevated from the $77 trough earlier this month but below the crisis spike of $85+ in June.

#### Geopolitical Significance
Three structural shifts are now locked in, regardless of how the immediate conflict resolves:

1. **The credibility of Gulf security guarantees has been tested and found wanting.** The June MOU collapsed within days of signing. No counterparty — ally or adversary — will treat US-brokered Gulf security frameworks as durable for years.

2. **Iran's willingness to strike civilian maritime targets is now demonstrated.** The "two rogue supertankers" episode is a precedent that will affect insurance pricing and shipping routes permanently.

3. **The Hormuz transit toll gambit — even if abandoned — is now in the political vocabulary.** The IMO rejected it. But the precedent that the US *considered* charging a toll for use of international waters has altered the global perception of freedom of navigation guarantees.

Vice President Vance's suggestion that "the conflict can only be resolved through diplomacy" is notable precisely because it is the first official US signal that military objectives may be hitting limits.

#### Investment Implications
| Asset | Direction | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Brent crude | Structurally elevated | 5% Hormuz traffic = supply disruption, not just risk premium |
| LNG (spot) | Bullish | Europe rerouting from Gulf; Qatar LNG under partial disruption |
| Tanker shipping (VLCC) | Bifurcated — war premium on non-Gulf routes | Gulf routes uninsurable; Atlantic/Pacific tankers at structural premium |
| Defense sector (US, European) | Bullish | Active conflict driving procurement urgency |
| EM oil importers (India, Korea, Japan) | Bearish on current account | Higher import bills compress fiscal space |
| Gold | Bullish | Uncertainty premium; central banks accumulating |
| USD | Ambiguous | Safe-haven inflows vs. fiscal concern from war costs |

**CEO View:** The IEA's $74/bbl Q3 forecast is a pre-escalation artifact. With Hormuz at 5% of normal flow, the structural supply disruption is real — not just a risk premium. Brent at $80–$90 is the base case through Q3 unless a diplomatic breakthrough emerges. The Vance signal bears watching — if back-channel diplomacy is occurring, a sudden ceasefire could cause a sharp oil reversal. **Long oil, long defense, short EM current account-deficit sovereigns.**

### EVENT 2 — UK: Andy Burnham Becomes Prime Minister
**Date:** July 20, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** MEDIUM | **Continuity:** New

#### What Happened
Andy Burnham — former Mayor of Greater Manchester — was sworn in as the UK's 59th Prime Minister today, becoming the country's seventh head of government in ten years. He succeeds Keir Starmer, who resigned the Labour leadership in June after two years in office. King Charles III formally asked Burnham to form a government at Buckingham Palace this morning. In his inaugural speech, Burnham framed his tenure as "a circuit breaker for Britain" promising "a new political model and a new economic model." Within hours of the swearing-in, President Trump posted that Britain is a "Poverty Stricken Disaster" — an immediate signal of trans-Atlantic friction.

#### Geopolitical Significance
The UK's chronic political instability — seven PMs in ten years, spanning two parties — is now a structural sovereign risk story, not an episodic one. Burnham represents a distinct shift in Labour's center of gravity: away from Westminster technocracy (Blair/Brown/Starmer) toward what the CFR describes as "a politics of place, investment, and national renewal" — closer to continental social democracy, with an emphasis on industrial policy and regional devolution.

The Trump "Poverty Stricken Disaster" comment on day one is strategically significant: it signals that the US-UK "special relationship" (already under stress during the tariff negotiations) faces another friction point. The EU-UK relationship will be a key variable — Burnham is more sympathetic to EU alignment than Starmer was, but Brexit constraints remain.

#### Investment Implications
| Asset | Direction | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| GBP/USD | Near-term uncertainty → medium-term watch | Political transition creates short-term uncertainty; Burnham's EU-lean could be GBP positive over 12-18 months if trade frictions ease |
| UK gilts | Neutral to cautious | Burnham's industrial policy agenda implies fiscal expansion; watch first budget |
| FTSE 100 | Neutral | Large-cap UK is globally exposed, less domestically sensitive |
| FTSE 250 | Bullish on "industrial policy" themes | Mid-caps more domestically sensitive; UK infrastructure/energy names benefit from industrial agenda |
| UK defense | Bullish | NATO commitment, active conflict backdrop; bipartisan consensus on defense spending |

**CEO View:** The UK political instability story is itself an investable signal — chronic leadership turnover is a reason UK assets trade at a structural discount to peers. Watch Burnham's first fiscal statement for clues on industrial policy scope. The Trump antagonism is a headwind for any US-UK trade deal, which had been on the table under Starmer. **Neutral-to-cautious on GBP in the near term; revisit post-budget.**

### EVENT 3 — US Tariff Architecture in Legal Crisis: Section 122 Expires July 24
**Date:** July 16–24, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** HIGH | **Continuity:** Escalating from prior weeks

#### What Happened
The Trump tariff architecture is entering a structural inflection point. The Supreme Court's earlier 2026 ruling struck down IEEPA-based tariffs (the original "Liberation Day" framework), forcing the administration to pivot to alternative legal tools. The current fallback — a 10% Section 122 global tariff on imports from most countries — **expires July 24, 2026**, four days from now. The administration is reportedly racing to rebuild tariff authority before the expiry. Meanwhile: (1) The EU deal entered into force July 1 at 15% on most imports. (2) Mexico faces a 30% tariff as of August 1 (subsequently delayed 90 days). (3) A new Section 301 investigation against Germany on pharmaceutical pricing was opened June 18. Separately, Trump this week "hinted at imminent trade news for minimum 7 countries."

#### Geopolitical Significance
The tariff architecture is the central instrument of US economic statecraft under Trump 2.0. The Supreme Court clipping IEEPA authority is arguably the most significant constraint on executive foreign economic policy since the 1970s. It matters for two reasons:

1. **Legal uncertainty is now structural.** Every tariff measure is potentially challengeable. Trading partners know the US cannot easily reimpose blanket tariffs without congressional authorization or alternative legal hooks. This weakens US negotiating leverage precisely as the Iran war is consuming diplomatic bandwidth.

2. **The 10% floor disappearing would be disinflationary shock.** US import prices would fall, benefiting consumers and creating a deflationary pulse — the opposite of what the administration wants on domestic grounds. Watch July 24 closely.

#### Investment Implications
| Asset | Direction | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| USD | Bearish near-term | If Section 122 expires without replacement, tariff architecture gaps = USD negative |
| US Treasuries | Ambiguous | Disinflationary tariff reduction bullish for duration; but fiscal concern offsets |
| S&P 500 (domestic importers) | Bullish if tariffs fall | Lower import costs = margin expansion for consumer and manufacturing names |
| MXN | Bullish if 30% tariff delay holds | 90-day delay is MXN positive; watch for reversal |
| EU equities / EUR | Marginally positive | 15% rate locked in; legal uncertainty reduced for EU exporters |
| Pharma (Germany) | Watch | Section 301 investigation is a new pressure vector; German pharma exporters face new risk |

**CEO View:** July 24 is a hard date. If Section 122 lapses without a replacement mechanism and Trump's "7 countries" announcement fills the gap, markets will need to rapidly re-price the tariff landscape. The signal to watch: any emergency executive action or congressional vote before July 24. The structural message of the Supreme Court ruling is that US tariff power under executive discretion is more constrained than 2025 assumed. **This reduces the sustained global trade disruption risk but increases policy unpredictability. Near-term: tactically bullish on import-sensitive US consumer names on tariff relief.**

### EVENT 4 — China Q2 GDP: 4.3% Growth, Politburo Meeting Imminent
**Date:** July 15, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** HIGH | **Continuity:** Intensifying prior trend

#### What Happened
China reported Q2 2026 GDP growth of 4.3%, down from 5.0% in Q1 — the slowest quarterly expansion in nearly four years. The miss was driven by sluggish consumption (reflecting weak domestic demand and the deflationary environment), partially offset by strong export growth (the overcapacity/export-dumping dynamic discussed in Lesson 179). The full-year target remains 4.5–5%, the lowest since the early 1990s. Analysts now widely expect a late-July Politburo meeting to signal new stimulus measures. However, Bloomberg reports that Beijing is expected to deploy "targeted" stimulus — precision-guided rather than the 2008-style bazooka — focused on consumption support and selective industrial capacity.

#### Geopolitical Significance
China's Q2 miss is more than an economic story — it is a signal about the internal pressures driving Chinese foreign policy behavior. The triple constraint we covered in Lessons 178-181 (demographics + property debt + overcapacity) is now confirmed in the data. This matters geopolitically in three ways:

1. **A domestically stressed China has elevated incentive for external assertion** as a distraction and nationalist consolidation tool. Watch Taiwan Strait, South China Sea incidents.

2. **China's deflationary export dumping intensifies at 4.3% growth.** The overcapacity pressure exports deflation globally, hitting commodity-exporting EMs and raising protectionist responses from the EU and US.

3. **The Politburo meeting is a China stimulus catalyst.** If Beijing signals meaningful consumer stimulus (rather than more infrastructure), it could rerate Chinese consumer and retail equities and lift commodity demand expectations.

#### Investment Implications
| Asset | Direction | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Chinese equities (A-shares, H-shares) | Cautiously bullish near Politburo meeting | Stimulus catalyst; but structural headwinds limit duration of any rally |
| Copper | Bullish on stimulus signal | China consumes ~55% of world copper; any construction/infrastructure stimulus is copper-positive |
| Iron ore | Modestly bullish | Same dynamic; more levered to construction than copper |
| AUD | Bullish on stimulus signal | Australia's trade is deeply China-linked; AUD is a liquid China proxy |
| EM FX (commodity exporters) | Bullish on stimulus signal | Brazil (iron ore, soy), Chile (copper) benefit from Chinese demand |
| Luxury sector (LVMH, Richemont) | Watch | Chinese luxury consumption at 4.3% growth is pressured; any stimulus targeting consumption is key |

**CEO View:** The Politburo meeting (expected late July — possibly before our next briefing) is the single most important near-term catalyst for commodity markets. If Beijing signals consumer-side stimulus, buy copper and AUD. If it signals more infrastructure/manufacturing investment, it deepens the overcapacity/deflation problem and the trade response accelerates. **The asymmetric trade: long copper/AUD as a Politburo optionality play ahead of the meeting. Position before, not after.**

### EVENT 5 — Russia-Ukraine: Deadliest Month for Civilians, Ceasefire Stalls
**Date:** July 13–20, 2026 | **Asset Class Impact:** MEDIUM | **Continuity:** Persistent

#### What Happened
Ukraine's civilian death toll in the past month reached 293 killed and 1,990 wounded — the deadliest period for civilians since April 2022, according to UN monitoring. Trump met Zelensky bilaterally at a NATO summit in Ankara on July 8, in what US officials framed as a sign the "tide has turned in Ukraine's favor." Yet ceasefire negotiations remain gridlocked over the two core issues they have been stuck on since early 2025: territorial control of the Donbas (Russia insists on formal cession; Ukraine refuses unilateral withdrawal) and security guarantees (Ukraine demands NATO membership or equivalent; Russia refuses). April and May truces were both violated within hours. The conflict is now entering its fifth year.

#### Geopolitical Significance
The Ankara meeting signals that the US has not abandoned Ukraine diplomatically — a meaningful message given earlier Trump-era ambiguity. But the meeting did not produce a framework for resolution. The pattern of announced truces followed by immediate violations is now established: neither side is ready for a durable ceasefire, and both retain enough capacity to continue fighting.

The civilian death toll escalation matters for European political dynamics. It increases pressure on EU governments to maintain Ukraine support and sustain defense spending — which aligns with the lesson-163/166 themes of European rearmament. The Germany-Poland-Baltics axis is the most engaged; Burnham's UK may increase support given the political optics of his first week.

#### Investment Implications
| Asset | Direction | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| European defense (Rheinmetall, Leonardo, Saab) | Bullish | Sustained conflict = sustained demand; civilian casualty spike increases political will to fund |

| Russian assets | Effectively uninvestable | Sanctions + capital controls + war economy; stay out |

**CEO View:** The Russia-Ukraine war is now a background structural input, not a weekly market mover — unless a major escalation (attack on NATO territory) or a sudden peace deal emerges. The investment play remains European defense and NATO-adjacent industrial policy names. Watch for any signal of a US-brokered framework post-Ankara. **Long European defense; long wheat/agriculture on ongoing Black Sea disruption risk.**
