## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The week's defining shift is **entrenchment replacing escalation** — and that is more dangerous to portfolios than a fresh shock, because entrenchment is what markets misprice as "de-risking." The Strait of Hormuz standoff has moved from acute crisis to "blockade diplomacy": OFAC sanctioned the body Iran's IRGC was using to toll and direct shipping (the Persian Gulf Strait Authority), oil slid roughly 3% on reports Trump is reluctant to restart full-scale war, and yet Iran still refuses to reopen the Strait until Israel halts operations in Lebanon — the exact three-principal deadlock flagged in last week's briefing, now simply *quieter*. Quieter is not resolved. Elsewhere: Armenia's Pashinyan claimed a sweeping re-election victory that (if it holds against contested exit polls) locks in the West-leaning tilt of the South Caucasus; the PLA shadowed a Dutch frigate through the Taiwan Strait in a textbook gray-zone signal as Taipei's $25bn rearmament bill becomes law; Washington advanced a new Section 301 tariff track targeting 60 countries over forced-labor concerns, with the dollar (DXY) reclaiming the 100 handle on safe-haven and rate-differential flows; and the EU pressed its sanctions architecture deeper into crypto rails, banning Russian/Belarusian exchanges and the ruble-stablecoin RUBx outright.

None of these five is a surprise reversal. All five are the **same structural trends compounding** — a fragmenting Gulf order, a hardening Russia-EU financial wall, a more assertive multipolar contest in two straits at once (Hormuz and Taiwan), and a US trade and currency posture that keeps adding friction even as it claims to be negotiating it away. The CEO's read: markets are pricing a "calming week." The structure says otherwise — it says *consolidation of the new normal*, which is the phase that lasts longest and that most participants stop pricing for.

## EVENT 1 — HORMUZ: FROM CRISIS TO "BLOCKADE DIPLOMACY"

### What Happened
The Iran standoff entered a new phase this week — not resolution, but routinization. On May 27, OFAC sanctioned the **Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA)**, the body the IRGC had stood up to toll commercial vessels and dictate their routing through Hormuz in exchange for "safe passage." Through the week, oil prices whipsawed on headline noise: Brent and WTI rose after Netanyahu told CNBC that Israel and the US were "prepared to attack Iran again if necessary" (June 3), then fell roughly 3% two days later (June 4) on reporting that Trump is reluctant to restart full-scale war. WTI settled near $93/bbl, Brent near $95 — both still roughly 15-20% above pre-crisis baseline. Iran continues to refuse to reopen the Strait until Israel halts operations in Lebanon — the precondition that has frozen the MOU since late May.

### Geopolitical Significance
"Blockade diplomacy" is the right label, and it is a regime change in itself: both sides have stopped trying to win and started trying to *manage* an indefinite stand-off. The PGSA sanction is functionally an admission that the US expects the toll-and-control apparatus to persist — you don't sanction an institution you expect to disappear next month. Lufthansa cutting flights and European governments openly planning for fuel shortages tell you this is no longer being treated by serious actors as a transient spike. The structural deadlock identified three weeks ago — Iran's political leadership, the operationally autonomous IRGC, and Netanyahu's Lebanon campaign forming an unsolvable three-body negotiation — has not moved an inch. It has simply stopped making daily headlines, which is precisely the condition under which markets quietly re-rate risk lower than the facts justify.

## EVENT 2 — ARMENIA'S ELECTION: THE SOUTH CAUCASUS TILTS WEST (PROVISIONALLY)

### What Happened
Armenians voted June 7 in a high-stakes parliamentary election that doubled as a referendum on the country's geopolitical orientation — continue Pashinyan's pivot toward the West and a peace track with Azerbaijan, or swing back toward the Russia-aligned opposition. Official preliminary tallies from the Central Electoral Commission show Pashinyan's **Civil Contract at ~57%**, with Samvel Karapetyan's Strong Armenia Alliance a distant second (~21%), Kocharyan's Armenia Alliance (~8%) and Tsarukyan's Prosperous Armenia (~5%) trailing. Turnout was just under 59%. Pashinyan has already declared a "historic" victory — but an independent News.am exit poll shows a dramatically different picture (Civil Contract at ~33%, combined opposition above the threshold at ~53%), and final official results are due Monday, June 8.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is a textbook small-state pivot point with outsized regional consequences — exactly the dynamic covered in Lesson 44 (New Non-Alignment & Middle Power Hedging). If Pashinyan's mandate holds, it locks in: continuation of the Armenia-Azerbaijan peace process (with the associated "Trump Route"/Zangezur corridor implications for trans-Caspian trade), deeper EU/Western institutional integration, and a further loosening of Russia's traditional grip on the South Caucasus — a region Moscow has treated as its backyard for two centuries. If the contested exit poll is closer to the truth and the official count is challenged, Armenia risks a legitimacy crisis that Russia-aligned actors would be well-positioned to exploit — precisely the kind of "color revolution vs. managed succession" contest that has defined post-Soviet space politics for two decades. Either path matters for energy-corridor geography (Caspian gas routes bypassing Russia) and for whether the Caucasus becomes a new friction point between Brussels/Washington and Moscow even as attention is fixed on Ukraine and the Gulf.

## EVENT 3 — TAIWAN STRAIT: GRAY-ZONE SIGNALING MEETS A $25BN REARMAMENT LAW

### What Happened
The PLA Eastern Theater Command confirmed it scrambled naval and air assets to track the Dutch frigate **HNLMS De Ruyter** as it transited the Taiwan Strait — a deliberate, publicized show of capability rather than an attempt to prevent passage. In parallel, Taiwan's legislature has now passed into law the **$25 billion supplemental defense package (2026–2033)**, with a portion earmarked specifically for counter-air capabilities against Chinese threats.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the gray-zone playbook executing exactly as Lesson 46 described it: assert the claim, demonstrate the capability, generate the headline — all without crossing the threshold that would force a Western military response. Tracking a *European* frigate (not a US Navy vessel) is itself a signal: it tells Brussels and individual European capitals that "freedom of navigation" exercises in the Strait now carry a visible cost, at a moment when Europe is already stretched thin by the Hormuz and Ukraine fronts. Taiwan's defense bill becoming law — covered as "passed" in last week's briefing, now enacted — confirms Taipei is treating the multi-year horizon (through 2033) as the operative planning frame, not a near-term flashpoint. Both data points argue for the CEO's standing base case: Beijing is playing a long game of attrition and normalization of presence, not preparing a near-term invasion. That is good news for near-term tail risk and bad news for anyone hoping the chip-supply-chain rerouting trade is a short-term phenomenon — it isn't; it's structural and multi-decade.

## EVENT 4 — WASHINGTON'S NEXT TARIFF FRONT: SECTION 301 TARGETS 60 COUNTRIES

### What Happened
The USTR issued a Federal Register notice tied to the looming **Section 122 tariff expiration on July 24**, proposing a new **10–12.5% Section 301 tariff on goods from roughly 60 countries** flagged in an ongoing forced-labor investigation. Simultaneously, the **US Dollar Index (DXY) reclaimed the 100 level**, with markets citing tariff-driven inflation uncertainty and muted rate-cut expectations ahead of new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first major test at the June 16–17 FOMC meeting (current target range: 3.50–3.75%).

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the trade-war architecture metastasizing rather than resolving — precisely the "living trade war" dynamic from Lesson 101. Section 301 actions framed around forced-labor findings are dual-purpose: they carry a legitimate human-rights rationale while functioning as a flexible, broadly-applicable tariff lever that can be aimed at large blocs of countries (likely including many in the China-adjacent supply chain) without a formal new statute. Layering this onto the Section 122 expiration creates a "tariff cliff" dynamic in late July that the market has not yet begun to price. Meanwhile, the DXY's return above 100 is a reminder that "structural dollar weakness" (the CEO's standing multi-year thesis) and "tactical dollar strength on rate differentials and risk-off flows" can — and currently do — coexist. New Fed leadership under fiscal-dominance pressure (Lesson 73/91 territory) makes the June FOMC meeting a genuine inflection point to watch, not a routine event.

| USD (DXY) | **Tactically firm near-term (100+); structurally bearish 2–5yr — thesis intact, just a counter-trend bounce** | Rate-differential and safe-haven flows dominate near-term; nothing in this week's data changes the long-horizon reserve-currency erosion thesis |

## EVENT 5 — EU PUSHES THE SANCTIONS WALL INTO CRYPTO RAILS

### What Happened
The EU's sanctions architecture against Russia continued to deepen at the technical layer: a **total sectoral ban on Russian and Belarusian cryptocurrency exchanges and service providers**, an explicit prohibition on transactions involving the **ruble-backed stablecoin RUBx**, and a bar on dealings involving Russia's digital ruble are now in force as part of the 20th sanctions package (adopted April 23, now being operationalized). Separately, the EU's AMLA flagged sanctions-evasion enforcement — particularly via crypto rails through Baltic, Nordic, and Eastern European member states — as a top 2026 priority. In a smaller but symbolically loaded story, Russia's Foreign Ministry sanctioned a 17-year-old British blogger (and four other UK nationals) over a crypto-laundering exposé — a sign of how seriously Moscow takes the crypto-evasion fight.

### Geopolitical Significance
This is the financial-warfare frontier moving exactly where the CEO predicted in Lessons 30 (Cyberwarfare & Financial Infrastructure) and 39/96 (CBDC & Monetary Sovereignty): both blocs are racing to build — and to wall off — parallel settlement rails before the other side can establish a durable bypass. The EU isn't just sanctioning entities; it is pre-emptively closing the *technical* loopholes (stablecoins, CBDCs, exchange rails) that a sanctioned economy would naturally migrate toward. That Moscow is sanctioning a teenager over a laundering exposé tells you the stakes Russia itself places on keeping its evasion channels intact — when a regime spends political capital punishing a single blogger, the channel being protected is doing real economic work. This is slow-moving, structural, and exactly the kind of "no single headline, but the trend is unmistakable" story that the CEO flags as more important than louder, more transient news.
