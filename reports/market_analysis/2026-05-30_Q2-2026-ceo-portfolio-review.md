## 1. Geopolitical Regime Update — Q2 2026

### 1.1 Iran / Hormuz

The 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis is the dominant geopolitical event of Q2. Key developments since April 4:

- **April–May:** Hormuz disruptions reached peak severity; Brent hit $107+ (confirmed in investment log May update)
- **May 24–29:** US-Iran negotiators reached a tentative 60-day MOU on ceasefire extension + nuclear negotiation framework — awaiting Trump approval
- **May 29:** Iran fired ballistic missiles at Kuwait; attack drones launched toward Strait the same day
- **Brent:** $92.56 as of May 29 — down ~19% in May, driven entirely by MOU optimism, not by actual Hormuz reopening

**Constraint analysis update (Papic):** The ceasefire MOU structure is confirmed as the constrained outcome (as predicted in L87). However, the continued strikes on Kuwait and drone activity toward the Strait confirm that the Iranian regime's operational military arm is NOT fully bound by the political constraint. This is a split-principal problem: the political negotiators may agree while the IRGC continues to operate. The risk premium is compressed but not eliminated.

**CEO determination:** The war premium is partially compressed but structurally intact. Long non-Gulf energy positions should not be closed on MOU optimism alone. The falsification condition (formal deal signed + structural normalization + non-Gulf supply expansion) has not been met.

### 1.2 US-China Technology War

Key Q2 developments:
- **January 15, 2026:** BIS shifted to case-by-case review for advanced AI chips to China (softening of the presumption-of-denial regime — technically a partial de-escalation)
- **January 14, 2026:** 25% tariff on semiconductors (new escalation lever, separate from export controls)
- **May 2026:** NVIDIA China market share at ~50%, down from 90%+; China's Hua Hong readying 7nm capacity domestically

**Framework interpretation:** This is the classic two-track pattern in tech cold wars: diplomatic softening on process (case-by-case review) combined with structural escalation on substance (tariffs, domestic capacity buildout). The bifurcation of the global semiconductor supply chain is accelerating, not pausing. The BIS case-by-case shift is a tactical adjustment, not a strategic reversal. The Technology Cold War structural thesis (L10, L57) remains fully intact.
